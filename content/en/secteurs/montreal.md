Montreal and Laval form a single market for most of the buyers I work with. Few people search on only one side of the Rivière des Prairies: they search for a property type, a budget and a commute.

## A market decided borough by borough

On the island, the Montreal average means nothing. A triplex in Villeray and a triplex in Rivière-des-Prairies share neither the same buyer, nor the same price dynamic, nor the same resale horizon.

**Ahuntsic-Cartierville and Villeray** attract buyers who want the orange line, neighbourhood streets and schools. Competition is real for well-prepared properties and much weaker for those that are not. The gap between the two categories has widened since 2023.

**Rosemont-La Petite-Patrie** remains one of the most liquid plex markets on the island. The price per square foot is high, but resale is rarely a problem.

**Saint-Léonard, Anjou, Montréal-Nord and Rivière-des-Prairies** still offer single-family homes and duplexes at prices that no longer exist in central neighbourhoods. These are also sectors where parking, lot size and interior space matter more to local buyers than metro proximity.

**Mercier-Hochelaga-Maisonneuve** has seen the fastest transformation in the east end. That creates opportunities, and also surprises: two streets apart can trade at very different prices.

## What I check before you make an offer

On the island, three things cause most of the bad surprises.

**The state of the contingency fund**, for any condo purchase. An undercapitalized building transfers its debt to the next buyer in the form of a special assessment.

**The existing leases**, for any plex. An income property with below-market rents is not a property where you can freely raise rents. Quebec's rent-setting regime tightly frames what you can do, and the return calculation has to account for that from the start.

**Permit and renovation history.** A basement finished without a permit, an undeclared unit conversion or a roof redone without invoices are problems that surface at the notary, not during the visit.

## Selling in Montreal

Montreal inventory is deeper than on the north shore, which means your property is compared to more options, faster.

In practice: preparation before listing matters more here than anywhere else. Professional photography, decluttering, visible minor repairs, and a price that reflects real comparables rather than seller aspiration. A property that has to cut its price after three weeks almost always sells for less than a property priced correctly on day one.

You can start with an [online estimate](/en/tools/home-estimate/), then we refine it together with the comparables from your street.
