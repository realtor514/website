Saint-Jérôme is the regional hub of the Laurentians: hospital, CEGEP, courthouse, administrative services. That creates a local economy and a real estate market more diversified than in the purely residential cities nearby.

## A two-speed market

On one side, a single-family market driven by buyers looking for affordability: families who could not make the numbers work in Blainville, Sainte-Thérèse or Mirabel, and buyers who prioritize space over commute time.

On the other, an income property market more active than in the rest of the Laurentians, supported by the CEGEP student population, hospital staff and a stable tenant base. Entry prices are noticeably lower than in Laval, which attracts investors from the metropolitan area.

These two markets do not respond to the same signals. A single-family seller and a fourplex seller in Saint-Jérôme do not have the same strategy or the same timeline.

## The sectors

**Bellefeuille** offers the largest lots and a semi-rural character. It is the preferred sector for buyers who want space and accept being further from services.

**Lafontaine** is denser, better served, and its housing stock is more varied: single-family homes, semi-detached, small buildings.

**Saint-Antoine** sits between the two, with good access to Highway 15.

**Downtown and the CEGEP sector** concentrate most of the rental market and the income properties.

One detail that matters: these former municipalities each have their own price dynamic. Comparing a Bellefeuille property to a recent Lafontaine sale produces a false estimate. It is a mistake I see often in automated valuations.

## Investing in Saint-Jérôme

The advertised return is almost always more attractive than in Laval or Montreal. That is real, but you have to look at what sits behind it.

**Local vacancy rates** vary a lot from sector to sector. A building near the CEGEP does not rent like a building on the outskirts.

**The age of the housing stock.** Part of Saint-Jérôme's income property stock dates from the 1960s and 1970s. The advertised return generally does not account for the major work ahead: roof, windows, plumbing, electrical service.

**Remote management.** If you live in Laval or Montreal, a tenant call at 10 p.m. on a Sunday costs you more in practice than in theory. Either you budget for professional management, or you accept the time it takes. Both are valid; the mistake is not choosing.

## Selling in Saint-Jérôme

The average time to sell is generally longer than in the immediate north shore, simply because the buyer pool is smaller. That makes the initial list price even more decisive: an overpriced property does not correct quickly here.
