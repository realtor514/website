Since May 20, 2026, Old Terrebonne has been a heritage site declared by the Government of Quebec, the 14th in Quebec according to the Ville de Terrebonne. Buying or selling here means handling a regulatory file at the same time as a building.

## What the declaration actually changes

The Répertoire du patrimoine culturel du Québec records the declaration on May 20, 2026, taking effect on July 2, 2025: the measures of the Cultural Heritage Act already apply. The site covers about 25 hectares and some 210 buildings built from the 18th century onward, in four landscape units. The Ville de Terrebonne bounds it by rue Saint-Louis to the north, rue Saint-Joseph to the east and the shore of the rivière des Mille Îles to the south, including Île des Moulins and rue Saint-Pierre.

The city lists the acts requiring an authorization from the ministère de la Culture et des Communications: subdividing a lot, changing the siting of a building, carrying out construction, a repair or a modification affecting the exterior appearance, demolishing in whole or in part, excavating the soil, changing signage. Facade, windows, doors, roof: all of that affects the exterior appearance. Urban planning by-law 1011, on site planning and architectural integration programmes, applies on top.

The general regime is detailed in [the guide to old and heritage buildings](/en/articles/heritage-property-montreal/), including what happens when work was done without authorization: a fine or an order to restore the property, secured by a legal hypothec, according to the Government of Quebec. Hence the rule here: a buyer planning renovations confirms what is permitted before making an offer.

## The four landscape units

**Bas-de-la-Côte** occupies the largest part of the site: boulevard des Braves, rues Saint-Pierre, Saint-François-Xavier, Sainte-Marie, Saint-André and Saint-Joseph. An orthogonal grid, limited setbacks, vernacular architecture in wood siding, brick masonry and sheet metal roofing, according to the Répertoire.

**Rue Saint-Louis** lines up larger houses, built through the 19th century and the early 20th, with deeper setbacks and large trees along the street.

**The institutional sector**, north of rue Saint-Louis, brings together the Saint-Louis-de-France church and presbytery, the former convent and the Masson manor, today collège Saint-Sacrement.

**Île des Moulins** is a distinct case: its group of heritage buildings has been classified since December 12, 1973, well before the site declaration. It holds the seigneurial office, the new mill, the bakery and the municipal library.

**The recent infill at the edge of the old core**, including the Terrebourg ensemble, trades differently. The building is young, but the lot can sit inside the perimeter: the lot decides, not the year of construction.

## Water and the regulatory calendar

The site is bounded to the south by the rivière des Mille Îles and includes an island. The city states that its civil security organization monitors water levels with Hydro-Météo and that an ice jam can cause flooding in winter.

The provincial framework has changed: the new regulation on flood zones has been in force since March 1, 2026 and sorts zones into four intensities, from low to very high, according to the Government of Quebec. The preliminary maps of the Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal, presented in Terrebonne on February 26, 2025, cover the rivière des Mille Îles, but their publication was postponed. So the check is done address by address.

## Transit, schools and work under way

Autoroute 25 serves the town centre at exit 22, toward route 344 and boulevard des Seigneurs, and meets autoroute 640, which runs along the rivière des Mille Îles. The exo train, line 15 Mascouche, stops at gare Terrebonne, outside the old core, with about 700 park and ride spaces and no weekend service. École primaire Saint-Louis, at 539 rue St-Sacrement, falls under the Centre de services scolaire des Affluents.

The city also plans to bury the utility wires in Old Terrebonne, under Hydro-Québec's responsibility, in several phases, with paving and sidewalks redone: know which phase touches your street.

## Selling in Old Terrebonne

Here the paperwork sells the house as much as the house does. Gather the Ministry authorizations and the permits for every visible intervention, with the invoices: that is what is missing when a transaction stalls.

The city has extended its heritage restoration financial aid programme to inventoried buildings inside the declared heritage site. It reimburses 75 per cent of eligible expenses for restoring doors, windows and roofing with traditional materials, 60 per cent for other protected characteristic elements and 70 per cent for a building health record, up to $50,000 cumulative per property over the three years of the programme. Renovation with contemporary or imitation materials is excluded.
