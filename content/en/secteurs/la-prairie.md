La Prairie is one of Quebec's oldest municipalities, and it shows in the structure of its real estate market: a protected heritage core on one side, recently planned neighbourhoods on the other.

## Two markets in one city

**Vieux-La Prairie** is a declared historic district. Properties there have character that cannot be reproduced, but they come with a strict regulatory framework: materials, windows, colours and facade modifications are all governed. A buyer planning work should verify what is permitted before making an offer, not after.

**Symbiocité and the recent developments** work in reverse: new or near-new properties, high standards, reliable comparables. The question here is not heritage but the construction warranty and the real state of the land.

## What I check here

**The historic district by-law**, before any exterior renovation project in Vieux-La Prairie.

**The residential construction warranty** and its status, for any property under ten years old: what is still covered, what is not, what has already been claimed.

**Drainage**, in sectors developed on former farmland where water management is a concrete issue.

## Selling and buying in La Prairie

For a seller, the key is targeting the right pool: Vieux-La Prairie and Symbiocité attract completely different buyers, and the marketing has to reflect that.
