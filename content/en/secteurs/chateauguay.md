Châteauguay offers one of the best floor-area-to-price ratios on the South Shore. It is also the city where the commute calculation matters most, because of the Mercier bridge.

## An accessible market

The housing stock is mostly single-family homes built between 1960 and 1990, with generous lots for the price. That is what attracts buyers priced out of Brossard, La Prairie or Candiac.

The city also has a significant historic anglophone community, which widens the buyer pool and makes bilingual service useful in practice, not just in theory.

**Vieux-Châteauguay** and the **river sector** offer mature lots and character that newer developments do not have.

**Nouvelle-France, Domaine Robitaille and Saint-Bernard** carry the family volume.

**The waterfront**, on the Châteauguay river and Lac Saint-Louis, is a distinct market.

## What to check here

**System age**, because a large share of the stock is between 35 and 60 years old: roof, electrical service, windows, French drain. It is the item that most often explains the gap between two apparently comparable properties.

**Flood zone mapping** along the river.

**Renovations without permits**, common in housing stock of this era: finished basement, extension, conversion. These are problems that surface at the notary, not during the visit.

## Selling and buying in Châteauguay

For a seller, preparation matters a lot: in a market where buyers are price-sensitive, a property whose obvious work has not been done gets negotiated hard.

The comparative analysis is prepared at no charge and tell you frankly what needs correcting before listing.
