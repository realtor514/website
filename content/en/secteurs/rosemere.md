Rosemère is a thin, expensive and illiquid market. Those three characteristics go together, and they completely change how you buy or sell here.

## Structurally limited supply

The city chose to preserve its large lots and tree cover rather than densify. The result: little new construction, few available lots, and housing stock made up mostly of single-family homes on lots you no longer find at this distance from Montreal.

That scarcity supports prices, but it has a flip side: transaction volume is low. In some sectors, a handful of properties sell in a year.

## Why online estimates fail here

An algorithm needs comparables. In Rosemère they are scarce and often unsuitable: two properties of the same floor area can have completely different lots, orientations and condition.

In practice, I widen the analysis to neighbouring cities when necessary, then adjust for the market difference. It is manual work, but it is the only way to reach a defensible price.

## What I check here

**Shoreline buffers and flood zones** for properties near the Rivière des Mille Îles.

**The condition of mature trees**, which are a real asset but also a maintenance cost and sometimes a risk to the foundation or roof.

**Septic systems and wells**, for unconnected properties.

## Selling in Rosemère

The clientele is narrow, so the marketing has to be precise: professional photography, showcasing the lot, and a price that reflects real comparables rather than aspiration.

I prepare the comparative analysis at no charge and tell you frankly what needs correcting before listing.
