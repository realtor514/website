Brossard has changed faster than any other South Shore city since the REM opened. It is an area I cover on the same footing as the others, and it demands a finer reading than average.

## A market that split in two

Since the REM went into service, Brossard functions as two distinct markets.

**The REM corridor** (Panama, Du Quartier, the DIX30 area) is dominated by condos and recent projects. Demand comes largely from buyers who work downtown and have calculated their door-to-door commute. It is also the part of Brossard where new supply is most abundant, which means a reseller competes directly with the developer.

**The residential alphabetical sectors** (A, B, C, L, M, N, O, P among others) remain single-family and semi-detached markets, with family buyers and far more stable price dynamics. Each letter corresponds to a development period: comparing a sector B property to a sector P sale produces a false estimate.

## What I look at before recommending an offer

**The contingency fund**, for any condo. A recent building is not a risk-free building: it is often where the fund is weakest, because the initial budget was set by the developer to look attractive.

**The declaration of co-ownership**, for short-term rental and pet restrictions, both of which directly affect resale.

**Parking**, in projects near the stations. A unit without a parking space resells to a considerably narrower pool.

## Selling in Brossard

If you are selling a condo near the REM, your competition is not only the neighbour: it is the developer's new inventory, with its warranties and incentives. The list price has to account for that from day one.

If you are selling a single-family home in a residential sector, the logic is reversed: inventory is limited and preparation matters more than timing.
