Varennes is one of the few South Shore cities that kept both genuine accessible river frontage and a heritage village core. That is what structures its market.

## A market organized around the river

**The riverfront sector** is a distinct market from the rest of the city. Few transactions, a narrow but constant clientele, and prices that follow their own logic. Two streets inland, you return to the municipal average.

**Vieux-Varennes**, around the Sainte-Anne basilica, offers real heritage character with the realities of older construction: foundations, insulation, electrical systems.

**Marie-Victorin, Domaine des Îles and the northern sectors** carry the family volume, with housing stock built mostly since 1980.

## What I prioritize here

**Shoreline buffers and flood zone mapping**, for any property near the river. It affects insurability, financing and resale.

**Precise location relative to the industrial zones**, which varies considerably from one sector to another.

**System age** in Vieux-Varennes, where part of the stock is over fifty years old.

## Selling and buying in Varennes

With thinner inventory than Sainte-Julie or Boucherville, a well-prepared and correctly priced property generally receives its best offers in the first three weeks. An overpriced property misses that window and then sells lower.

I prepare the comparative analysis at no charge, clearly separating the riverfront market from the rest of the city.
