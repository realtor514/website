Carignan is a fast-growing city. More than half of its dwellings were built since 2001, according to the 2021 census, and 80% are single-detached houses. The territory is split in two by Chambly and bordered by the Richelieu, the L'Acadie River and large farming areas. It is an area I cover on the same footing as the others.

## A market of recent homes

Carignan's housing stock is young: 22% of dwellings were built between 2016 and 2021 alone.

**The new developments** offer many recent comparables. For a seller, the direct competition is often a new home of a similar model, and the list price has to account for it.

**The riverfront sectors**, along the Richelieu and the L'Acadie River, and **Île Goyer and Île aux Lièvres** form a market of their own, where the view and water access count as much as the house.

**The more rural sectors** offer large lots, but not always municipal services.

## What to check here

**Flood zones** near the Richelieu and the L'Acadie River. They affect insurability, financing and resale.

**Septic systems and wells.** Part of the city is not connected to municipal water and sewer, and septic tank pumping is mandatory there. Ask for the pumping records and the condition of the leach field.

**Taxes and warranty on new homes.** For a house bought from a builder, GST and QST apply: check whether they are included in the advertised price. Also check what the new home warranty still covers, and until when.

## Selling and buying in Carignan

Price is read against new construction and against [Chambly](/en/real-estate-broker/chambly/), its immediate neighbour. A resale home listed at the price of a comparable new home attracts few buyers.

The comparative analysis is prepared at no charge. On the buying side, plan for [closing costs](/en/tools/closing-costs/) and the [welcome tax](/en/tools/welcome-tax/), which come on top of the down payment.
