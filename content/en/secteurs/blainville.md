Blainville holds a particular position in the Lower Laurentians: it is often the reference point buyers compare other cities in the region against, even when they end up buying elsewhere.

## What supports prices here

Blainville was developed with more rigorous urban planning than the regional average. Newer sectors preserved green space, commercial arteries were concentrated, and the housing stock is largely single-family homes in good condition.

Add to that a school reputation that directly drives demand. A significant share of Blainville buyers are families relocating specifically for the school sector, which makes demand more resilient than in cities where price is the only argument.

The result: a market where corrections are generally less severe than elsewhere in the Laurentians, but where the entry price excludes part of the buyer pool.

## The sectors

**Fontainebleau** is the city's upper-end sector, with larger properties and higher construction standards. Resale depends heavily on marketing quality: the buyer pool is narrower, and a poorly presented property sits on the market.

**Chante-Bois and Notre-Dame** are the family core of Blainville: homes from the 1980s through the 2000s, residential streets, schools nearby. This is the most active segment of the city.

**The Domaine Vert area** benefits from proximity to the regional park, a concrete selling point for part of the clientele.

## Selling in Blainville

In a market where buyers have high expectations, preparation is not an extra: it is the condition for a good price.

In practice, properties that sell quickly and at asking price in Blainville share three things: they are decluttered, visible minor repairs are done, and the photography is professional. Properties that accumulate days on market have almost always failed on at least one of those three points, before price is even the issue.
