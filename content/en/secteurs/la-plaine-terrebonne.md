In the third quarter of 2026, single-family homes there sold at a median price of $520,000, compared with $632,500 in the Terrebonne sector and $695,000 in Lachenaie, according to Centris.

## From farming parish to Terrebonne sector

According to the City of Terrebonne, the rang de La Plaine sud, today chemin Forest, appears in 1840, and Canadian Pacific built a station at La Plaine in 1903. The municipality of the Parish of Saint-Joachim was founded in 1922 and became the municipality of La Plaine in 1969. In 2001, La Plaine, Lachenaie and Terrebonne merged to form the current city.

The growth is recent. The sector had 19,415 residents in 2006 and 24,235 in 2016, an increase of nearly 25% in ten years, according to the City's parks master plan. Centris reports 24,706 at the 2021 census. Boundaries differ slightly from one source to another, but the order of magnitude is the same: about 25,000 residents.

## What sets the sector apart

The parks master plan describes the sector as "somewhat off-centre in the north of the territory": a small densely urbanized part and, for the rest, woodland and farmland. It is also the only urbanized sector of Terrebonne located outside the band between the Rivière des Mille Îles and Autoroute 640.

Route 337, boulevard Laurier, crosses the sector and is its main artery. Autoroute 640 does not pass through La Plaine: you reach it by Route 337, at exit 38.

The plan counts 9 neighbourhood parks, 7 sector parks and two large parks in the sector, Philippe-Villeneuve and du Boisé. It notes, however, that the north of the sector is not served by any large park.

## What to check before buying

**The agricultural zone.** According to the City, 37% of Terrebonne's territory is in the agricultural zone, and a good part of La Plaine is woodland and farmland. In the agricultural zone, several actions, such as subdividing a lot or changing its use, require authorization from the Commission de protection du territoire agricole du Québec. For a large lot or a property on the edge of this zone, check what is allowed before the offer, not after.

**Water and sewer.** In a partly rural sector, do not assume the connection. Check whether the property is connected to municipal water and sewer, or whether it has a well and a septic system, which are inspected and maintained differently.

**Flood zones.** The Mascouche River borders the sector to the south. The provincial flood zone framework has been in force since March 1, 2026, and the status is checked address by address. I verify the applicable map before the offer, because the status affects insurance and financing.

**The commute.** Drive the actual route to your workplace, at the time you would really make it. That is the true price of the gap with Lachenaie.
