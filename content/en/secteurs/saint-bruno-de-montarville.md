Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville is a city in the Longueuil agglomeration, without being part of the City of Longueuil. It had 26,273 residents at Statistics Canada's 2021 census, according to Centris. It is an expensive, thin market: about 58 single-family sales per quarter, at a median price above $880,000.

## Assessment roll and welcome tax

Property assessment is handled by the assessment department of the Longueuil agglomeration. The 2025-2026-2027 roll reflects the market on July 1, 2023. According to the City of Longueuil, the average value of a single-family home in Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville on this roll is $725,800, up 38% from the previous roll, and that of a condominium unit is $535,900 (+32%).

For transfer duties, the City applies a 3% rate in 2026 on the portion above $500,000. The tax base is the highest of the sale price, the amount stated in the deed and the roll value multiplied by the comparative factor, 1.06 in 2026. For a house sold at $882,500, the calculation gives $17,085.50, of which $11,475 is for the portion above $500,000 alone. How it works, and the 2027 thresholds, are in the [welcome tax guide](/en/articles/welcome-tax-quebec-guide/).

## What sets this market apart

Mont-Saint-Bruno National Park, managed by Sépaq, is in the city. According to Sépaq, it includes five lakes, an orchard, a historic mill and dozens of kilometres of trails. The park takes part of the territory out of residential development, which limits new construction.

Saint-Bruno station, at 1200, rue Lenoir, is on exo's 13 Mont-Saint-Hilaire train line, in fare zone B, with 549 parking spaces.

Public schools fall under the Centre de services scolaire des Patriotes (French) and the Riverside School Board (English), according to the City.

## The sectors

**Vieux-Saint-Bruno**, with mature lots and services close by.

**Montarville and Des Tilleuls**, residential streets of homes from the 1970s to the 1990s.

**Sommet Trinité** and the newer sectors, with larger and more uniform properties.

**The lakes sector**, along the park, with few transactions per year.

## What to check here

**Servitudes and zoning constraints** for properties next to the national park. Some lots carry development restrictions that do not appear in the listing.

**Drainage** in the sloped sectors at the foot of the mountain, where runoff management matters.

**The septic system and well**, for properties not connected to municipal networks.
