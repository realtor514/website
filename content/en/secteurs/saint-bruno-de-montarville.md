Saint-Bruno-de-Montarville is a thin, sought-after market. That combination produces particular behaviour: little inventory, ready buyers, and wide gaps between a well-presented property and another.

## What sets this market apart

The Mont-Saint-Bruno national park occupies a significant share of the territory, which mechanically limits residential development. Fewer available lots means less new construction, therefore less downward pressure on the existing stock.

The school reputation attracts a family clientele that moves specifically for the sector, which makes demand less cycle-sensitive than in cities where price is the only argument.

Finally, the walkable town centre is a real asset: few suburbs in the region have one that genuinely works.

## The sectors

**Vieux-Saint-Bruno** is the most sought after, with mature lots and immediate proximity to services.

**Montarville and Des Tilleuls** are the family core: homes from the 1970s through the 1990s, quiet residential streets.

**Sommet Trinité** and the newer sectors offer larger, more standardized properties with more predictable resale.

**The lakes sector**, bordering the park, is a distinct market with few transactions and steady demand.

## What I check here

**Servitudes and zoning constraints** for properties adjacent to the national park. Some lots carry development restrictions that do not appear in the listing.

**Drainage** in the sloped sectors at the foot of the mountain, where runoff management is a real issue.

**The age of the septic system and well**, for the few properties not connected to municipal services.

## Selling in Saint-Bruno

With thin inventory, the first three weeks are decisive. A correctly priced, well-prepared property receives its best offers in that window. An overpriced property misses it and then sells below what it should have.
