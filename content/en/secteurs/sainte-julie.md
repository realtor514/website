Sainte-Julie is a stable family market with homogeneous housing stock and reliable comparables. It is one of the areas where the analysis is easiest to do correctly, provided you respect the logic of the neighbourhoods.

## A readable market

The city developed in successive phases since the 1970s, producing coherent sectors where properties resemble each other enough for comparison to mean something.

**The Village sector** is the older core, with mature lots and proximity to services.

**Le Grand Coteau and Domaine des Hauts-Bois** carry the family volume: homes from 1985 to 2005, residential streets, schools nearby.

**The northern sectors and Côte Saint-Louis** offer larger lots and a more rural feel.

School reputation is a real demand driver here: a significant share of buyers move specifically for the sector, which makes the market more resilient than in cities where price is the only argument.

## What I check here

**System age** in the 1980s sectors: roof, windows, French drain. A property with several systems reaching end of life at once is not worth what an identical, updated property is worth.

**The in-ground pool history** when there is one: it is the item whose cost surprises buyers most.

**Drainage** in the sloped sectors of the coteau.

## Selling and buying in Sainte-Julie

The market is seasonal: families buy around the school calendar, creating a denser spring window. Outside that period, the list price has to be more precise because there are fewer buyers to absorb a mistake.
