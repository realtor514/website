In the third quarter of 2026, single-family homes in Westmount sold at a median price of $2,525,000, a little more than three times the median for the island of Montreal ($812,000), according to Centris. In the borough of Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce (CDN-NDG), the median reached $1,289,500. For Outremont, Hampstead, Mont-Royal (the Town of Mount Royal), Montréal-Ouest (Montreal West) and Côte-Saint-Luc, Centris publishes no quarterly median: there were too few sales.

## Central-west Montreal by the numbers

The sector brings together two boroughs of the City of Montreal, Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce and Outremont, and five related municipalities (villes liées), each with its own council, budget and tax rates: Westmount, Mont-Royal, Hampstead, Côte-Saint-Luc and Montréal-Ouest. The borough dominates: 170,583 residents in the 2021 census according to its Centris page, and 181 residential sales in the quarter (-5%), against 56 in Westmount (+9%). The island recorded 3,344 (-13%). The changes compare with summer 2025; changes in time on market are expressed in days.

## What changed in a year

**A 24% increase to read with caution.** Westmount's quarterly median rests on 41 sales; over four quarters, the increase is 7%. A change in the median price does not measure the change in value of any given house, because the mix of properties sold changes from one quarter to the next.

**Supply is shrinking in three related municipalities and growing in the borough.** Across all categories, active listings fell in Hampstead (-15%), Mont-Royal (-10%) and Westmount (-6%), but rose 22% in CDN-NDG and 12% on the island. According to the APCIQ barometer, the Centre sector is the only one of the island's 18 sectors where they are decreasing (-5%).

**The regional context.** According to APCIQ, in the third quarter, single-family sellers keep the advantage in each of the major sectors of the metropolitan area, while condos on the island sit on the border between a balanced market and one that favours buyers. The Bank of Canada's policy rate remains at 2.25% as of October 20, 2026; the next announcement is scheduled for October 28.

**The assessment roll.** On the 2026-2027-2028 roll, which reflects the market as of July 1, 2024, the average single-family home is valued at $2,739,500 in Westmount, $2,324,400 in Outremont, $2,137,200 in Hampstead and $2,000,500 in Mont-Royal, against $927,300 in the agglomeration. Compared with the previous roll, single-family values rose 2.4% in Mont-Royal, 4.9% in Westmount and 5.0% in CDN-NDG, against 12.1% in the City of Montreal.

**Property tax.** Montreal's 2026 budget puts the tax bill for the average single-family home at $7,442 in Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce (+1.4%, the smallest increase of the 19 boroughs), $13,800 in Outremont (+3.1%) and $4,939 (+4.3%) for the city. The related municipalities vote their own rates: for 2026, Westmount shows a total rate of $0.6583 per $100 of assessment for residential properties of five dwellings or fewer. Elsewhere, ask the seller for the latest tax bill.

## What is moving in and around Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce

**Namur-Hippodrome: a plan, no timetable.** According to the City of Montreal's project page, updated on June 4, 2026, this future 95-hectare neighbourhood near Namur station, on the site of the former racetrack, is to hold 18,500 to 20,000 dwellings, more than half of them non-market housing. The master plan has been adopted since December 2024; the next step is welcoming the first real estate projects, with no construction date published.

**Transit.** Two REM stations now in service are located, according to their station pages, in Mont-Royal and in Outremont: Ville-de-Mont-Royal, on chemin Canora, and Édouard-Montpetit, which connects with the blue line. The mobility projects for Montreal's greater Southwest, whose study area includes Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, Westmount and Montréal-Ouest, were removed from the Plan québécois des infrastructures (Quebec's infrastructure plan) in Quebec's 2025-2026 budget, according to ARTM's capital expenditure program.

**Plexes: no exemption to convert.** Converting a rental building into divided co-ownership is prohibited throughout the agglomeration unless an exemption is granted, and the CDN-NDG borough has granted none since October 9, 2024 (by-law RCA24 17403). In CDN-NDG, a plex buyer therefore cannot count on a conversion to resell the units one by one. Undivided co-ownership follows other rules, described in [undivided co-ownership in Montreal](/en/articles/undivided-co-ownership-montreal/).

**Short-term rentals.** Outremont prohibits commercial tourist residences; CDN-NDG allows them only in certain commercial zones, such as boulevard Décarie.

**The rental market.** In CMHC's October 2025 survey, the most recent, the vacancy rate rose in one year from 2.5% to 4.0% in the "N.-D.-de-Grâce/Ct-St-Luc etc." zone and from 2.1% to 2.9% in the "Ct-des-Neiges/Mt-Royal/Outremont" zone. From January to September 2026, the first zone recorded 43 housing starts, against 254 a year earlier, and the second 492, against 425.

## Selling or buying in central-west Montreal

**Selling in a small related municipality.** With 12 single-family sales during the quarter in Hampstead and 8 in Montréal-Ouest, no median is published: the price is defended with comparable sales from the last four quarters. The method is described in [real estate comparables: auditing your neighbour's price](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).

**Selling in Westmount.** During the quarter, single-family homes sold there stayed on the market 102 days on average, six more than a year earlier, and twice the island's 51 days according to Centris. An average does not predict how long a particular address will take to sell.

**Buying a condo in Côte-des-Neiges or Notre-Dame-de-Grâce.** The choice has widened: 27% more listings in a year. On $700,000, transfer duties there come to $9,349.

**Buying a plex.** Start from the rents actually collected, in zones where vacancy has risen according to CMHC, and in CDN-NDG, do not count on a conversion to divided co-ownership.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it a seller's or a buyer's market in Westmount, Outremont or Mont-Royal?** APCIQ does not rule city by city, but by price range for its Centre sector, for single-family homes, over 12 months. Below $2,500,000, the market there favours sellers (4.1 to 7.2 months of inventory); from $2,500,000 to $3,000,000, it is balanced (8.3 months); at $3,000,000 and above, it favours buyers (16.0 months). How to read these ranges is explained in [the luxury real estate market in Montreal](/en/articles/luxury-market-montreal-laval-data/).

**Why is the welcome tax higher in Westmount than in Mont-Royal for the same price?** Because each city sets its own rate above $500,000. Westmount applies 3% from that threshold; Mont-Royal keeps 1.5% up to $630,100. On $1,500,000, the gap is $9,452.

**Will the Namur-Hippodrome project move prices in Côte-des-Neiges-Notre-Dame-de-Grâce?** No official source measures such an effect, and the City publishes no construction date.
