When a couple separates, the home is often the largest asset on the table. Yet
most of the decisions are technical: who has rights to what, who can finance
what, and how much a sale will really leave in your pocket.

I am a real estate broker, not a lawyer. What follows is general information, so
you arrive better prepared at your lawyer's, notary's or mediator's office. They
apply the rules to your situation.

## First question: what is your status as a couple?

| Status | Sharing the value of the home | Family residence protection |
|---|---|---|
| Married or in a civil union | Family patrimony, mandatory | Yes |
| De facto spouses in a parental union | Parental union patrimony, unless you opt out before a notary | Yes |
| Other de facto (common-law) spouses | None: the title and your written agreements decide | No |

## Married or civil union: the family patrimony

Marriage creates a family patrimony, regardless of which spouse holds title
(article 414 of the Civil Code of Québec). A civil union has the same effect. It
includes the family residences, their furniture, the family vehicles and pension
rights accumulated during the marriage.

On divorce or legal separation, its net value is divided equally. Three details
often change the math:

- **Only certain debts are deducted**: those taken on to acquire, improve,
  maintain or preserve the home. If the mortgage was increased to pay for a car,
  that portion is not automatically deducted. "Value minus mortgage balance" can
  therefore be wrong.
- **Some contributions come out of the calculation**: the home's net value at
  the time of the marriage if one of you already owned it, or an inheritance
  invested in it, along with the matching share of the increase in value.
- **The date matters**: in principle, market value on the date the divorce
  application is filed, or the date you stopped living together if the court so
  decides.

What is divided is a value, not the property itself: the partition is paid in
money or by transferring an asset. And you cannot waive it in advance.

## De facto spouses: the new parental union

Since June 30, 2025, de facto spouses who become parents of a child born or
adopted on or after that date are automatically in a parental union. Couples who
already had children enter it at the birth or adoption of their next child
together, or can opt in voluntarily.

The parental union patrimony includes the family residences, their furniture and
the family vehicles, but not pension plans. When the union ends, its net value
is divided equally, regardless of who owns the home, unless the spouses opted
out by notarial deed. Watch the clock: family residence protections last only
120 days after the end of cohabitation, and some court applications must be
filed within that period.

Other de facto spouses have neither a shared patrimony nor family residence
protection. If the home is in the other partner's name, the non-owner generally
cannot stay in it or oppose its sale. A partner who contributed to it can
sometimes claim compensation for unjust enrichment.

## When only one name is on the title

For a couple who is married, in a civil union or in a parental union, the owner
of a building with fewer than five dwellings used as the family residence cannot
sell or mortgage it without the other spouse's written consent.

The **declaration of family residence**, which either spouse can have registered
in the land register through a notary or a lawyer, changes everything. If it was
registered, a sale made without consent can be annulled. Without it, the sale
stands, although a claim for damages remains possible.

## The three options, and their traps

**Sell and split.** Both co-owners sign the brokerage contract, then the deed of
sale. The trap: reasoning on the sale price. What gets divided is the net
proceeds, after the mortgage penalty and selling costs, all detailed in [the
real cost of selling a house in
Quebec](/en/articles/real-cost-selling-home-quebec/).

**One spouse buys out the other.** The spouse keeping the home must qualify
alone, on their own income and credit, at the lender's qualifying rate. They
must finance the mortgage balance plus the other spouse's share, and a refinance
is generally capped at 80% of the home's value. Some lenders and mortgage
insurers have spousal buyout programs. The [affordability
calculator](/en/tools/affordability/) gives you a first order of magnitude, your
mortgage broker gives you the real answer.

**Stay co-owners for a while.** Useful to finish a school year. But in Quebec,
no one can be forced to remain in undivided co-ownership: either of you can ask
for a partition, and a court can go as far as ordering the sale. The trap is not
having a written agreement: who pays the mortgage, taxes and repairs, what
compensation the person living there alone owes the other, and how the exit will
work.

## The mortgage: what people find out too late

**The co-borrower stays liable.** Your agreement does not bind the bank. Until
the lender releases the departing spouse, both remain liable for the entire
debt.

**Breaking the mortgage is expensive.** On a fixed rate, the penalty is usually
the higher of three months' interest or the interest rate differential, often
several thousand dollars. Ask for the exact amount before choosing an option. If
one of you buys elsewhere, porting the mortgage, where the contract allows it,
can avoid the penalty.

## Income tax and welcome tax

**Principal residence.** If the home was your principal residence for every year
you owned it, the gain is generally exempt, but the sale must still be reported:
Schedule 3 and Form T2091 federally, Form TP-274-V in Quebec. If one of you buys
another property while still a co-owner, only one property per year can be
designated, and part of the gain could become taxable.

**Transfer between spouses.** A transfer to a spouse or former spouse in
settlement of rights arising from the relationship is generally tax-deferred:
the receiving spouse takes over the other's tax cost.

**Welcome tax (transfer duties).** A transfer between married or civil union
spouses is exempt as long as the union has not been dissolved. Once the divorce
takes effect, the exemption may no longer apply. Between former de facto
spouses, the transfer is exempt if it happens within 12 months of the
separation, or within 30 days of a summary of agreements signed by an accredited
mediator, the court approval of that agreement or a final judgment, under
conditions. The municipality can still charge a compensatory duty, set at $200
in most cases. Your notary will confirm the timing.

## Selling when communication is difficult

- Agree in writing, through your lawyers or in mediation, on the list price, the
  threshold for accepting an offer and how showings will work.
- I present every offer to both sellers, at the same time and in writing.
- If direct communication is hard, it goes through the lawyers or the mediator.

Couples with dependent children are entitled to 5 hours of free family mediation
with an accredited mediator (2.5 hours for a review). Couples with no common
dependent children, whether married, in a civil union or de facto, are entitled
to up to 3 free hours. Mediation can cover the division of property, not just
custody.

## A neutral value, before any negotiation

If each of you arrives with a different number, talks stall. A chartered
appraiser produces a formal report, preferable if the file may end up in court.
A broker's comparative market analysis, based on recent sales in the area,
quickly gives a realistic range. For a first benchmark, try the [home value
estimator](/en/tools/home-estimate/).

My role in these files is to be neutral: the same data, at the same time, for
both parties.
