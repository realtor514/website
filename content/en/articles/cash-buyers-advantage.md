Paying cash does not buy you an automatic discount. It buys certainty, and
certainty is paid for in protections given up. Here is what actually changes
when a buyer removes the financing condition, what that buyer loses along the
way, and what Quebec law requires the moment physical money enters a real
estate transaction.

## What the seller is buying is not your money

The seller receives the same amount whether it comes from your account or from
a mortgage lender. The advantage of a cash offer rests entirely on one thing:
the absence of a financing condition.

A promise to purchase conditional on financing opens a window during which the
buyer can walk away if the loan is not granted. For the seller, that window is
uncertainty: the property is out of circulation, other interested buyers move
on, and if the condition fails, the seller starts again with a listing that
now has a history. That window is what the cash buyer removes, and it is all
the cash buyer removes.

I will not give you a discount percentage. No official body publishes the
price gap a Quebec seller grants a cash offer. The numbers that circulate on
this are impressions, not data.

## Cash and pre-approved are not the same thing

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada is explicit: pre-approval does not
guarantee that you will get a mortgage. A lender can refuse a pre-approved
buyer, in particular because the property you want has to meet the lender's
own standards, or because of credit history. The pre-approved amount is a
possible maximum, not a commitment. Only the rate is genuinely held, for 60 to
130 days depending on the lender.

A financed buyer therefore brings two unknowns to the seller: the buyer and
the building. Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation sets the minimum down
payment at 5% up to $500,000, then 10% on the portion above that threshold,
and requires the property value to be below $1,500,000 for an insured
homeowner loan. Housing costs should not exceed 39% of gross household income,
and total debt obligations should not exceed 44%. A cash buyer passes through
none of these filters. That is where the advantage lives, and nowhere else. If
you are financing, the [pre-approval
mistakes](/en/articles/mortgage-preapproval-mistakes/) are where your file is
won or lost.

## Removing the condition: what you give up

An accepted promise to purchase is not an intention. Under article 1712 of the
Civil Code of Quebec, failure to sign the deed gives the other party the right
to obtain a judgment that stands in its place. In plain terms, a seller can
force you to buy. Article 1711 adds that any sum paid on the occasion of a
promise of sale is presumed to be a down payment on the price, which means
committed money, not a refundable good faith gesture.

The financing condition is the door through which a buyer leaves without
damage. Removing it is not boldness, it is giving up that door. It is done
only with a lender who has seen your complete file and the building itself,
never on the strength of a generic letter.

## The inspection has nothing to do with financing

This is the classic blind spot of the cash buyer: "I have no conditions, so I
skip the inspection." The two conditions do not serve the same purpose.

The Regulation respecting brokerage requirements, professional conduct of
brokers and advertising in fact requires the broker to recommend to every
buyer a complete inspection by a professional who carries liability insurance
against faults, errors or omissions, uses a recognized inspection service
agreement, inspects according to a recognized standard of practice and
delivers a written report (section 81).

The Civil Code explains why. Article 1726 imposes on the seller a warranty
against latent defects, but adds that the seller does not warrant an apparent
defect, and that a defect is apparent when a prudent and diligent buyer can
see it without needing an expert. Waiving the expert therefore widens, by your
own hand, the zone of what will be treated as apparent. Article 1733 adds that
a limitation of warranty holds in favour of a non-professional seller when the
buyer buys at their own risk. And article 1739 requires, once a defect is
found, written notice to the seller within a reasonable time.

If the calendar worries you, the inspection can be done before the offer is
submitted rather than after. The [cost and timing of a pre-purchase
inspection](/en/articles/home-inspection-cost-timing-quebec/) can be planned.

## Cash triggers duties that a wire transfer does not

Real estate brokers and agencies are reporting entities under the Proceeds of
Crime (Money Laundering) and Terrorist Financing Act. In concrete terms,
according to FINTRAC:

- a receipt of funds record is required whenever they receive funds, in any
  amount;
- a large cash transaction of $10,000 or more must be reported, and the
  24 hour rule groups amounts received within the same day;
- a suspicious transaction is reported as soon as practicable;
- records are kept for at least five years;
- the beneficial owner holding 25% or more must be identified, and politically
  exposed person status determined.

Quebec law is layered on top. Section 29 of the same brokerage regulation
requires the broker to verify the identity of every party to the transaction
who is not represented by another licence holder, and section 30, their legal
capacity. The OACIQ is categorical: if a party refuses to provide the
identification documents, the broker cannot act for that party and must
withdraw from the transaction. The OACIQ also notes that above a threshold of
$100,000 received in cash, the licence holder must, among other things,
establish the source of the cash and of the person's wealth, and have senior
management review the transaction.

And the notary? The common belief is that the same regime applies. It does
not, and the OACIQ says so itself in its guideline: certain professions,
including notaries and lawyers, are not subject to all the requirements of
that Act. This flows from the 2015 Supreme Court ruling on legal
professionals, whom the federal statute defines in Quebec as lawyers and
notaries.

This absolutely does not mean cash sails through. The Regulation respecting
the accounting and standards of professional practice of notaries is stricter
than the federal threshold: its section 6 forbids a notary from receiving from
anyone cash totalling $7,500 or more under a single service contract, and its
section 32 forbids the notary from withdrawing cash from the trust account.
Add that the Bank of Canada removed legal tender status from the $500 and
$1,000 notes on January 1, 2021, which leaves the $100 note as the largest
denomination. A cash transaction in Quebec is therefore, in practice, a
transfer from a documented account, not a suitcase. If the word intrigues you,
see [what the notary actually does](/en/articles/quebec-notary-process/).

## If you are financing, the game is played elsewhere

| Lever | What actually counts |
|-------|----------------------|
| Pre-approval | A complete file verified by the lender, not an estimate |
| Condition period | It is written into the form and is negotiable |
| Deposit | The broker must recommend a reasonable one, held in trust |
| Inspection | Before the offer if timing demands it, never dropped |
| Mortgage broker | Reachable while offers are being presented |

A financed buyer who controls those five points offers certainty close to that
of cash. The rest is price, and price is a conversation: read [the truth about
bidding wars](/en/articles/bidding-wars-truth-montreal/) before you settle on
your number.
