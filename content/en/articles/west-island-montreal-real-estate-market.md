Between July and September 2026, the single-family median prices published by Centris for the West Island of Montreal ranged from $635,000 in Pierrefonds-Roxboro to $1,005,000 in Kirkland, against $812,000 for the island as a whole. It was also the first full quarter with all four stations of the REM's Anse-à-l'Orme branch in service. The figures go city by city: there is no median for the West Island as a whole.

## Pointe-Claire, Kirkland, Dollard-des-Ormeaux: nine territories compared

**Kirkland, the only published quarterly median above one million.** It rose 12% on 43 sales, but only 2% over four quarters. A median follows the mix of homes that sell: it does not mean that any given house is worth 12% more.

**Baie-D'Urfé and Beaconsfield lead over a full year**, at $1,225,437 and $987,500 over four quarters. In Senneville, one sale in the quarter and four in a year: no price is published.

**Pierrefonds-Roxboro carries the volume**: 95 single-family sales, the lowest median of the sector, and 33 days on market against 51 for the island.

**Pointe-Claire sells fastest**, in 29 days, 9 fewer than a year earlier. **Selling times are getting longer** in Dollard-des-Ormeaux (56 days, 26 more) and in L'Île-Bizard (63 days, 24 more).

The profile of the city as a whole is on [the Montreal page](/en/real-estate-broker/montreal/).

## What changed in a year in the West Island

**More properties for sale almost everywhere.** All categories combined, active listings rose in six territories, from 10% in Pierrefonds-Roxboro to 54% in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue, and fell in Kirkland (-13%) and Beaconsfield (-3%). Sales fell 25% in Dollard-des-Ormeaux, but rose 23% in Kirkland. Across the whole island: 12% more listings, 13% fewer sales.

**More condos for sale.** Over four quarters, active condo listings rose 48% in Pointe-Claire and 43% in Pierrefonds-Roxboro.

**Construction.** From January to September 2026, CMHC counted 261 housing starts in Kirkland, 239 of them apartments, against 3 a year earlier.

**APCIQ's verdict, price range by price range.** Months of inventory divide the average inventory by the average monthly sales of the last 12 months: below 8, the market favours sellers; from 8 to 10, it is balanced; above 10, it favours buyers. The barometer's South area also includes Dorval and L'Île-Dorval; the North area groups Kirkland, Dollard-des-Ormeaux, Pierrefonds-Roxboro and L'Île-Bizard. ## Water: Pierrefonds-Roxboro, L'Île-Bizard and the waterfront towns

**Eight territories out of nine are named.** For spring flooding, the City of Montreal names every territory in the sector except Dollard-des-Ormeaux, which it lists for heavy rain. It is a list of territories, not of addresses.

**What the municipalities say.** Pierrefonds-Roxboro asks residents of the areas flooded in 2017 and 2019, whether by the river, the water table or through the basement, to consider themselves in a flood zone. In Senneville, the Village warns of flooding, ice and frazil ice from the Lac des Deux Montagnes.

**Which map applies.** The first batch of new maps from the Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal (CMM), which covers, among others, the Rivière des Prairies, the Lac des Deux Montagnes and Lac Saint-Louis, was to come into force on August 1, 2026. Quebec's environment ministry postponed it "to an undetermined date"; on October 13, the CMM was still calling for its publication "without delay." Until then, the old-generation maps apply, and what the government's interactive map shows is indicative only, with no legal value. The checks to make before an offer are in [the article on buying in a flood zone](/en/articles/buying-in-flood-zone-quebec/).

On the same $800,000 base, the gap between Senneville and Pierrefonds-Roxboro reaches $3,261.50. I found no rate published by Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue: ask the City or your notary. The base can exceed the price paid if the roll value, adjusted by the comparative factor, is higher. Run your own numbers with the [transfer duties calculator](/en/tools/welcome-tax/).

**The 2026-2028 roll** measures the market as of July 1, 2024, against the market as of July 1, 2021. For single-family homes, the average increase ranges from 5.5% in Beaconsfield to 19.6% in Senneville, against 9.6% for the agglomeration; it is 11.7% in Pierrefonds-Roxboro and 14.1% in L'Île-Bizard.

**The tax bill.** In the City of Montreal's 2026 budget, the average residential tax burden rises 6.3% in L'Île-Bizard-Sainte-Geneviève, the steepest increase of the 19 boroughs, and 4.2% in Pierrefonds-Roxboro, against 3.8% for the City. According to the same budget, a building whose value rose more than the average generally sees its tax burden rise.

## Selling or buying in the West Island of Montreal

**For a seller.** In six territories out of nine, buyers have more choice than a year ago. The price is set from dated comparable sales on your street, not from a median: see [how to audit comparables](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/). Near the water, a buyer may ask which map applies: have the answer in writing.

**For a buyer.** Choice is growing, but below the luxury thresholds APCIQ classifies every single-family price range as favourable to sellers. Budget for the welcome tax of the city you are targeting and, in L'Île-Bizard, for the 2026 tax increase. Near the water, get the municipality's classification and the insurer's answer in writing before you waive your conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Has the REM pushed prices up in Kirkland and Pointe-Claire?**
None of the sources consulted measures it. The median rose in Kirkland and fell in Pointe-Claire, and the mix of sales changes from one quarter to the next. Before buying near a station, make the trip at rush hour.

**Has the West Island become a buyer's market?**
Not in the price ranges where most sales happen. For single-family homes, according to APCIQ, only the ranges above $1,230,000 in the South and above $1,110,000 in the North favour buyers.

**My house is in Pierrefonds-Roxboro, near the river. Which map applies?**
The new maps have no date of coming into force. Until then, according to the Government of Quebec, the municipality's old-generation maps apply; the special intervention zone (zone d'intervention spéciale) refers to them. Ask the borough, in writing and with the lot number, which map applies to your address.

To go further, read [how the welcome tax is calculated](/en/articles/welcome-tax-quebec-guide/) and [how to contest your municipal assessment](/en/articles/contest-property-assessment-quebec/).
