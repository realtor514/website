A plex is two purchases in one: a home for you and a small rental business.
It is neither a shortcut to wealth nor a trap reserved for experts. It works
when you run the numbers honestly and learn Quebec's rules before you sign.

General information, not legal, tax or financial advice: check your case with
your lender, notary and accountant.

## Living in the plex or just owning it

For a first-time buyer, an income property is almost always a plex of 2 to 4
units. From 5 units, financing becomes commercial.

**Owner-occupant:** you live in one unit, the rents cover part of your costs
and financing is easier to get. **Pure investment:** you do not live there,
the numbers must stand alone and financing is stricter. For a beginner,
living there is usually the realistic way in. I compare it with a condo in
[triplex vs condo in Montreal](/en/articles/triplex-vs-condo-montreal/).

## Financing: the general rules

According to CMHC, putting less than 20% down requires you to live in one
unit of a 1 to 4 unit building priced under $1,500,000.

| Situation | Minimum down payment |
|---|---|
| Owner-occupied duplex | 5% of the first $500,000, 10% on the rest |
| Owner-occupied triplex or fourplex | 10% |
| 2 to 4 unit plex you do not live in | 20% |

Below 20% down, the insurance premium is added to your loan, but the 9% tax on
it is paid in cash at the notary. Revenu Québec has announced that this rate will
rise to 9.975% for premiums paid after December 31, 2026.

For an owner-occupied building, CMHC lets you add up to 100% of a duplex's
gross rents to your income and, for a triplex or fourplex, up to 50% of gross
rents or the net rental income. Each lender has its own policy: get
pre-qualified on the actual building.

## Real cash flow: a method, not a promise

Here is the method, with round numbers **made up for the example**, not market
rents or prices. A triplex: you live in one unit, two are rented.

| Item | Per year |
|---|---|
| Gross rents: 2 units at $1,500 a month | $36,000 |
| Less: vacancy and unpaid rent (5% assumption) | $1,800 |
| Less: municipal and school taxes | $6,000 |
| Less: building insurance | $2,400 |
| Less: maintenance reserve | $4,800 |
| Less: heating and electricity paid by the owner | $1,800 |
| **Net income before mortgage** | **$19,200** |
| Less: mortgage payments ($2,600 a month) | $31,200 |
| **Gap you pay out of pocket** | **$12,000, or $1,000 a month** |

Here, your own unit costs $1,000 a month, part of which pays down
principal: compare it with the rent you pay today. For a pure investment, run
it with every unit rented: a negative gap means you subsidize the building.

Then replace every figure with a real one: leases, [tax
bills](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/), an insurance quote, energy bills
and the payment from the [mortgage calculator](/en/tools/mortgage/). Also
budget for the [welcome tax](/en/tools/welcome-tax/) on the whole building.

## Rents: what the law actually allows

In Quebec, tenants have the right to stay as long as they meet their
obligations, and the lease renews automatically.

For a 12-month lease, a rent increase notice goes out in writing 3 to 6 months
before the lease ends. The tenant has one month to answer, and silence counts
as acceptance. If they refuse, you have one month to ask the Tribunal
administratif du logement (TAL), Quebec's rental housing tribunal, to set the
rent. Otherwise the lease renews on the same terms.

Since January 1, 2026, the TAL has used a simplified method, with its own
[calculation tool](https://www.tal.gouv.qc.ca/en/calculation-for-rent-increase).
The base is Quebec's average CPI over three years: 3.1% for leases starting
between April 2, 2026 and April 1, 2027. On top of that come the share of tax
and insurance increases above that base, and major work, recovered at 5% of
its cost per year.

So a low rent does not catch up with the market in a year, even at turnover:
a new tenant can have the rent set if they pay more than the lowest rent of
the previous 12 months (section G of the lease).

**The section F exception.** In a building ready for occupancy for 5 years or
less, the TAL does not set the rent, as long as section F of the lease says
so. If the lease was signed, and the building was ready, after February 20,
2024, the lease must also state the maximum rent for those 5 years. Without
these mentions, there is no exception.

## Repossession, eviction, assignment: the current rules

- **Repossession.** You can take back a unit for yourself, your parents, your
  children or a relative who depends on you as their main support, with
  notice at least 6 months before the lease ends. Silence counts as a
  refusal, and you then have one month to apply to the TAL.
- **Seniors.** No repossession if the tenant or their spouse is 65 or older,
  has lived there 10 years or more and has an income of no more than 125%
  of the threshold for low-rental housing, with exceptions, such as an owner
  aged 65 or older who wants to live there.
- **Undivided co-ownership.** If you buy in indivision with someone other than
  your spouse, you generally cannot repossess any unit.
- **Eviction.** Evicting a tenant to subdivide, enlarge or change the use of a
  unit is prohibited until June 6, 2027, with exceptions. Do not buy counting
  on it.
- **Lease assignment.** Since February 21, 2024, you can refuse an assignment
  without a serious reason, but the lease is then terminated on the
  assignment date given. No answer within 15 days means you accepted.

## Buying a building with tenants in place

The sale does not end the leases: you take them over as they are. Before you
waive your conditions, get every lease, the latest modification notices, the
rent roll (who pays for heating) and any open TAL files. Compare the rents
declared with the leases and the actual deposits. Keep the invoices and tax
bills: they will justify your increases.

If every unit is rented and you want to live there, you send the repossession
notice yourself, after signing. Buy in March a plex whose leases end June 30,
and it is too late for this year: tell your lender. Also notify each tenant in
writing of the change of owner.

## Inspecting a plex, not a bungalow

Have every unit inspected, not just the vacant one (the seller gives tenants 24
hours' notice). My priorities:

- **Electrical:** service entrances and panels for each unit, old wiring or
  wiring changed without a permit.
- **Fire safety:** fire separations between units, exits, smoke alarms.
- **Water heaters:** how many, how old, owned or rented.
- **Heating oil:** demand proof that any tank, especially a buried one, has
  been removed, since it can complicate insurance.
- **Foundations:** cracks, leaks, drains, and pyrite under the basement slab,
  a known risk in the region.
- **Permits:** check with the city that the number of units is authorized and
  that major work had permits.

See also [when to do the
inspection](/en/articles/home-inspection-cost-timing-quebec/).

## Taxes and insurance

Rental income is taxable, federally (form T776) and in Quebec (TP-128).
Capital cost allowance (CCA) is optional, applies to the building and not the
land, and cannot create or increase a rental loss. When you sell, the CCA you
claimed can be recaptured into your income.

According to the Canada Revenue Agency, the principal residence exemption only
covers the part you live in: the gain on the rented part is taxable. See an
accountant before the offer.

Tell your insurer the building is rented, and check your civil liability and
loss of rent coverage. The law does not require your tenants to be insured,
but the lease can.
