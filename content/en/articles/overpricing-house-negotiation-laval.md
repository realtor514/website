Listing your house high to keep room for negotiation is a tactic that neither Centris nor APCIQ measures: neither one publishes the gap between asking price and sold price, in Laval or in Montreal. What does get measured is price cuts: in Laval, 44 days before the first one, and an average revision of -2.8%.

These APCIQ figures cover single-family homes, from January to August 2026.

## Listing your house high to negotiate: what the data measures

By its own definitions, every price in the [third quarter 2026 barometer from APCIQ](https://com.apciq.ca/sam/pdf/bar/2026/202603-bar-mtl-fr.pdf) (the Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers) for the Montreal metropolitan area is a sale price, as on the Centris statistics pages.

At the level of a single house, the rule is confidentiality: under the OACIQ guideline on the protection of personal information (section 7.2.4), the price obtained, "whether or not it matches the initial listing price," stays confidential until the deed is published in the Registre foncier (Quebec's land register). That gap, sale by sale, is covered in [the article on asking price and sold price in Montreal](/en/articles/list-price-vs-sold-price-montreal/).

**What is measured instead.** In October 2026, APCIQ published a study drawn from the Centris system, [Le marché décodé](https://extranet.apciq.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/marche-decode-oct-26-fr-2.pdf), devoted to revisions of the asking price. In Quebec, from January to September 2026, nearly 67,500 price reductions were applied to 46,900 properties, almost twice as many as in 2021. Larger supply explains most of it, but not all of it: there were nearly 18.5 revisions per month for every 100 properties for sale, compared with about 17 from 2023 to 2025. The first cut also comes sooner: after 57 days on average in 2026, compared with two months from 2023 to 2025.

**The average revision is not the gap with the sold price.** APCIQ defines it as the average of price revisions, meaning the new price compared with the previous price: it is the size of a revision, not the distance between the first price listed and the price paid. A property can also be cut more than once.

**In Laval, sellers revise sooner, and by less.** Laval's 44 days are the shortest delay of the 17 regions in the table, and its average revision of -2.8% is the second smallest, after Capitale-Nationale (-2.7%). APCIQ places Laval among the tight markets, where sellers adjust faster but more modestly; where inventory is higher, they wait longer and often cut more.

**Two selling times, depending on whether the price moved.** In the same study, with no regional breakdown, APCIQ also measures the average time between listing and sale: 46 days for a single-family home whose price was not revised, 119 days when it was; 51 versus 121 for condos, 55 versus 143 for plexes. This chart compares two groups of sales: it does not say what a different starting price would have brought in, and the timeline of a revised house includes the time it spent on the market before its cut.

## Overpricing your house: what it sets in motion, mechanism by mechanism

None of the official sources consulted puts a figure on what an inflated price costs or brings in at signing. The mechanisms, on the other hand, can be checked.

1. **Searching by price.** On Centris.ca, the price menu moves in $25,000 steps up to $500,000, then in $50,000 steps up to $1,000,000 (page viewed on October 20, 2026). A house listed at $760,000 does not appear in a search capped at $750,000, nor in the alerts sent when new properties match a saved search. In the brokers' database, searches can also be run by price, APCIQ notes. None of the sources consulted says which caps buyers choose.
2. **The price range.** For single-family homes in Laval, over 12 months, the barometer counts 2.8 months of inventory between $470,000 and $790,000, 4.6 months between $790,000 and $950,000, and 7.2 months above that. Going past $790,000 means entering a range where supply represents more months of sales; all three ranges remain classified "Seller." On the Island of Montreal, the $1,220,000 and up range reaches 9.1 months, classified "Balanced."
3. **The days add up.** APCIQ counts days on market from the signing of the brokerage contract. According to the OACIQ, a price cut during the contract goes through the Amendments form (clause M2.2), signed by the seller and the broker: the contract's signing date, the starting point of the count, stays the same.
4. **The visible history.** The Centris database reserved for brokers contains the asking price, the sale price and the property's sales history, according to APCIQ. On Centris.ca, a listing can carry the label "Baisse de prix" (price reduction), as a Laval house did on October 20, 2026.
5. **Completed sales, on the buyer's side.** According to the OACIQ, a broker can give a buyer client the sheets of sold comparable properties, once anything that identifies the seller has been masked. The buyer can therefore compare the listed price with completed sales.

If showings are not coming, the symptom-by-symptom diagnosis is in [the article on a home that is not selling](/en/articles/home-not-selling-quebec/).

## The price your broker recommends: the rule

According to the [OACIQ page on setting the selling price](https://www.oaciq.com/fr/grand-public/vendre/etablissement-du-juste-prix-vente-0/), updated on December 17, 2025, the broker sets the price or advises the seller so the seller can set it in an informed way, notably through a comparative analysis of similar properties recently sold in the same area. Section 76 of the brokers' ethics regulation requires an opinion of value that is "well-founded and substantiated"; section 112 requires the advertised price to be the one in the brokerage contract.

**The asking price is not necessarily the estimated value.** The [OACIQ guide to the brokerage contract for a sale](https://www.oaciq.com/fr/titulaires-de-permis/guides-pratiques-professionnelles/principaux-formulaires-en-courtage-immobilier-residentiel/contexte-dutilisation-du-formulaire-ccv/prix-et-conditions-de-vente-ccv/) has the broker compare the market value obtained with the asking prices of comparable properties for sale. Depending on the seller's objectives and the marketing strategy, the broker will draw from it "possibly another value that will constitute the asking price."

**The OACIQ expressly targets a price that is too low.** A price clearly below market value and the comparables, set to spark a bidding war, is not permitted, the OACIQ writes. During the contract, the broker must advise and inform you so that you can adjust the price as the market evolves.

**The listed price has contractual weight.** In the [OACIQ exclusive brokerage contract](https://www.oaciq.com/media/yr3lojn4/contrat-courtage-residentiel.pdf), point 2 of clause 7.1 provides for remuneration if you refuse a promesse d'achat (promise to purchase) that complies with the conditions of sale set out in the contract.

Before that analysis, the [home value estimator](/en/tools/home-estimate/) gives you a first order of magnitude. How to check the sales that were used is described in [the method for auditing comparables](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Does listing high to negotiate work in Laval?**
Neither Centris nor APCIQ publishes the gap between asking price and sold price that would confirm it or rule it out. What is published is price cuts: in Laval, APCIQ counts 44 days before the first price revision on a single-family home, from January to August 2026.

**House negotiation margin: is there a figure for Montreal?**
No: neither Centris nor APCIQ puts a number on it. The closest measure, the average revision of the asking price, was -3.8% for a single-family home in Montreal from January to August 2026, according to APCIQ, and it compares one price with the previous one, not with the price paid.

**Asking price vs sold price: where can I find the gap for a specific house?**
Neither Centris nor APCIQ publishes it. The sale price stays confidential until the deed is published in the Registre foncier, according to the OACIQ; before then, a broker can give you sheets on comparable sales, with the seller's identity masked.

**Asking price and negotiation: can I refuse a full-price offer?**
Yes, but not necessarily without cost: point 2 of clause 7.1 of the exclusive brokerage contract provides for remuneration if you refuse a promise to purchase that complies with the conditions of sale in the contract. Your notary or lawyer can confirm its scope.

**How long before lowering the price of a house in Laval?**
The decision is yours, and it is signed on the Amendments form, according to the OACIQ. APCIQ counts 44 days before the first price revision on a single-family home in Laval and 57 in Montreal, from January to August 2026.
