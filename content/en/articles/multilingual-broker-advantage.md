When a broker mentions working in several languages, the reaction is usually polite acknowledgement: convenient for some clients. The actual significance is broader than that.

## Language is not communication, it is comprehension

A real estate transaction involves legal documents, financial concepts and a regulatory framework that are hard to grasp fully in a second language. Not for lack of intelligence, but because nuance in a second language demands cognitive effort a native speaker does not spend.

Explaining a mortgage stress test to a buyer in their first language, or walking through a brokerage contract with a family in the language they use at home, is not repeating the same information in a different code. It is meeting people where their understanding is complete.

The difference shows up in one specific place: the questions asked. A buyer comfortable in the language of the transaction asks the awkward questions, the ones about what they did not understand. A buyer already working to keep up does not.

## The particular case of Greater Montreal's immigrant communities

Greater Montreal has substantial Arabic-speaking and Spanish-speaking communities: recent immigrants, established families, professionals and investors from Lebanon, Morocco, Algeria, Egypt, Syria, Colombia, Mexico and Venezuela. Many are approaching Canadian real estate for the first time.

Quebec's notarial system, the OACIQ regulatory framework, mortgage insurance rules and the mechanics specific to Quebec civil law are genuinely unfamiliar territory for a newcomer. These systems exist nowhere else in this form, and intuition acquired in another country is often misleading here.

## What it changes in practice

Service delivered in French, English, Spanish and Arabic with the same professional rigour changes three things:

**Nobody signs a document they do not understand.** That is the baseline, and it is far from universal.

**Multigenerational families can function.** When parents do not share a language with the children accompanying them, the transaction runs three ways. Being able to address each person directly prevents anyone deciding on someone else's behalf.

**Questions get asked at the right moment.** Before the offer, not at the notary.
