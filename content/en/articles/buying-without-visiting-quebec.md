You are in Beirut, in Bogota or in Calgary, and the property you want is in
Laval. Someone offers you a video tour. The question is not whether it can be
done: it happens every week in Quebec. The question is what to put in place so
the transaction still holds up the day you finally walk through the door for
real.

## What a Virtual Tour Shows, and What It Will Never Show

A video tour does show three things well: the layout of the rooms, how you move
between them, the general condition of the finishes. That is already enough to
rule a property out.

Its limits come from the nature of a camera. It has no sense of smell: heating
oil, mould and closet damp do not travel through a screen. It has no reliable
ear: the microphone compresses sound, and the highway 300 metres away
disappears. It feels neither a sloping floor, nor a draft under a window, nor
the temperature of a basement in February. The wide angle lens enlarges rooms,
and the hour of filming decides the light.

Keep in mind who is holding the camera, too. If the video comes from the
listing broker, it comes from the side that is selling. That does not make it
dishonest: the OACIQ imposes on every licence holder a duty to verify, meaning
to take steps to discover the factors that could unfavourably affect the
parties, and to disclose those that come to their knowledge. But a duty to
disclose is not an independent set of eyes.

## Buying Without Visiting Does Not Reduce Your Own Duty to Verify

This is the point most remote buyers miss. Article 1726 of the Code civil du
Québec says the seller is not bound to warrant against an apparent defect, and
that an apparent defect is one "that can be perceived by a prudent and diligent
buyer without the need to resort to an expert". The measure is not what you
were able to see on a screen. Article 1725 goes the same way for public law
restrictions: the seller is not answerable for them when a prudent and diligent
buyer could have discovered them from the nature, the location and the use of
the premises, or when they are registered at the Bureau de la publicité
foncière (Quebec's land publication office).

Distance therefore creates no lighter regime. It obliges you to reconstruct,
through third parties, the examination you would have carried out yourself. The
[first visit checklist](/en/articles/first-visit-property-checklist/) remains
your starting point: hand it to the person who will go on site.

## Delegating to the Inspector: What to Ask Them to Film

The broker has an ethical obligation to recommend a complete inspection by a
professional who carries liability insurance against faults, errors or
omissions, uses a recognized service agreement, works to a standard recognized
by the OACIQ (AIBQ, ATIB, APCHQ or BNQ 3009-500) and delivers a written report.
If the broker gives you a list, it must contain more than one name, and the
broker must not offer to pay the inspector in your place, because of the
appearance of a conflict of interest. For the selection criteria, see
[how to choose a home inspector](/en/articles/choosing-home-inspector-quebec/).

The OACIQ writes that it is important for the buyer to be present during the
inspection, so that the inspector can point out the items that deserve
attention. If you cannot be there, be on live video for the whole inspection,
not only for the final summary, and send ahead the written list of what you
want filmed.

| What to have filmed | Why |
|----|-----------|
| The electrical panel, opened | Protection, heat marks, connections |
| The bottom of the basement walls | Efflorescence, tide marks, cracks |
| The attic and the insulation | Possible vermiculite, ventilation, water stains |
| The slope of the lot, all the way around | Water coming back to the foundation |
| The data plate on the heating unit | Real year, energy source, tank |
| The street, on a weeknight | Noise, parking, traffic |

Ask the inspector to comment out loud while filming. Those observations have to
end up in the written report in any case.

## The Conditions to Write Into the Promesse d'achat

A promesse d'achat (promise to purchase) is not a sale. Your protections are
the conditions you write into it and their deadlines.

**The inspection period.** Clause 8.1 of the Promesse d'achat form sets the
deadline for fulfilling the inspection condition. The OACIQ reminds brokers to
allow a period long enough for the context, notably the availability of
inspectors. From a distance, add the time difference and the delay of a further
expert assessment if the inspector recommends one.

**A visit before signing.** Expressly provide for a visit of the premises
before the signing at the notaire's office, yours or your mandatary's.

**The documents.** Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble (the seller's
declarations on the property), certificat de localisation (certificate of
location), tax bills, and for a condo the minutes, the financial statements and
the contingency fund.

One warning: the courts have established that not every defect revealed by the
inspection automatically gives the right to cancel the promesse d'achat. The
factor has to be significant. While you are setting your deadlines, put numbers
on the closing with the
[closing costs calculator](/en/tools/closing-costs/).

## Procuration: What Your Mandatary Can Do in Your Place

Article 2130 of the Code civil du Québec is the basis: mandate is the contract
by which one person gives another the power to represent them in a juridical
act, and the writing that records that power is called the procuration (power
of attorney).

Three things matter. The scope: a useful procuration names the property, the
maximum price, and says whether the mandatary may borrow and grant a hypothec
in your name. The person: article 2138 requires the mandatary to act with
prudence and diligence, honesty and loyalty, and to avoid conflicts of
interest, but that obligation refunds you nothing if the choice was a bad one.
The revocation: article 2176 lets you revoke the mandate and demand the return
of the procuration so that the end of the mandate can be noted on it.

If you sign the procuration abroad, article 2823 is the one that counts: a
procuration under private signing made outside Quebec makes proof against all
persons when it is certified by a competent public officer who has verified the
identity and the signature of the mandator. If it is contested, article 2825
puts the burden of proof on the person invoking it. The officiating notaire
will tell you the form they accept: ask weeks before the closing, not the night
before.

## Signing at a Distance at the Notaire's Office

### The Exception, Not the Rule

The rule is the opposite of what many people believe. Article 46 of the Loi sur
le notariat (Quebec's Notaries Act) provides that a notarial act is closed by
the signature of the parties in the physical presence of the officiating
notaire (notary). The notaire may exceptionally authorize a party to sign at a
distance, on three conditions: that the circumstances require it, that the
party request it, and that it be done with respect for the rights and the
interests of the parties. The authorization may be revoked at any time. The
following paragraphs of article 46, on the place of closing and on a signature
received before another notaire, including abroad, are marked as not in force
in the official text.

In November 2023, the Chambre des notaires stated publicly that signing at a
distance remains permitted, but supervised, and that what is being called into
question is the systematic use of the virtual. So you have no right to it: it
is the notaire's professional judgment that decides, case by case.

Why so much rigour? Because article 2988 of the Code civil du Québec rests a
great deal on a single signature: the notaire attests, by that signature alone,
that they have verified the identity, the quality and the capacity of the
parties, and that the document reflects the will the parties expressed. The
full sequence is in
[the notaire's role, step by step](/en/articles/quebec-notary-process/).

### Identity Verification When You Are Abroad

The broker must verify the identity of all parties to the transaction, under
the Loi sur le courtage immobilier (Quebec's real estate brokerage act) and the
federal legislation on proceeds of crime. If a party refuses to provide the
required information and documents, the licence holder cannot act for that
party and must withdraw from the transaction.

When you are not physically present, the OACIQ guideline is precise: the broker
must obtain a scanned version of your government issued photo identification
and examine its characteristics and its security features. Seeing you hold up
your passport in a video call is not enough. A live video session then serves
to compare your face and your name to those on the document. The verification
may also be entrusted by written agreement to a mandatary, preferably a member
of a professional order.

## Buying Without Visiting: What No Clause Makes Up For

Let us be clear: a purchase made at a distance is not as safe as one where you
walked through the house yourself. A pre-purchase inspection is visual and
involves no demolition, and the report is an opinion on a given date. A latent
defect claim is litigated over years, and article 1726 first confronts you with
the diligence you should have exercised. No condition buys back the judgment
you were not able to form yourself.

What makes the purchase defensible is a sequence, not a clause.

| Order | Step |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-approval and identity verification settled before the offer |
| 2 | Documents read: Déclarations du vendeur, certificate, taxes |
| 3 | Live visit by an independent person, written list in hand |
| 4 | Promesse d'achat with lengthened deadlines and a visit before signing |
| 5 | Inspection filmed live, written report, further assessments done |
| 6 | Notaire chosen early, form of the procuration confirmed in writing |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I buy without ever setting foot in Quebec?**
Yes, it is legally possible. But someone you trust has to set foot there in
your place, and you have to be able to show that you acted as a prudent and
diligent buyer.

**Is my notaire obliged to accept a signature at a distance?**
No. Article 46 of the Loi sur le notariat makes it an exception that the
notaire may authorize, and the authorization may be revoked at any time. Settle
the question before you set the closing date.

**Will a procuration made in my country be accepted?**
Article 2823 of the Code civil du Québec recognizes it if a competent public
officer has certified the identity and the signature of the mandator. It is
still the officiating notaire who will confirm the exact form he or she
requires.

**Can I cancel if the inspection reveals a problem?**
Not automatically. The courts have held that not every defect revealed by the
inspection gives a right to cancellation: the factor has to be significant, and
the notice must be sent within the period provided.
