Three requests land on the same desk under three different names: renew,
transfer, refinance. Only one of those opens a new loan, triggers a penalty and
gets registered all over again at the registre foncier (Quebec's land register).
It is almost always the one the client calls by another name. Here is how to
know which one you actually want, and how to check whether it holds up.

## Renewing, transferring, refinancing: three different moves

**The renewal.** At the end of the term, with the same lender. The balance and
the amortization carry on, no new loan is created. The Financial Consumer Agency
of Canada (FCAC) states that a federally regulated lender has to give you a
renewal statement at least 21 days before the end of the term, and warns that if
you do nothing, the renewal can be automatic. You are probably not getting the
best rate that way.

**The transfer.** Same moment, different lender. Same balance, same remaining
amortization, no penalty since the term has run out. What changes is the cost of
opening the file with the new lender: according to FCAC, discharge, registration,
transfer or assignment fees. Since December 16, 2024, the Department of Finance
Canada has removed the obligation to apply the minimum qualifying rate to
low-ratio loans that change lenders at renewal. Three conditions: the
contractual amortization is kept, no equity take-out is allowed, and the balance
can only go up by $3,000 to cover the cost of the transaction.

**The refinancing.** This is where you break something. You reopen the loan
before maturity, or you increase the balance, or you change the amortization. It
is a new loan: full qualification, a new registration at the registre foncier,
and a penalty if the term is not finished.

| | Renewal | Transfer | Refinancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timing | At maturity | At maturity | Any time |
| New loan | No | No | Yes |
| Penalty | No | No | Yes, outside maturity |
| Higher balance | No | No, except $3,000 of fees | Yes, up to the ceiling |
| New application | Not required | Yes, without minimum qualifying rate | Yes, full |

## The 80% ceiling that frames every mortgage refinancing

**This ceiling is not a bank policy, it is a law.** Section 418 of the Bank Act
prohibits a bank from making a loan secured by residential property that,
together with claims of equal or prior rank, exceeds 80% of the value of the
property. The only way out, set in the same section: the excess portion has to
be guaranteed or insured by a government agency or by an approved private
insurer.

**And an ordinary refinancing cannot be insured.** Since November 30, 2016, the
Department of Finance Canada has imposed seven criteria on the insurance of
low-ratio loans. The first one targets the purpose of the loan: the purchase of
a property, or the subsequent renewal of such a loan. A refinancing is neither.
No insurance, therefore no way past the 80%.

The arithmetic is a surprise. Home recognized at $600,000, balance of $380,000:
the ceiling is $480,000. You are not pulling out $220,000 of equity, you are
pulling out $100,000 at best, before fees.

**If part of the amount takes the form of a line of credit.** Guideline B-20 from
the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions expects federally
regulated institutions to limit the non-amortizing component of a home equity
line of credit to an authorized loan to value ratio of no more than 65%.
Whatever goes beyond that has to be amortized.

**One exception has existed since 2025.** For insurance applications submitted on
or after January 15, 2025, an insured refinancing is allowed if the money is
used to build additional housing units: up to 90% of the value after the work,
value under $2,000,000, amortization of 30 years at most, four units maximum.
SCHL (CMHC) adds that the unit created cannot be rented for periods of less than
90 consecutive days.

## The penalty, the number that decides everything

**A closed loan breaks against a penalty.** FCAC is clear: an open loan can be
paid off without a prepayment penalty, a closed loan cannot. The penalty is
usually the higher of two amounts, either three months of interest on the
outstanding balance, or the interest rate differential.

**The differential is not calculated in a single way.** FCAC explains that the
lender compares two rates. For the first one: the posted rate at the time you
signed, or your current or discounted rate depending on the contract. For the
second: the current posted rate for a term of similar length, or that rate minus
the discount you were given at the start. Four combinations, four results.

The agency's worked example shows the size of the gap. Balance of $200,000, rate
of 6%, 36 months remaining, current posted rate of 4% for a 36 month term. Three
months of interest gives $3,000. The differential gives $12,000. It is the
$12,000 that is payable, plus possible administration fees.

Hold on to the mechanics: the differential only bites if the current rate for a
comparable term is lower than yours. If they are equal or higher, it collapses
toward zero and the three months of interest is what remains. For context, the
Banque du Canada (the Bank of Canada) held its target for the overnight rate at
2.25% on September 2, 2026.

Before you pay anything at all, measure the gap in your payment with the
[mortgage calculator](/en/tools/mortgage/). If the savings added up over the
months left in the term do not cover the penalty and the fees, the answer is
already in front of you.

## What you lose by breaking an insured loan

**The premium was paid once, and it does not come back.** The SCHL (CMHC) scale
for an owner-occupant loan runs from 0.60% of the loan amount up to a loan to
value ratio of 65%, to 4.00% between 90.01% and 95%. On a $400,000 loan taken
out at 95%, that is $16,000 added to the loan.

**The tax, on the other hand, left your account.** SCHL (CMHC) specifies that
certain provinces, including Quebec, apply a provincial sales tax on the
premium, and that this tax cannot be added to the loan amount. It is paid in
cash at closing. On that item, see
[the real cost of SCHL insurance](/en/articles/down-payment-myth-debunked/).

**Portability will not save you.** SCHL (CMHC) allows an existing insurance to be
carried over to a new property, with a premium credit of 100% within six months
of the initial closing, 50% within twelve months and 25% within twenty-four
months. But it serves a purchase: SCHL (CMHC) requires that the property be sold
and that the new loan finance the acquisition of the next one. A refinancing
triggers none of that. You break an insured loan, the premium stays paid, and
the loan that replaces it cannot be insured in turn.

## The fees to add up before you say yes

FCAC lists what you should have quoted before breaking a contract: the penalty,
the administration fees, the appraisal fees, the reinvestment fees, and the
discharge fees to strike the current charge and register a new one. If you had
received cash back at signing, a portion may also have to be repaid.

In Quebec, two of these items have a public price. Here is the registre foncier
tariff, in force since April 1, 2026.

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Registration of a right, including a hypothec | $145 per application |
| Striking, discharge or release | $165 per application |
| Additional registration to be struck | $101 more |
| Certified statement of rights | $17 |
| Consultation of a document | $1.50 |

The rest has no set tariff. The Quebec government points out that only notaries
and lawyers are qualified to prepare a discharge, that fees are payable to
publish it, and that striking a charge is in no way automatic: it has to be
requested. See
[the notary's role in Quebec](/en/articles/quebec-notary-process/). For appraisal
fees, no public tariff exists: ask for the amount in writing before you
authorize the order.

## The three situations where mortgage refinancing holds up

**High-rate debt that is choking the budget.** FCAC puts it without enthusiasm:
consolidating high-interest debts into a lower-rate product may save you money,
but it can stretch out the repayment period and cost more in interest over time.
The condition is easy to state and hard to keep: hold the amortization, do not
stretch it. Its report on home equity lines of credit shows why: 25% of
consumers only pay the interest or the minimum.

**Work that creates a housing unit.** This is the only case where the 80%
ceiling lifts, up to 90%, under the rules in force since January 15, 2025. The
money has to serve to build the unit or units, and the additional financing
cannot exceed the cost of the project.

**A down payment on an income property, at the right moment.** Pulling equity
out to acquire an asset that produces income holds up, on the condition that the
penalty is small or nil: close to the end of the term, or when the differential
collapses. The down payment thresholds are in
[building a real estate portfolio](/en/articles/build-real-estate-portfolio/).

And one case where it almost never holds up: refinancing over 25 years an
expense that will be dead in five.

## The file to prepare for a refinancing in Quebec

- **The up to date mortgage statement**: balance, end of term, rate and
  prepayment privileges.
- **The written calculation of the penalty**, with the method and the two rates
  used. Not a verbal estimate.
- **The certified statement of rights**, at $17, to see the charges still
  registered against the property. Every additional registration to be struck
  adds $101.
- **The tax accounts** paid up, and the certificat de localisation (location
  certificate) if your lender requires it.
- **Proof of income and the list of your debts**: this is a new application, not
  a renewal.
- **The name of your notary**, chosen early rather than late.

## Frequently asked questions

**I want to refinance during my term. Is that possible?**
Yes, on a closed loan, against a penalty. Ask for the amount in writing before
any other question. If the penalty is bigger than the savings over the remaining
months, waiting for maturity costs less than acting today.

**My neighbour says he refinanced with no penalty. How?**
Two likely explanations. He transferred his loan at maturity, which is not a
refinancing. Or he used a blend and extend option, which FCAC describes as a way
of extending the loan before the end of the term without a penalty, the lender
blending the old and the new rate.

**Do I have to pass the stress test again?**
For a refinancing, yes: it is a new uninsured loan. The December 16, 2024
exemption covers only low-ratio loans that change lenders at renewal, and it
excludes any equity take-out.

**Can the bank say no after the appraisal?**
Yes. The 80% ceiling is calculated on the value the lender recognizes, not on
the one you hope for and not on the municipal assessment. A gap on the low side
reduces the amount available, sometimes enough to make the whole operation
pointless.
