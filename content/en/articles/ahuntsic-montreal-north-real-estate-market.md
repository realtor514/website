In the third quarter of 2026, single-family homes in the Montreal borough of
Ahuntsic-Cartierville sold at a median price of $917,500, according to Centris.
Further east, along the same Rivière des Prairies, Montréal-Nord shows $524,875
over the last four quarters: no territory on the island has a lower single-family
median price, among those Centris publishes. Here is what the figures say for
these two boroughs, and what is moving around them.

## Ahuntsic-Cartierville and Montréal-Nord compared with the rest of Montreal

**Two price levels.** Over four quarters, the single-family median in
Ahuntsic-Cartierville is more than $100,000 above the island's. For condos,
both boroughs stay below the island, but Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles
is lower still, at $349,125. A change in median price is not the change in
value of a given house: from one quarter to the next, it is not the same
properties that change hands.

**Plexes lead sales.** They are the best-selling category on both sides: 71 of
the 185 residential sales in Ahuntsic-Cartierville, 36 of the 77 in
Montréal-Nord. In Ahuntsic-Cartierville, a plex sells for more than a
single-family home in the same quarter. In Montréal-Nord, it costs less than on
the island, but it needs 63 days, 11 more.

**Time on market.** Single-family homes in Ahuntsic-Cartierville found a buyer
in 31 days on average, 20 fewer than on the island. At that price, part of the
tax base falls in the $552,300 to $1,104,700 bracket, which the City of
Montreal taxes at 2% in 2026: the [transfer duties
calculator](/en/tools/welcome-tax/) puts a figure on the bill before you make
an offer.

## What changed in a year

For the metropolitan area, APCIQ counts 9,265 sales in the quarter, 12% fewer
than a year earlier, and places condos on the island at the border between a
balanced market and one that favours buyers. As of October 20, 2026, the Bank
of Canada's policy rate has been unchanged at 2.25% since the cut announced on
October 29, 2025; the next announcement is scheduled for October 28, 2026.

**Sales.** Montréal-Nord is down 31%, the second-largest drop Centris publishes
on the island, behind Mercier-Hochelaga-Maisonneuve (-34%).
Ahuntsic-Cartierville holds up better, at -3%, while the island loses 13%. Over
four quarters, Ahuntsic-Cartierville loses 5% of its sales and Montréal-Nord
13%, against 7% for the island.

**Supply.** Ahuntsic-Cartierville received 421 new listings (+24%), the largest
increase among the island territories for which Centris publishes the change.
Its active listings are up 40%, against 12% for the island. In Montréal-Nord,
the supply of condos nearly doubled, on a base of 51 listings.

## The Rivière des Prairies and flood zones

The City of Montreal lists Ahuntsic-Cartierville and Montréal-Nord among the
"areas concerned" by high water. In Montréal-Nord, it specifies, the river runs
along the entire length of the borough.

**A flood barrier on rue Crevier.** In Ahuntsic-Cartierville, a flood
protection structure is being built along rue Crevier, between boulevard Gouin
and the river. The sheet piles have been in place since fall 2025; the concrete
panels were to follow in spring 2026, according to the borough's page updated
May 22, 2026, which does not say whether they were installed. In spring 2026,
sandbag dikes were set up on rues Notre-Dame-des-Anges, Crevier, Cousineau,
Olivier, Leblanc, Du Ruisseau and Jasmin.

**The new maps are slow to come.** The first batch of new-generation maps from
the Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal (CMM) covers the Rivière des
Prairies. On July 30, 2026, the CMM asked the Ministère de l'Environnement to
reverse its decision to postpone their publication, planned for August 1, to
an undetermined date. As of October 20, 2026, I found no official source
announcing these maps in force. The government points out that, failing that,
the old-generation maps, obtained from the municipality, apply. For a riverside
address, the classification can therefore change after the purchase: see what
[buying in a flood zone in Quebec](/en/articles/buying-in-flood-zone-quebec/)
involves.

## What is moving in north Montreal

### The assessment roll and the tax bill

The 2026-2027-2028 roll measures the market as of July 1, 2024, and compares it
with July 1, 2021.

| Category | Ahuntsic-Cartierville | Montréal-Nord | City of Montreal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single-family homes | +10.3% | +18.2% | +12.1% |
| Condos | +14.1% | +15.5% | +9.5% |
| 2 to 5 dwellings | +8.9% | +16.4% | +9.8% |

Source: [2026-2027-2028 property assessment rolls](https://montreal.ca/sujets/roles-devaluation-fonciere), City of Montreal, pages 55, 58 and 65.

The City spreads the change over three years: each year, one third of the
difference is added to the old value. According to the 2026 budget, the
property tax burden on single-family homes rises on average by 5.5% in
Montréal-Nord and 3.6% in Ahuntsic-Cartierville, against 4.3% for the City.
Your bill depends on how much your property rose compared with the others: see
how [property taxes in Montreal and Laval](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/)
are calculated.

### The Louvain écoquartier, in Ahuntsic-Cartierville

On the Louvain Est site, a municipal lot of nearly 8 hectares north of rue de
Louvain Est, the project plans about 1,000 permanently affordable housing
units, at least half of them social and community housing, an elementary school
and shops. A first project of 323 units has been under construction since March
2026, with move-ins planned for 2028-2029. For neighbours, the practical
consequence is a construction site whose schedule runs until 2033.

### The Pie-IX BRT and the blue line, on the Montréal-Nord side

Since December 2023, the Pie-IX BRT (SRB) has run its full length between
boulevard Saint-Martin, in Laval, and avenue Pierre-De Coubertin, according to
ARTM. STM route 439 serves the Henri-Bourassa and Pie-IX intersection, which
the City places in Montréal-Nord. The BRT will connect to Vertières, the future
blue line station at the corner of Jean-Talon and Pie-IX; the STM plans to open
the extension in 2031. None of the official sources consulted measures the
effect of these lines on prices in the area.

### Rents and housing starts, according to CMHC

In CMHC's Montréal-Nord zone, the vacancy rate was 0.7% in October 2025 (0.2% a
year earlier), the lowest among the island zones where a total rate is
published; the City of Montreal was at 3.0%. For the Ahuntsic/Cartierville
zone, CMHC publishes no total rate for 2025. From January to September 2026, it
recorded 355 housing starts there, almost all apartments, against 288 a year
earlier; Montréal-Nord had 45, against 12. These zones do not necessarily match
Centris territories.

## Selling in Ahuntsic-Cartierville or Montréal-Nord

**Single-family homes in Ahuntsic-Cartierville** sell quickly, but 102 were
listed (+15%), and the quarterly median price did not move. A price backed by
recent sales of similar houses holds up better than a median: see how to
[audit real estate comparables](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).

**Condos in Ahuntsic-Cartierville** face more competition: 229 active listings
for 70 sales. Yet APCIQ's barometer classifies every one of its price ranges as
"Seller", at 7.2 months of inventory at most, while it places balance between 8
and 10 months. That calculation covers the last 12 months; the rise in supply
measured by Centris covers the quarter alone.

**In Montréal-Nord**, with 23 single-family sales, Centris publishes neither a
price nor a time on market: each comparable sale carries a lot of weight. For a
plex, gather the leases and the occupancy history before listing.

## Buying in north Montreal

**The choice.** Ahuntsic-Cartierville offers more listings in every category:
102 single-family homes, 229 condos and 137 plexes, against 46, 51 and 67 in
Montréal-Nord.

**Condos in Montréal-Nord.** Over 12 months, APCIQ classifies as "Seller" the
$270,000 to $460,000 range (6.2 months of inventory), where most of the supply
sits, and as "Buyer" the higher ranges: 10.1 months from $460,000 to $550,000,
21.3 months above that, on an average inventory of 8 and 5 properties. Above
$460,000, the choice is thin.

**Plexes.** A low vacancy rate in the zone does not guarantee the rent of a
specific unit: build your income figures from the actual leases and validate
them with your mortgage broker. Montréal-Nord prohibits commercial tourist
residences across its entire territory; Ahuntsic-Cartierville accepts them only
in the principal residence. Renting your principal residence to tourists
requires an annual permit from the City and is only possible from June 10 to
September 10.

**The address.** Near boulevard Gouin, check the flood zone boundary with the
borough, and ask for the latest tax bill.

## Frequently asked questions

**Have Ahuntsic homes gained value in a year?** The median price of the 44
single-family homes sold in the third quarter is stable (0%); over four
quarters, it is up 6%. These figures describe completed sales, not the value of
a specific house.

**Why does Centris not give the price of houses in Montréal-Nord?** With 23
sales in the quarter, Centris considers the transactions insufficient for a
reliable statistic. The last four quarters figure, $524,875, rests on 118
sales.

**Single-family assessments in Montréal-Nord rose 18%: are taxes up as
well?** Not by as much. The increase is spread over three years, and it is measured against
that of other properties. In 2026, the property tax burden on single-family
homes in the borough rises by 5.5% on average, according to the City's budget.
