A plex is a business. A condo is a home whose building management is delegated.
Comparing them means comparing two commitments, not two rankable investments.
Here are the rules that govern each.

## The down payment depends on the unit count and on whether you live there

For a CMHC-insured loan, the home must be intended for homeowner occupancy, by
the borrower or by a related person in the cases provided for. The minimum
equity is then 5% of the first $500,000 of lending value and 10% of the
remainder for a 1 or 2 unit property, but 10% as soon as the building has 3 or
4 units. Property value must stay below $1,500,000, the maximum amortization is
25 years, and gross and total debt service ratios are capped at 39% and 44%.

If you will not live in the building, the logic changes. CMHC Income Property
covers 2 to 4 unit rental properties that are non-owner occupied, with a
maximum loan-to-value of 80%, so a minimum equity requirement of 20%, and a
property value below $1,000,000.

The same building therefore does not require the same down payment depending on
whether you live in it. That is the first line of the budget, before any yield
calculation.

## What the lender does with the rent

CMHC also frames how rent enters your file. For an owner-occupied property that
is the subject of the loan application, the gross rental income approach may go
up to 100% in a 2 unit building, but only up to 50% of gross rental income, or
the net rental income approach, in a 3 or 4 unit building. For a non-owner
occupied property, the net rental income approach applies, meaning gross rents
less operating expenses. Under the gross approach, the percentage of rents is
added to gross annual income.

A condo brings no rent into the calculation, but it adds a charge: 50% of the
condominium fees go into both ratios. Two buyers with identical employment
income therefore do not have the same borrowing capacity. Test it from
[pre-approval](/en/articles/guide-hypotheque-premier-acheteur-quebec/) onward.

## Rents are not set freely

This is what yield spreadsheets forget most often. A residential lease is
renewed by operation of law (article 1941 of the Civil Code). To change it, the
landlord must send a notice at least three months and not more than six months
before the term of a lease of 12 months or more, or one to two months if the
lease is shorter (article 1942), stating in dollars the new rent or the
increase (article 1943). The tenant has one month to refuse (article 1945). If
he refuses, the landlord has one month to apply to the Tribunal administratif
du logement, failing which the lease is renewed on the previous conditions
(article 1947).

The tribunal does not decide by feel. The Regulation respecting the criteria
for the fixing of rent sets the components: a base percentage applicable to the
rent, the variation in municipal property and service taxes, the variation in
school taxes, the variation in fire and liability insurance premiums, a 5%
percentage applicable to capital expenditures made during the reference period,
and expenses arising from a new service. Each criterion is applied according to
the share attributable to the dwelling. The base percentage comes from an
average of three annual variations in Quebec's consumer price index, and the
TAL set it at 3.1% for notices of modification given on or after 1 January 2026.

Two rules complete the picture at purchase. Article 1896 requires the landlord
to give a new tenant, at signature, a notice stating the lowest rent paid in
the preceding 12 months; a false declaration or a knowing omission opens the
door to punitive damages. And article 1950 lets that new tenant have the rent
fixed within 10 days of the lease, or within two months if he never received
that notice.

A building with below-market rents therefore does not reset when the deed of
sale is signed. Article 1937 is explicit: the voluntary or forced alienation of
an immovable comprising a dwelling does not allow the new lessor to resiliate
the lease, which is continued, and the new lessor has the rights and
obligations resulting from it toward the tenant. Only a newly built immovable
escapes the rent-fixing remedy, for five years, and only if the lease says so
and states the maximum rent (article 1955).

## The real work of a landlord

Since 2024, a landlord notified of an assignment of lease may refuse to consent
for a reason other than a serious one, but the lease is then resiliated on the
assignment date stated in the tenant's notice, and the assigning tenant may not
require any consideration (articles 1978.1 to 1978.3). Where rent is late,
resiliation becomes available beyond three weeks (article 1971).

Repossessing a dwelling to live in it yourself or to house a close relative
requires a notice at least six months before the term of the lease (article
1960) and, if refused, an application to the tribunal within the month, where
you must show your genuine intent (article 1963). Article 1959.1 prohibits it
where the tenant or his spouse is 65 or older, has occupied the dwelling for at
least 10 years and has income at or below 125% of the maximum for low-rental
housing eligibility, subject to the exceptions provided. And eviction to
subdivide, substantially enlarge or change the use of a dwelling requires
payment of moving expenses plus an indemnity of one month's rent per year of
uninterrupted occupancy, with a minimum of three months and a maximum of 24
(article 1965).

These are deadlines, notices, proof and sometimes hearings. [Rental
management](/en/articles/rental-property-management/) is a job, not a revenue
line.

## Rental taxation is not a footnote

Revenu Québec requires rental income and expenses to be reported on form
TP-128. Capital expenses are not deductible in the year they are incurred: they
are spread through capital cost allowance. That deduction is optional and is
limited to the rental income left after all other expenses, so it cannot create
or increase a loss. Land is not depreciable, the class 1 rate is 4%, and you
are entitled to it only for the rented portion: nothing on the unit you live in.

The reverse arrives on resale. If you dispose of the property above its
purchase cost, you add the recapture of capital cost allowance to that year's
rental income, on top of the capital gain. And if you rent out the whole
residence you used to live in, the change of use triggers a deemed disposition
at fair market value.

## What a condo asks instead

No tenant, no TAL, no rental bookkeeping. In exchange, expenses you do not set:
the board fixes them each year after consulting the general meeting (article
1072). What you can demand before signing is set out in [the guide to condo
fees](/en/articles/condo-fees-investment-killer/), and what you are really
delegating in [condo or house](/en/articles/condo-vs-house-montreal/).

## The decision framework

Neither is a better investment than the other. They simply do not ask for the
same things.

- **Do you want an asset or an occupation?** A plex gives you landlord
  obligations, framed by the Civil Code and the TAL, all year long.
- **Does your down payment match the structure you are aiming at?** Five, ten
  or twenty per cent: the difference comes from the unit count and your
  occupancy.
- **Can you live with the current rents?** Those are the ones you are buying.
- **Have you read the leases and the rent roll?** That is the first step of an
  [income property purchase](/en/articles/income-property-beginners/).
