The choice of a banner comes up early in the conversation, and almost always in
the wrong place. People compare logos when the real question is narrower: in a
real estate file, what actually depends on the brand, and what would be
identical with any licensed broker?

The need to draw that line is documented. A Léger survey conducted for the OACIQ
from March 20 to 22, 2026, among 1,001 Quebecers shows that 62% of them rate
their knowledge of the main risks in residential real estate at 6 or less. The
average score on the knowledge test was 13.5 correct answers out of 20, and only
4% of respondents got everything right.

So here is the dividing line, item by item.

## What does not depend on any banner

### The licence and the body that issues it

In Quebec, a real estate broker holds a licence issued by the OACIQ, regardless
of the agency they are attached to. The same regulation applies to everyone: the
Regulation respecting brokerage requirements, professional conduct of brokers
and advertising.

Its section 1 requires the licence holder to disclose that status to every
person dealt with in a brokerage transaction, and to produce the licence on
request. Its section 5 requires the holder to verify the information provided to
the public and to be able at all times to prove its accuracy. Its section 4
prohibits offering services with any distinction, exclusion or preference based
on a ground of discrimination set out in section 10 of the Charter of human
rights and freedoms. Its section 6 prohibits inserting in a contract of
professional services a clause that would exclude the holder's professional
civil liability.

A banner can neither add to nor subtract from these obligations.

### The two funds behind the licence

**The Real Estate Indemnity Fund**, the FICI, covers victims of fraud,
fraudulent tactics or misappropriation of funds committed by a licence holder.
The maximum compensation is $100,000 per claim for an act committed on or after
May 10, 2018, and $35,000 for an earlier act. The claim must be filed within two
years of becoming aware of the alleged act.

What the FICI does not cover deserves to be said clearly: it does not address
good-faith errors or ordinary ethical faults, because fraud and deceit involve a
voluntary element. And if the person paid for the brokerage transaction does not
hold a valid licence, there is no recourse to the Fund.

**FARCIQ** is the mandatory professional liability insurer of brokers and
agencies. It compensates the loss resulting from an unintentional fault, error
or omission. Three conditions must be met: an unintentional fault, a financial
loss, and a causal link between the two. The OACIQ itself points out that
violating ethical rules does not automatically entitle anyone to compensation
from FARCIQ.

### The mandatory forms

The OACIQ has developed some fifty brokerage forms. In residential transactions,
brokers are required to use the mandatory forms: the brokerage contract to sell,
exclusive or non-exclusive, with the obligation to present both types to you;
Declarations by the seller of the immovable; the brokerage contract to purchase,
without which a broker can neither advise the buyer on the price to offer nor
negotiate on their behalf; the promise to purchase, the counter-proposal, the
annexes and the Amendments form.

That same regulation also governs the broker's conduct around these documents.
Its section 46.7 requires the broker, before having a mandatory form that they
have completed signed, to allow the parties to take cognizance of its terms and
conditions and to provide all explanations and answers to the questions asked.
As for the term, the OACIQ states that no brokerage contract developed by the
Organization may renew automatically, and the mandatory form provides that if no
end date and time are entered, the contract ends 30 days after it is made.

The text of the promise to purchase you will sign with a RE/MAX broker is
therefore exactly the same as at any other agency. That is by design.

## What a banner actually adds

RE/MAX Québec adds programs offered by participating brokers. They replace
nothing described above: they come on top. And each one has conditions that are
checked before, not after.

### Tranquilli-T: legal assistance, and its limits

[Tranquilli-T](/en/advantages/tranquilli-t/) is given free of charge to the
eligible client in the form of a protection certificate, once all the
conditions of the promise to purchase have been fulfilled, except the signing of
the deed of sale. The program has five components: delay and withdrawal, death,
legal assistance, small claims, and assistance toward an amicable settlement.

The legal information phone service is provided by lawyers who are members of
the Barreau du Québec and experienced in real estate transactions, Monday to
Friday from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m., except Quebec statutory holidays. The number and
length of calls are unlimited during the protection period.

What it does not do: lawyers are not admitted before the Small Claims Division,
so you have to represent yourself there. The three hours provided are meant to
guide you and give you document templates, not to argue your case. And the
program excludes new construction, land, abandoned properties, repossessions
except on the buyer's side, as well as partnerships, companies and developers.
The sale price must not exceed $5,000,000. RE/MAX Québec points out that the
content of its website is not a contract and that the terms, limitations and
exclusions are set out in the policy.

### Integri-T: the warranty, its cost and what it excludes

[Integri-T](/en/advantages/integri-t/) protects the buyer up to $50,000 against
hidden defects for 3 years following the deed of purchase, in partnership with
ACQ Résidentiel. When the buyer also joins the warranty, the seller's liability
is limited to the first $5,000.

Unlike Tranquilli-T, it is not free. RE/MAX Québec offers it to owner-sellers
for a base amount of $1,050, payable when the warranty certificate is issued,
at the notary's office or beforehand. The amount can be higher for a condo,
with $150 more per additional unit, and rates can change without notice: the
fee schedule is available from your broker or from customer service.

Several exclusions matter as much as the ceiling. Commercial buildings,
buildings less than 5 years old, mortgage repossessions and properties sold
without legal warranty are not eligible, and in a condo, only the private
portions are covered. Above all, items that an inspector detected and recorded
in the report are removed from coverage: the warranty applies to what was
hidden, not to what was written down. Finally, it must be put in place before
the notarial deed is signed, and reviewing the file takes about five business
days. After the notary, there is no catching up.

### Coproprie-T: competence, not a cheque

[Coproprie-T](/en/advantages/coproprie-t/) is a training program for brokers,
developed with Me Yves Joli-Coeur, founder of CondoLegal.com: workshops, video
capsules, podcasts and practical sheets on divided and undivided co-ownership
law.

Let's be precise, because the name can be misleading: it is neither insurance
nor a warranty. No money is paid to anyone. The benefit comes upstream, in the
checks carried out on the contingency fund, the minutes of meetings and the
declaration of co-ownership before you sign.

The fourth program, [My RE/MAX Discounts](/en/advantages/mes-rabais-remax/), is
not a protection at all: it is free access to discounts with partners, after
the transaction. Useful, but it belongs in the right box.

## Checking a licence takes thirty seconds

The OACIQ keeps a public register of licence holders, updated daily. You can
search by name or by licence number, and filter by agency, by region or by
authorized field of practice, residential or commercial.

Two details few people use. A checkbox lets you include in the search brokers
whose licence is suspended or revoked: tick it, otherwise a problematic file may
simply not show up. And the register displays disciplinary measures as well as
the training courses completed.

The register is available at [registre.oaciq.com](https://registre.oaciq.com/en/).
For a mortgage broker, the check is done elsewhere: that profession falls under
the Autorité des marchés financiers, as explained in the article on the
[difference between a broker, an agent and a
REALTOR](/en/articles/broker-agent-difference-quebec/).

## What no banner can do

A brand does not guarantee the quality of any particular broker's work. It does
not speed up a sale, it does not produce a better price, and it replaces neither
the analysis of comparables, nor the reading of a co-ownership file, nor the
preparation of a negotiation.

What it does, in this case, is more modest and more verifiable: it adds legal
coverage given free of charge to the eligible client during the critical period,
a financial warranty against hidden defects, paid for by the seller, if the file
is eligible and set up in time, and ongoing co-ownership training for the
brokers who take it. The rest depends on the person, and is judged by their
method.
