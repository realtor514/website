"List in spring" is the most repeated piece of advice given to sellers in Greater Montreal. The useful question is not whether it is true, but by how much: does the month you choose change your price, your timeline, or mostly the number of sellers standing beside you?

## Fourteen months of data, Montreal CMA

The Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers (APCIQ) publishes sales, listings, median prices and average days on market every month, drawn from the Centris system. Here is July 2025 through August 2026 for the Montreal census metropolitan area (CMA), each line taken from the publication for that month.

| - | - | - | - | - | - |
| July 2025 | 3,731 | 5,148 | 17,395 | 35 | 46 |
| August 2025 | 3,330 | 5,581 | 17,515 | 42 | 50 |
| September 2025 | 3,520 | 7,135 | 18,835 | 40 | 48 |
| October 2025 | 3,968 | 6,568 | 19,001 | 38 | 50 |
| November 2025 | 3,542 | 4,798 | 18,205 | 38 | 52 |
| December 2025 | 2,831 | 2,529 | 15,532 | 42 | 59 |
| January 2026 | 2,364 | 6,550 | 16,798 | 50 | 67 |
| February 2026 | 3,930 | 7,629 | 18,695 | 39 | 53 |
| March 2026 | 5,045 | 8,574 | 19,952 | 33 | 48 |
| April 2026 | 4,744 | 8,241 | 20,959 | 34 | 50 |

Three readings stand out.

**Volume swings enormously.** 2,364 sales in January 2026, 5,045 in March 2026: more than double, in the same region, seven weeks apart.

**Competition arrives with the buyers.** 2,529 new listings in December 2025, 8,574 in March 2026. Spring brings both sides at once, and what matters to you is the ratio between them in your segment, not the total volume.

**Time on market follows the season more clearly than price does.** Single-family homes sold in an average of 30 days in May 2026 and 50 days in January 2026; condominiums, in 47 and 67 days. The single-family median price runs from $615,000 in January 2026 to $652,250 in March 2026, then $650,000 in August 2026. A median describes the properties that sold that month, not the value of yours.

## What a monthly gap does not prove

APCIQ seasonally adjusts its own series. Its September 4, 2026 release on the Montreal CMA gives 2,853 sales in August, down 13% year over year, then adds: "on a seasonally adjusted basis, the decline was 2% compared with July 2026" (translated from the French release). A good share of any monthly gap is the calendar, not the market.

A pattern seen in 2025 and 2026 is not a promise for 2027. The cycle also follows rates: the Bank of Canada has held its policy rate at 2.25% since October 29, 2025, a level confirmed on September 2, 2026. The method for reading these series is set out in [reading the market data](/en/articles/marche-immobilier-montreal-analyse-donnees/).

## What weighs more than the month

**Price against comparables.** A property listed above what recent comparable sales support sits on the market in May the same way it does in November, and the buyer's question eventually changes: no longer whether it suits them, but why nobody else wanted it. For an order of magnitude, [the value estimate](/en/tools/home-estimate/) is a starting point, not an appraisal.

**Condition and preparation.** Buyers eliminate online before they visit. Dark photos and cluttered rooms cost showings, and the number of showings drives the timeline.

**Inventory in your own segment.** In August 2026, in the Montreal CMA, APCIQ counted 9,957 condominiums listed against 1,046 condominium sales, versus 7,753 single-family homes listed against 1,498 sales. Two different markets, same month, same region; the timelines followed, 62 days against 42. In thin segments, APCIQ sometimes publishes nothing at all: its tables carry the note "Insufficient number of transactions to produce a reliable statistic", as was the case for plexes in Laval in August 2026.

## The calendar constraints specific to Quebec

**June 30 and July 1.** The Tribunal administratif du logement puts it plainly: in Quebec, the majority of residential leases end on June 30. A new tenant's right to occupy begins on the first day of the lease, and the outgoing tenant has no grace period. Anything involving a rented dwelling, plexes included, is planned around that date.

**Notice periods.** According to the TAL, a tenant who does not renew a fixed term lease of 12 months or more gives notice 3 to 6 months before the lease ends, and 1 to 2 months for a shorter lease or a lease of indeterminate term. If the buyer wants to occupy the dwelling, that is a repossession: notice is given at least 6 months before the end of a lease longer than 6 months, 1 month for a lease of 6 months or less, and 6 months before the planned date for a lease of indeterminate term. Some long standing older tenants also have a protection. Check your case on tal.gouv.qc.ca and have it validated by a lawyer or your notary: these delays set the possession date, and therefore the month in which listing makes sense.

**The school calendar.** The Régime pédagogique provides at section 16 for a student calendar of "a maximum of 200 days, of which at least 180 must be devoted to educational services", spread from late August to late June. In my experience, families try to be settled before the school year starts, which concentrates their search in spring and early summer. That is professional observation, not a statistic.

**Bank of Canada announcements.** Eight dates a year, published in advance. For 2026: January 28, March 18, April 29, June 10, July 15, September 2, October 28 and December 9. They move buyers' borrowing capacity. I am not a mortgage broker: for the effect on a specific file, speak to an AMF certified mortgage broker or to your lender.

## What you control, and how long it takes

The listing month is not a free choice: it follows from your preparation time.

- **The certificate of location.** OACIQ provides that the seller give the broker, on signing the brokerage contract or shortly after, the certificate describing the current state of the immovable; work done since may require a new one. The Ordre des arpenteurs-géomètres du Québec suggested fee for 2026 is about $1,630 plus taxes, and surveyor lead times stretch in high season.
- **The Declarations by the Seller.** This form is mandatory. You supply the documents that support your answers, invoices, permits, reports, leases, and tell your broker about any new information.
- **Visible repairs and photos.** The detail is in [how to prepare your home for sale](/en/articles/preparer-maison-vente-montreal-laval/).

Once live, the first weeks do most of the work: that is when the listing appears in the automatic alerts of buyers whose criteria match. After that, it is seen only by those who arrive, one at a time. The full timeline is in [how long it takes to sell](/en/articles/how-long-to-sell-house-montreal/).

## The cost of waiting

Waiting three months is not free: mortgage interest, municipal and school taxes, insurance, condo fees, heating. Add them up before comparing them to the hoped for gain from a better month; the complete list is in [the real cost of selling](/en/articles/real-cost-selling-home-quebec/).

And if you are buying too, the market on the other side moves as well. Selling in March 2026 in the Montreal CMA meant facing 8,574 new listings; it also meant buying among those 8,574. The month that favours your sale does not necessarily favour the whole operation.

## When the month matters little, and when it matters

It matters little for a well prepared single-family home, priced to comparables, in a sector where inventory is low. It also matters little when the sale is not voluntary: an estate, a separation, a job transfer.

It matters more in three cases:

- **A condominium in a crowded building or sector.** When several comparable units are listed at the same time, the month you arrive decides how many direct competitors you face: see the 9,957 listings and 62 days of August 2026.
- **A cottage or a waterfront property.** A thin, seasonal buyer pool, and the kind of segment where APCIQ sometimes publishes no reliable median at all.
- **A plex sold on its rents.** The leases, their expiry dates and a possible repossession set the calendar before you do.

## What to keep

The month changes volume, competition and timeline, measurably. It changes your price far less than your price changes your timeline. When the time comes to decide, look at that month's APCIQ publication for your category and your sector, rather than a ranking of the seasons learned by heart.
