Noise is a defect buyers rarely check. You note the year of the roof, you ask
for the contingency fund, then you visit for twenty minutes on a Tuesday
afternoon with no idea what you will hear on a Thursday at 11 p.m. Yet noise
can be checked: it has a physical cause, a numerical building standard, a
clause in the declaration of co-ownership, and a precise recourse in the Civil
Code.

## Airborne noise and impact noise are not fixed the same way

**Airborne noise** is voices, the television, music, the dog. The sound travels
through the air, hits the partition between the two dwellings and passes partly
through it. It also goes around it: through the junctions of wall and floor,
through the adjoining walls, through an electrical outlet, through a
ventilation grille.

**Impact noise** is footsteps, a chair being dragged, a dropped object. The
energy goes straight into the structure, which vibrates and radiates the sound
further away. That is why adding drywall to the ceiling so often gives a
disappointing result: the source is not in the air, it is in the floor of the
unit above you.

The distinction decides who does the work. Airborne noise is corrected mostly
at your place, impact noise mostly at the other person's.

## The soundproofing standard between two dwellings in Quebec

Chapter I, Building, of the Code de construction du Québec is made up of the
National Building Code and the Quebec amendments. The edition based on the 2015
NBC came into force on January 8, 2022, the one based on the 2020 NBC on April
17, 2025. The Régie du bâtiment extended the transition period by one year
through regulations in force since September 10, 2026: projects that start
before October 17, 2027 may still follow the previous edition.

**The required values.** According to the FPInnovations technical sheet on
noise control, published in 2024 with input from the Régie du bâtiment and
based on the edition of the Code derived from the 2015 NBC, a separating
assembly and the adjoining construction must provide an ASTC (ITSA) of at
least 47 or an STC (ITS) of at least 50, with adjoining constructions that
comply with article 9.11.1.4 (articles 5.8.1.1 and 9.11.1.1).

**Two ways to comply.** The sound transmission class, the indice de
transmission du son (ITS), accounts only for direct paths, through the wall or
the floor separating two dwellings. The apparent sound transmission class, the
indice de transmission du son apparent (ITSA), introduced with the 2015 edition
of the NBC, accounts for direct and indirect paths, so for the junctions and
the adjoining surfaces. A wall that performs excellently in a laboratory can
give a mediocre result if the junctions are badly built.

**Nothing mandatory for footsteps.** The same sheet states it: for the moment,
the Code has no requirement for controlling the transmission of impact noise.
The Code only recommends that a bare floor, without carpet, reach an impact
insulation class (IIC) of at least 55. The Garantie de construction
résidentielle technical sheet on article 9.11 confirms it: ITS and ITSA
characterize the attenuation of sound in air.

**The Code applies to the work, not to the existing building.** A 1925 triplex
converted to divided co-ownership was never built to separate two households
acoustically, and converting it made it compliant with nothing. For a specific
building, the applicable edition depends on the date of the project: the Régie
du bâtiment or an acoustical consultant can confirm it.

## The floor covering imposed by the declaration of co-ownership

Since the Code does not protect against footsteps, this clause does the job
instead. It is also the one few buyers read before the offer.

Article 1053 of the Civil Code allows the constituting act to contain any
agreement concerning the private portions, including a penal clause. Article
1063 adds that each co-owner has free use and enjoyment of their private
portion, on the condition of observing the by-laws of the immovable and of not
impairing the rights of the other co-owners or the destination of the
immovable. Article 1057 makes those by-laws binding on a tenant or an occupant
as soon as a copy is given to them.

Many declarations require carpet over a minimum proportion of the floor, an
acoustic membrane under any rigid covering, or written authorization from the
board of directors. So a buyer who wants to pull up the carpet for hardwood
does three things before signing: reads the clause, asks the syndicate in
writing whether the project is allowed, and checks whether the floor in the
unit above was approved. The [renovation rules in a
condo](/en/articles/condo-renovation-rules-quebec/) apply to the same project.

The syndicate's leverage is serious. Under article 1080, where a co-owner's
refusal to comply with the declaration causes serious and irreparable injury,
the court may enjoin them to comply, and order the sale of their fraction if
they violate the injunction. These documents deserve the same attention as
[condo fees and the contingency fund](/en/articles/condo-fees-investment-killer/),
and they belong with the [co-ownership documents to obtain before
buying](/en/articles/condo-buyer-disclosure-quebec/).

## Outside noise: street, train, flight path

Three sources can be checked on a map, before the visit.

**The road.** The road noise management policy of the ministère des Transports
et de la Mobilité durable, published in March 2025, sets the intervention
threshold at 65 dBA Lden on the ground floor or the first floor for existing
road infrastructure, and recognizes 55 dBA Lden as an acceptable level. The
corrective approach covers only sensitive areas whose permit was issued before
March 1998 and that count at least 10 dwelling units. A noise barrier is
therefore neither automatic nor fast.

**The train.** A rail corridor is visible on any map, and freight also runs at
night. The policy above covers the road network, not the rail. Vibration is a
problem distinct from noise.

**The plane.** Transport Canada publishes a noise exposure forecast system, the
NEF contours, around airports. Any level above 25 will very likely bother
people, the department advises against new residential development beyond 30,
and above 35 complaints are numerous.

These markers are part of the portrait of a sector, as on the [page about
Laval](/en/real-estate-broker/laval/).

## Five noise tests to run during a condo visit

1. **Two visits, two moments.** One in the daytime, one in the evening or on a
   Friday night. A highway and a neighbourhood bar are not audible on a Tuesday
   at 2 p.m.
2. **Sixty seconds of silence.** In the bedroom, door closed, nobody talks and
   nobody moves. Listen with the window closed, then open.
3. **The impact test.** Ask your broker to arrange for someone to walk on the
   floor above, in shoes, at a normal pace. Nothing else reveals a rigid floor
   laid without a membrane.
4. **The weak points.** Put your ear near the outlets on the party wall, the
   recessed light fixtures, the ventilation grille and the plumbing stack:
   these are the holes airborne noise travels through.
5. **The corridor.** Open the front door, listen, close it again. Check the
   weatherstripping and whether the door is solid or hollow.

These five moves add to the [first visit
checklist](/en/articles/first-visit-property-checklist/) and take ten minutes.
A pre-purchase inspection does not measure acoustics: see [what an inspector's
mandate covers](/en/articles/choosing-home-inspector-quebec/).

## What can be fixed, and what it involves

| Source | What helps | What it involves |
|:-|:-|:-|
| Airborne, party wall | Independent framing, insulation, drywall on resilient channels | Lost space, outlets and baseboards to redo |
| Airborne, point leaks | Sealing the outlets, replacing the recessed fixtures | Light work, real effect |
| Airborne, windows | Higher performing glazing, ventilation reviewed | High cost, permit possible |
| Impact, floor above | Membrane under the neighbour's floor covering | Depends on the neighbour and the declaration |
| Impact, ceiling | Ceiling decoupled from the structure | Lost height, partial result |
| Corridor | Solid door, weatherstripping | Often a common portion |

No official source publishes the cost of this work: have it estimated by two
licensed contractors. In co-ownership, as soon as it touches a common portion
or the structure, article 1063 sends you back to the by-laws of the immovable.

## The recourse when the noise comes from a neighbour

Article 976 of the Civil Code is the basic rule: "Neighbours shall suffer the
normal neighbourhood annoyances that are not beyond the limit of tolerance they
owe each other, according to the nature or location of their land or local
usage." Noise does not open a recourse because it is bothersome, but because it
is abnormal in that context. Éducaloi specifies that the disturbance must be
continuous or repetitive, and serious. The steps are covered in [the guide to
neighbour disputes](/en/articles/neighbour-disputes-quebec/).

**The municipal route.** In Montreal, noise falls to the boroughs, and the
rules vary from one to another. Where it applies, the noise by-law, R.R.V.M.
c. B-3, declares a nuisance any noise that exceeds the set maximum or that is
specifically prohibited (article 2). In the Mercier-Hochelaga-Maisonneuve
codification, article 9 prohibits, when it is heard outside or in another
premises, noise from sound devices, shouting and uproar, and musical
instruments during the night period, at all times if they are percussion or
electric. Plateau-Mont-Royal adopted its own noise by-law on May 4, 2026, now
in force. The complaint goes through 311. In Laval, by-law L-12084 governs
nuisances: renovation work is tolerated on weekdays from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. and
on Saturdays from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.

**If you are a tenant.** Article 1854 requires the lessor to provide the lessee
with peaceable enjoyment of the dwelling throughout the term of the lease, and
article 1860 forbids a lessee from disturbing the normal enjoyment of the other
lessees. Article 1861 allows the lessee, after reporting the disturbance to the
common lessor and if it persists, to obtain a rent reduction or the resiliation
of the lease, and damages from the lessor unless the lessor proves they acted
with prudence and diligence. The Tribunal administratif du logement (Quebec's
rental board) wants evidence: a log with dates and times, a registered formal
notice with a deadline, witnesses.

**If you are a co-owner.** The Tribunal administratif du logement has nothing
to do with your file. You go through the syndicate, which enforces the
declaration, and if needed through the injunction of article 1080. Against a
neighbour your declaration does not bind, it is the ordinary court and article
976. That call belongs to your lawyer.
