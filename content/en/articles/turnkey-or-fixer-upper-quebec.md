Two listings, one budget. One is ready to move into. The other needs a kitchen, a
bathroom and new wiring, and it costs less. The arithmetic looks obvious right up
to the moment the lender explains when the money for the work actually comes out.
That moment settles the question for most households, long before taste, layout
or the colour of the cabinets.

## The asking price does not tell you who funds the work

A property that needs work sells for less because someone will have to pay for
that work. The only real question is who fronts the money, and when.

If you have the cash to run the job after closing without touching your down
payment, the comparison goes back to being a matter of taste. If you are counting
on the mortgage to finance the work, the mechanism below applies, and it changes
the order of the payments.

## Fixer upper or turnkey: the purchase plus improvements loan

The product exists. CMHC calls it SCHL Améliorations (CMHC Improvements): it lets
you buy an existing residential property and make improvements to it within the
same loan. Insured financing can reach 95% of the value after improvements for 1
or 2 owner occupied dwellings, and 90% for 3 or 4 dwellings.

**The lending value is not the one you are hoping for.** It corresponds to the
lesser of two calculations: the value after improvements, or the current value
plus the cost of the improvements. CMHC also requires documents to support it:
lists and cost estimates, plans in the case of major renovations, or a building
permit. So your quotes are not there only to build a budget: they set the amount
of the loan.

**Disbursement is the point that decides everything.** CMHC provides for two
modes. A single advance when the cost of the improvements represents 10% or less
of the value of the renovated property. Progress advances beyond that threshold,
with two possible treatments: under full service, CMHC authorizes up to four
consecutive advances at no cost; under basic service, the lender validates the
advances without prior CMHC authorization.

On a property whose value after the work would be $600,000, the 10% threshold
sits at $60,000. Below that amount, one single advance. Above it, a schedule of
advances validated one at a time.

Hold on to the consequence: an advance is paid out after validation, not before
the job. The OACIQ Promise to Purchase form provides, at clause 5.3, that the
buyer has the amount corresponding to the new hypothecary loan paid to the
officiating notary, in trust. CMHC, for its part, does not say who holds the funds
earmarked for the work between the signing and the validation: that is read in
your lender's commitment, and it is the question to ask in writing before you
promise anything. What does not change: the contractor gets paid before the
advance arrives. So you fund the job up front, with your own cash or with
temporary financing. CMHC adds that the lender must take into account the
borrower's ability to absorb cost overruns.

## What a lender does with a property in poor condition

Before work even comes up, one condition eliminates part of the listings. The
property must be suitable for and available for full time, year round occupancy,
and accessible year round. A house with no working heat or no usable bathroom does
not fit that description.

Then comes the lending value: the lesser of market value, purchase price or
construction cost. Paying above market creates no lending value at all. And nobody
can guarantee you the value the appraiser will retain after the work: I cannot,
and neither can your lender.

| Parameter | SCHL Améliorations |
|---|---|
| Maximum LTV, 1 or 2 dwellings | 95% of the value after improvements |
| Maximum lending value | under $1,500,000 |
| Maximum amortization | 25 years |
| Minimum credit score | 600 |
| GDS and TDS ratios | 39% and 44% |
| Qualifying rate | the higher of the contract rate plus 2% or 5.25% |
| Premium, LTV from 90.01% to 95% | 4.00% of the total loan |

These thresholds are not decorative. A premium of 4.00% is calculated on a total
loan that includes your work, and the ratios are calculated before the first wall
comes down. [Mortgage pre-approval](/en/articles/mortgage-preapproval-mistakes/)
does not shield you from that verification.

## Without that structure, the money comes from somewhere else

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada describes four ways of borrowing against
the equity in a home: a second mortgage, up to 80% of the value; a home equity
line of credit, up to 65%; a home equity loan paid out as a lump sum, up to 80%;
and a reverse mortgage, up to 55% of the appraised value, reserved for those aged
55 and over.

Read those percentages with your down payment in mind. Equity is the portion of
the home that belongs to you, meaning the value less the debts charged against it.
A buyer who has just put down 5% does not have 65% of equity to mobilize. That
route belongs to the owner who has been in place for years.

The FCAC also spells out what these arrangements cost: a home appraisal, a title
search, title insurance, legal fees, and sometimes a mortgage loan insurance
premium. It puts closing costs at between 1.5% and 4% of the price, inspection
fees included. Before choosing, run your numbers through the [borrowing capacity
calculator](/en/tools/affordability/): the 39% and 44% ratios apply to the total
loan, work included.

## What an inspection actually changes about your offer

Clause 8 of the Promise to Purchase form makes the promise conditional on the
buyer being able to have the immovable inspected within the stated period. If the
inspection reveals a factor liable to significantly reduce the value or the income
of the immovable or to increase the expenses it generates, the buyer must notify
the seller in writing and give him a copy of the report, no later than the fourth
day following the expiry of the period, before 8 p.m. The promise then becomes
null and void as soon as the notice is received. Without notice in proper form,
the buyer is deemed to have waived the condition.

Read that carefully: the clause cancels, it does not renegotiate. A price
reduction is negotiated by mutual agreement, and the seller has no obligation to
accept. That is why a budget backed by quotes is worth more than an inspection
report brandished as an argument.

The form also carries a warning in capitals: a promise to purchase without an
inspection clause is appropriate only in particular circumstances, for example
when the immovable is to be replaced or majorly renovated. That is exactly your
situation, and it is also the trap: without an inspection you have nothing to
price the quotes your lender will ask for. The [pre-purchase inspection
checklist](/en/articles/home-inspection-checklist-montreal/) shows what gets
looked at.

Financing has a calendar of its own. Clause 6.2 binds the buyer to provide the
seller, within the stated period, with a copy of a lender's commitment. Failing
that, clause 6.3 gives the seller five days to require a new application to a
lender he designates or to render the promise null, and his silence within that
period renders it null as well. A purchase plus improvements structure calls for
quotes, sometimes plans and a permit, before that commitment. Negotiate that
period accordingly.

## Permit, contractor and calendar: the time that gets added

The Régie du bâtiment du Québec develops the Building chapter of the Construction
Code, but the regulations applying to residential buildings fall to the
municipalities, and each one chooses the edition of the code it applies. The RBQ
therefore refers you to your municipality to learn the exact code that applies to
your building. The permit, for its part, is often one of the documents that
support the value after improvements in the lender's eyes.

On the execution side, the RBQ recommends always verifying the validity of the
licence in the licence holders' registry. Time exists on that side too: on 24
September 2026, the RBQ said it was processing regular licence applications
received on 24 August 2026. The full method is in [choosing a renovation
contractor](/en/articles/choosing-renovation-contractor-quebec/), along with the
legal hypothec of construction.

## Fixer upper or turnkey in Quebec: the honest comparison

| Item | Turnkey | Fixer upper with financing |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price | Higher | Lower |
| Documents required | The usual ones | Quotes, plans, often a permit |
| Cash going out | Down payment and closing costs | The same, plus the job fronted |
| Value retained | Lesser of price or value | Lesser of value after work or current value plus cost |
| Payment for the work | Not applicable | Single advance or progress advances |
| Calendar | Occupancy date set in the promise | Permit, contractor, advances |

The line that settles it is the third one. Turnkey asks a higher price but a
single cash effort, known in advance. The property that needs work asks less at
purchase and more later, at a moment you only partly control. A household that
ends its down payment at zero should not pick the second one: the job gets paid
for before it gets reimbursed.

Two qualifiers before you decide. A renovation is not automatically recovered in
the price: [your renovations will not add what you
think](/en/articles/renovations-dont-add-value/) explains the ceiling of the
sector. And if new construction is also on your list, [new build or resale in
Quebec](/en/articles/new-build-or-resale-quebec/) puts numbers on the GST and QST
that separate the two markets.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I add my work to the mortgage?**
Yes, through a product of the SCHL Améliorations type, up to 95% of the value
after improvements for an owner occupied property of 1 or 2 dwellings. The amount
retained stays the lesser of the value after improvements or the current value
plus the cost of the work.

**When is the money for the work paid out?**
After the advance is validated, by CMHC under full service or by the lender under
basic service. So plan on paying the contractor before being reimbursed, and have
the lender put in writing who holds the funds in the meantime.

**The house has no usable kitchen or bathroom. Is it financeable?**
Put the question to the lender before you promise. The property must be suitable
for and available for full time, year round occupancy. An uninhabitable building
is not treated like a simple renovation.

**Should I drop the inspection clause to win the house?**
The form itself warns that a promise without an inspection clause is appropriate
only in particular circumstances. And without an inspection, you do not have what
you need to get the quotes your lender will require.

maintenance costs in Quebec](/en/articles/home-maintenance-cost-quebec/).
