A retail space on the ground floor, two dwellings above, a posted income that
seems to hold up. Here the real risk is neither the roof nor the price: it sits
on paper, in leases, a soil history and an assessment roll that do not follow
residential rules. Here is the order of the checks, and where a specialist has
to take over.

General information, not legal, tax or environmental advice.

## Buying a commercial building: the broker's licence gets checked first

**A real estate broker licence does not authorize everything.** The OACIQ
(Quebec's real estate regulator) grants separate rights to practise. The
residential right, defined at article 3 of the Règlement sur les permis de
courtier et d'agence (the brokerage and agency licence regulation), covers a
mainly residential building of fewer than 5 dwellings, vacant land intended for
residential use, and a fraction of a residential building. The commercial right,
at article 4, covers the commercial building, vacant land intended for
commercial use, the enterprise whose assets are mainly immovables, and
commercial leasing. Fewer than 5 dwellings, residential; 5 and more, commercial.
For a mixed building, the main use decides.

A broker restricted to residential cannot handle a commercial transaction, and
the OACIQ specifies that this holds true even in a co-listing. Article 73 of the
Règlement sur les conditions d'exercice d'une opération de courtage (the
regulation on the conditions for carrying out a brokerage transaction) adds that
no broker may accept a transaction outside their field of competence without the
necessary assistance.

Another surprise: the OACIQ's mandatory forms are not required in commercial
matters, and neither is the Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble form (the
seller's declarations on the immovable), even though the OACIQ strongly
recommends it. See also [realtor, broker,
agent](/en/articles/broker-agent-difference-quebec/).

## The leases: they are what you are buying

**The sale does not put an end to the leases.** Article 1886 of the Code civil
is blunt: alienation of the leased property does not by itself terminate the
lease. You inherit the tenants, the rents and the lessor's obligations.

Article 1887 frames the only way out. If a fixed-term immovable lease has more
than 12 months left to run, the acquirer may terminate it at the end of those 12
months, with six months' written notice. But not if the lease was registered in
the registry office before the deed of sale: publication in the Registre foncier
(Quebec's land register) decides whether a tenant is there for the duration.
Article 1879 adds tacit renewal, meaning ten days of occupancy past the expiry
date without opposition from the lessor, which renews the lease for one year.

**The dwelling above the store is not always a dwelling.** Article 1892 sets
aside the rules on the lease of a dwelling, and therefore the oversight of the
Tribunal administratif du logement (Quebec's rental board), when more than one
third of the total area of the dwelling serves a purpose other than habitation.
To be settled with a lawyer or a notary.

### Audit the rent roll instead of believing it

The seller's rent roll is a sales document, not proof. Require, as a condition
of your promise to purchase:

- Every signed lease, its schedules and its amendments, not a summary.
- The remaining term, the renewal options and the indexation.
- The additional rent: taxes, insurance, maintenance of the common areas.
- Twelve months of bank statements, to compare what was collected with what was
  declared.
- The suretyships, the arrears, and written confirmation from each tenant.

## The soil: phase I, phase II and phase III characterization study

**This is the check that costs the most when you skip it.** The Guide de
caractérisation des terrains (the land characterization guide) published by the
ministère de l'Environnement describes three phases. Phase I is the review of
existing information and the history of the activities carried on the land; the
Guide notes that it may be required by a financial institution or by a new
owner. Phase II follows when phase I has flagged at least one area of risk:
sampling and laboratory analysis to confirm the presence or absence of
contamination. Phase III comes if phase II found some, to establish its nature,
its extent and the volumes to be managed.

The Loi sur la qualité de l'environnement (LQE, Quebec's environment quality
act) also imposes two obligations:

- **Cessation of activity, article 31.51.** Whoever permanently ceases an
  industrial or commercial activity of a category designated by regulation must
  send the minister and the owner a characterization study within 12 months.
  Where the regulatory limit values are exceeded, a rehabilitation plan follows
  within three months.
- **Change of use, article 31.53.** Whoever plans to change the use of such land
  must send a characterization study beforehand. Article 31.54 makes that change
  conditional on the minister approving a rehabilitation plan where the limits
  are exceeded.

A change of use includes any activity different from the previous one,
residential included: converting a garage into dwellings is not only an
architect's project.

The study has to be signed by a professional within the meaning of article
31.42, meaning either a professional under the Code des professions (Quebec's
professional code) or a person accredited under standard ISO 17024. Also check
the contamination notice of article 31.58, in the Registre foncier, and the
public list of contaminated land that every municipality keeps under article
31.68.

## Zoning, permitted use and municipal taxation

### The use has to be authorized, and attested

A building occupied by a business for 20 years does not prove the use is
compliant. In Laval, a certificat d'occupation (occupancy certificate) is
mandatory to validate that the use of a space complies, and a separate
certificate is required for each use when the building holds several spaces. It
stays valid as long as the use is not changed or interrupted for more than six
months, which matters when you are buying a space that has sat vacant for a
year. Budget $279 for the first 250 m2 of floor area of a new use, plus $196 per
additional block.

Ask for written confirmation of the authorized use and of any acquired rights
being invoked: a verbal answer at the counter cannot be pleaded. Detailed method
in [investing in Laval](/en/articles/laval-investment-city/).

### The non-residential rate changes the bill

Articles 244.29 and 244.30 of the Loi sur la fiscalité municipale (Quebec's act
respecting municipal taxation) allow several general property tax rates by
category of assessment unit, and one unit can belong to several categories: a
mixed building can carry two rates on the same account.

| Category, Laval 2026 | General property tax |
|---|---|
| Base rate | $0.4973 |
| Buildings of 6 dwellings or more | $0.6629 |
| Non-residential buildings under $1M | $2.0464 |
| Non-residential buildings over $1M | $2.2510 |

These rates apply per $100 of assessment: non-residential runs more than four
times the base rate. Add the droits de mutation (transfer duties), which you can
estimate with the [welcome tax calculator](/en/tools/welcome-tax/), and review
how the roll works in [property taxes in Montreal and
Laval](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/).

## Financing a commercial building: the lender looks at the building

**Here it is no longer your salary that decides.** CMHC's standard rental
housing product covers multi-unit buildings of at least five dwellings.
Non-residential space must not exceed 30% of the gross floor area or 30% of the
total lending value, and the loan attached to it is capped at 75% of its lending
value. Beyond that, you step outside the insured framework and negotiate
conventional commercial financing.

The maximum loan-to-value reaches 85% of lending value. The central criterion is
the debt coverage ratio, meaning the ability of the building's net income to pay
the debt service: at least 1.10 on purchase and 1.20 on refinancing for the
residential space of a 5 or 6 unit building, and 1.40 to 1.50 for
non-residential space depending on the length of the term. Amortization can
reach 40 years on an existing building, with a premium surcharge beyond 25
years.

For the contrast with a plex, see [the first income
property](/en/articles/income-property-beginners/) and [the second
building](/en/articles/build-real-estate-portfolio/).

## GST and QST: the rule flips compared with residential

**The seller is not always the one who collects.** Article 423 of the Loi sur la
taxe de vente du Québec (LTVQ, Quebec's sales tax act) provides that a supplier
making a taxable supply of an immovable by way of sale is not required to collect
the tax where the recipient is registered, except, for an individual, where it is
a residential complex or a cemetery plot. The federal side follows the same
logic: according to memorandum 19.1 of the Canada Revenue Agency, the registered
purchaser accounts for the tax in their own return, and others use form GST60, no
later than the last day of the month following the month in which the tax becomes
payable.

In practice, a registered buyer does not advance the taxes to the seller at the
notary: they self-assess, then claim their input tax credit. A buyer who is not
registered pays them for good. The OACIQ points out that determining the taxable
proportion is up to the seller, not the broker, with their accountant, a tax
specialist or Revenu Québec.

## The professionals to assemble before making an offer

- **A broker holding the commercial right to practise**, for the comparables and
  the promise to purchase.
- **A notary or a commercial law lawyer**, for the leases, the servitudes, the
  acquired rights and the titles.
- **An accountant or a tax specialist**, for the GST, the QST and the ownership
  structure.
- **A professional within the meaning of article 31.42 of the LQE**, for phase I
  and, if needed, phase II.
- **A chartered appraiser**, because value here is calculated on net income.
- **An engineer or an inspector used to commercial work**, for the structure, the
  systems and fire compliance.
- **A commercial mortgage broker**, to test the debt coverage ratio before
  setting a price.

My licence is residential: on a commercial building, my role stops where the
role of a colleague holding the commercial right to practise begins.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my residential broker represent me on a four-unit building with a
convenience store on the ground floor?**
It depends on the main use. The OACIQ confirms that a residential broker may act
in a building of fewer than 5 dwellings even if the area is split equally with
the business, but article 73 obliges them to obtain the assistance required.

**The seller says the land was never contaminated. Is that enough?**
No. The contamination notice of article 31.58 is registered in the Registre
foncier, and the municipality keeps a public list of contaminated land under
article 31.68.

**Can I end a commercial tenant's lease in order to occupy the space myself?**
Not freely. Article 1887 allows termination only if the fixed-term lease has
more than 12 months left to run, with six months' notice, and never if the lease
was registered in the registry office before the deed of sale.
