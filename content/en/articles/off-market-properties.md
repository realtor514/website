Off-market is the most misused phrase in Quebec real estate. It suggests a
back door and an insider price. The reality is more ordinary and, for a buyer,
more demanding. Here is what it actually is, what the law requires of the
broker, and what you give up by buying a property nobody else has seen.

## What an off-market property actually is

An off-market property is simply a property that is available but not
published on Centris or any other public platform. It is not a legal status
and not a category of sale. It is a marketing decision.

The Regulation respecting brokerage requirements, professional conduct of
brokers and advertising says so by implication. Under section 44, the broker
must list the immovable on the information dissemination service when the
party they represent wishes to use one, and must do so as marketing begins.
Section 111 adds that the broker cannot disseminate any information about an
immovable without express written authorization. In other words, off-market
exists because a seller chose not to publish, not because a parallel network
exists.

In practice these properties move through three channels only: a brokerage
contract without public dissemination, a sale by an owner who has no broker,
and word of mouth between brokers, only where the seller has chosen in writing
not to publish, or to delay the listing for an exceptional reason written into
the contract. There is no secret database.

## Why it is not a guaranteed discount

A seller who does not publish is not a seller in a hurry. Often the reason is
the exact opposite: no showings, no interior photos online, no questions from
the neighbours. The motivation is discretion, and discretion does not lower a
price.

The real problem is elsewhere, and it is serious: the price has never been
tested. In a public marketing campaign, the market answers. Here nobody
answers, and the only opinion available is the seller's. The regulation in
fact forbids a broker from giving an opinion on the value of an immovable
unless it is founded and substantiated in accordance with usage and the rules
of the art (section 76). A serious broker will therefore never tell you a
property is a bargain because it is off Centris.

## Exactly what the buyer loses

**Comparables.** The Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers
brings together more than 14,000 brokers and agencies, and publishes Quebec
residential market statistics from the brokers' provincial Centris database. A
property that never enters Centris feeds neither those statistics nor anyone's
comparables, including yours.

**Competition.** This cuts both ways, in fairness: nobody outbids you, but
nobody reveals the true ceiling either. You negotiate alone against an
expectation.

**A validated price.** The sale will eventually be public. Article 2938 of the
Civil Code of Quebec subjects the acquisition of an immovable real right to
publication, and registration in the land register follows. But it follows
after your signature, not before your decision.

**Often, the seller's declarations.** When no mandatory form applies, in
particular where the seller has no broker, section 82 of the regulation
requires the broker to recommend that the owner provide their declarations on
the immovable to anyone proposing to acquire it. Recommend, not impose. Ask
for them explicitly.

## The broker's duties do not disappear off Centris

The duty to verify and advise depends on no platform. The regulation is
precise and applies in full:

- **Section 83.** The broker must advise and inform with objectivity the party
  they represent and all other parties, on all facts relevant to the
  transaction and on its object, without exaggeration, concealment or false
  declaration.
- **Section 84.** The broker must take steps to discover, in accordance with
  usage and the rules of the art, factors that may adversely affect the
  parties or the object of the transaction.
- **Section 85.** The broker must inform of any known factor that may
  adversely affect the parties or the object of the transaction.
- **Section 5.** The broker must verify the information provided and always be
  able to demonstrate its accuracy.
- **Section 87.** Before showing an immovable that carries no exclusive
  brokerage contract, the broker must obtain the owner's consent.
- **Sections 15 and 16.** The broker protects the interests of the party
  represented while giving fair treatment to all parties, and must promptly
  inform any unrepresented party of that position.

A broker who opens an off-market door owes you exactly the same verifications
as on a public listing. Before choosing one, see [the questions to
ask](/en/articles/questions-hire-broker/).

## Disclosure of interest, the rule off-market makes critical

This is where the risk concentrates. When a property circulates in a narrow
network, the person bringing it to you may hold an interest in it.

Section 2 of the regulation requires a licence holder to avoid conflicts of
interest and, where a conflict cannot be avoided, to disclose it without delay
and in writing. The Real Estate Brokerage Act goes further: section 21
requires disclosure of any conflict of interest, and section 98 provides that
failure to do so draws at minimum a fine, on a scale running from $2,500 to
$62,500 per count, doubled on a repeat offence.

The disclosure notice itself is governed by section 18 of the regulation. A
broker who holds or proposes to acquire an interest in the immovable must,
before the transaction proposal is drafted or accepted, send a written notice
stating among other things the nature of that interest, by a means proving the
date and time of receipt. Failing that, the person owed the information may
withdraw without penalty from any offer or promise as long as the contract has
not been signed. The notice is entered in the register of disclosure notices.

The rest follows: a broker holding an interest in an immovable cannot
represent the buyer (section 20), a broker who wants to acquire an immovable
they are mandated to sell must end the brokerage contract before submitting a
proposal (section 22) and cannot claim any retribution (section 23). The Civil
Code locks the principle in at article 1709: a person charged with selling the
property of another cannot become its acquirer, even through an intermediary.

## Double representation, prohibited since 2022

If the broker showing you the property also holds the listing, the question is
settled. Section 29.1 of the Real Estate Brokerage Act requires that broker to
terminate your purchase brokerage contract as soon as they learn you intend to
bid on an immovable they hold for sale, to recommend another licence holder,
and they cannot claim any retribution for that termination.

Only two exceptions exist, at section 16.1 of the regulation: no other licence
holder has an establishment within 50 km, or the agency acts through two
different brokers. In those cases, section 17 requires the broker to inform
you in writing and obtain your written consent before you submit your offer.
On the roles themselves, [broker, agent,
REALTOR](/en/articles/broker-agent-difference-quebec/) sets out the rest.

## Positioning yourself without buying the myth

Sign a written purchase brokerage contract: since June 10, 2022 any verbal
contract is null, and you have three days to cancel at your discretion after receiving a
signed copy. Give precise criteria, because section 46 of the regulation
requires the broker to present only immovables that match them and to explain
the reasons behind the selection. Insist on the seller's declarations, have
the property inspected as you would anywhere else, and prepare the visit with
[the full checklist](/en/articles/first-visit-property-checklist/). For a
plex, add the leases and the financial statements: [the beginner's
guide](/en/articles/income-property-beginners/) sets out which ones.

Off-market is one more channel, never a complete strategy.
