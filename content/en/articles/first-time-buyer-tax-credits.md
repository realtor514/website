Almost every first-time buyer I meet misses at least one program. And in 2026
the list changed: Quebec now refunds part of the welcome tax, Ottawa refunds the
GST on a first new home, and the HBP grace period has been extended. Here is
each measure, with figures checked with the CRA, Revenu Québec and Quebec's
Ministère des Finances.

I am a real estate broker, not a tax advisor: this is general information. Have
your plan reviewed by an accountant or a financial planner.

## "First-time buyer" does not mean the same thing everywhere

The basic rule: you did not live, in the current year or the previous four
calendar years, in a home that you or your spouse owned. The details vary, and
one point surprises newcomers: for the FHSA, the HBP, the federal credit and
the GST rebate, a home you owned and lived in abroad counts too.

## The FHSA: the account to open first

The First Home Savings Account (CELIAPP in French) combines the best of the
RRSP and the TFSA. To open one, you must be a resident of Canada, be 18 to 71,
and be a first-time buyer.

- **Limits:** $8,000 a year, $40,000 lifetime. Unused room carries forward, up
  to $8,000, but it only starts building once the account is open: open it
  early, even with a small amount.
- **Deduction:** federal and Quebec, and you can carry it forward to a year
  when your income is higher.
- **Withdrawal:** tax-free and never repaid. You need a written agreement to
  buy with the purchase completed before October 1 of the following year, you
  must still be a first-time buyer and a resident of Canada, and you must plan
  to live in the home. Withdraw no later than 30 days after you buy.
- **If you don't buy:** the account lasts 15 years at most. The money can then
  go directly into an RRSP, tax-free and without using your RRSP room, or come
  out as a taxable withdrawal.

## The HBP: a loan to yourself

The Home Buyers' Plan (RAP in French) lets you withdraw up to $60,000 from your
RRSPs tax-free, per person: $120,000 for an eligible couple.

You repay over 15 years, normally starting the second year after the
withdrawal. For a first withdrawal made between 2022 and 2028, repayment starts
in the fifth year: the extension to 2026 through 2028 became law in June 2026,
and Quebec is matching it. A missed repayment is added to your taxable income.

Keep in mind: leave a contribution in the RRSP at least 90 days before
withdrawing it, or it may not be deductible. Stay a resident of Canada until you
buy, and buy before October 1 of the year after the withdrawal.

## FHSA and HBP together

You can use both for the same home. An eligible couple can put together up to
$200,000: $40,000 from the FHSA and $60,000 from the HBP each, plus the FHSA's
growth. My order: the FHSA first, because it is never repaid, then the HBP. See
also [the 20% down payment myth](/en/articles/down-payment-myth-debunked/).

## Two tax credits in the year you buy

**Federal (line 31270):** $10,000 at the 14% rate in 2026, so $1,400. For a
Quebec resident, the Quebec abatement brings its real value down to about
$1,169, according to Quebec's Ministère des Finances. The home must be in
Canada and become your principal residence within a year of buying.

**Quebec (line 396):** $10,000 at 14%, so $1,400, if you live in Quebec on
December 31 of the year you buy.

Both can be shared, without exceeding the maximum, and both are non-refundable:
they reduce the tax you owe, nothing more. The $1,500 figure still going around
dates from before the lowest tax rate was cut.

## New in 2026: Quebec refunds part of the welcome tax

There is still no exemption: you pay the municipality's bill. But the
refundable tax credit for access to homeownership, announced on April 17, 2026,
gives part of it back: 100% of the first $5,000 and 25% of the next $3,500, up
to $5,875.

It covers homes acquired since January 1, 2026, duplexes and triplexes
included, that will become your principal residence. It shrinks once the tax
base passes $750,000 and reaches zero at $1,000,000. You must have paid the
transfer duties and live in Quebec at the end of the year.

The Ministère des Finances example: a $616,000 house in Laval, $9,091 in
transfer duties, a $5,875 credit. Estimate your bill with the [welcome tax
calculator](/en/tools/welcome-tax/). An advance payment is planned from October
2026 when the credit exceeds $1,000, on request to Revenu Québec by December 1
of the year.

In Montreal, the Programme d'appui à l'acquisition résidentielle has not
accepted new applications since July 7, 2026: the city says this credit
replaces it. The City of Laval does not advertise any municipal program.

## New homes: GST and QST

**First-time home buyers' GST rebate:** in force, and administered by Revenu
Québec in Quebec. Up to 100% of the GST, maximum $50,000, on a new home of
$1,000,000 or less, then phased out up to $1.5 million. On a $600,000 new
condo, that is $30,000 back.

Conditions: 18 or older, Canadian citizen or permanent resident, first-time
buyer, rebate never received by you or your spouse, first occupant, and
principal residence. From a builder, the agreement must be signed on or after
March 20, 2025 and before 2031, and construction must start before 2031 and be
completed before 2036. It does not cover the QST.

**Standard rebates, for a new home you will live in:** GST, 36% of the tax,
maximum $6,300, in full up to $350,000 and nil at $450,000, and the CRA says it
can be combined with the rebate above. QST, 50% of the tax, maximum $9,975 up
to $200,000, nil at $300,000: a threshold rarely reached in Greater Montreal.

| HBP (RAP) | $60,000 from your RRSP, tax-free | Repay over 15 years | CRA |

| Quebec credit | $1,400 | Live in Quebec on December 31 | Revenu Québec, line 396 |

## For newcomers

The FHSA and the HBP require you to be a resident of Canada for tax purposes,
not a citizen. The first-time buyers' GST rebate requires citizenship or
permanent residence. Your RRSP room is based on the previous year's earned
income: in the year you arrive, you often have little. The FHSA gives you
$8,000 of room as soon as you open it, whatever your past income.
