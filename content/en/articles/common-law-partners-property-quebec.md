You have lived together for twelve years, the house is in his name, and you have
paid half of every mortgage payment. In Quebec, that situation gives you, on its
own, no right whatsoever over the property. The Civil Code does not treat living
together as a matrimonial regime, and the union parentale (parental union)
regime that came into force on June 30, 2025 covers only part of couples. Here
is who is protected, who is not, and what gets signed before the purchase.

I am a broker, not a notary: this is general information.

## The patrimoine familial does not apply to common law partners

**The patrimoine familial (family patrimony) is created by marriage, not by
living together.** Article 414 of the Civil Code is clear: marriage entails the
establishment of a family patrimony consisting of certain property of the
spouses, regardless of which of them holds a right of ownership in that
property. A civil union produces the same effect. Article 415 gives the list:
the residences of the family, the furniture that furnishes them, the vehicles
used for family travel, the rights accrued during the marriage under a
retirement plan, and the earnings recorded under the Régime de rentes du Québec
(the Quebec Pension Plan).

No length of cohabitation triggers the equivalent. Five years, fifteen years,
thirty years: the house does not enter any shared patrimony merely because you
live together. It is the most expensive misunderstanding I see in practice.

Tax law feeds the confusion. For the Canada Revenue Agency, you are common law
partners, conjoints de fait, after twelve months of continuous cohabitation, or
as soon as your partner is the parent of your child. That definition serves to
calculate your income tax. It creates no ownership right.

## Union parentale since June 30, 2025: who is covered, who is not

**The regime exists, but it does not cover every common law couple.** Article
521.20 provides that a union parentale is formed as soon as two conjoints de
fait become the father and mother or the parents of the same child. The law
applies it automatically only to couples whose common child is born or adopted
on or after June 30, 2025.

A couple whose children were all born before that date does not enter it on its
own. They will enter it at the birth or adoption of their next common child, or
they can join voluntarily by notarial act or by a contract signed before two
witnesses, as JuridiQC explains. A couple with no common child is never in a
union parentale, no matter how many years have passed.

The same article specifies that conjoints de fait are two persons who live
together and present themselves publicly as a couple, regardless of how long
they have lived together: there is no three year threshold, contrary to what
many people believe.

For the couples covered, article 521.29 creates a patrimoine d'union parentale
(parental union patrimony) made up of the residences of the family, the
household furniture and the family vehicles. When the union ends, its value,
less the debts contracted for the acquisition, improvement, maintenance or
preservation of that property, is divided equally (art. 521.34). Two limits:
retirement plans and the Régime de rentes du Québec are not in it, and article
521.30 excludes property devolved by succession or gift. A house received as an
inheritance stays outside the division. The partners can also withdraw from the
regime by mutual agreement, by notarial act en minute (art. 521.33).

| Situation of the couple | Home in a shared patrimony | Consent required to sell |
|---|---|---|
| Married or in a civil union | Yes, patrimoine familial (art. 414) | Yes (art. 404) |
| Union parentale, child since June 30, 2025 | Yes (art. 521.29) | Yes (art. 521.24) |
| Children born before June 30, 2025, no adhesion | No | No |
| Conjoints de fait with no common child | No | No |

## Common law partners and the home in Quebec: title decides almost everything

**Outside a union parentale, only what is entered in the Registre foncier
(Quebec's land register) counts.** If the property is in the name of one partner
alone, the other is not an owner, even after paying half the payments for ten
years. The owner can sell or mortgage without anyone's agreement: the family
residence protections of articles 401 to 407 cover married spouses and, through
the effect of article 521.24, partners in a union parentale. Nobody else.

One recourse remains, and it is not a right of ownership. Article 1493 requires
a person enriched at the expense of another to indemnify the person deprived, up
to the amount of their enrichment, where there is no justification. Article 1495
adds that the indemnity is owed only if the enrichment continues to exist on the
day of the demand, and the prescription period is three years (art. 2925). That
claim is won with documents: bank statements, invoices, dated transfers.

When both names are on title, you are undivided co-owners. The trap is article
1015: the shares of the undivided co-owners are presumed equal. A down payment
of 80% and 20% that is written down nowhere becomes a 50/50 split.

## The convention d'indivision: the clauses that count

The convention d'indivision (undivided co-ownership agreement) is the document
that replaces the presumptions of the law with your decisions. The clauses that
actually change the outcome:

- each person's real share, the one that follows the money invested, otherwise
  article 1015 decides in your place;
- the contribution to expenses, given that article 1019 provides only for a
  contribution to the common charges in proportion to the shares;
- the occupancy indemnity: article 1016 makes the person who has exclusive use
  of the property liable for an indemnity, so set it in advance;
- decisions. Article 1026 requires a majority in number and in shares for
  administration, and unanimity to sell or charge the property;
- the right of first refusal, which obliges the partner who wants to sell their
  share to offer it to the other first;
- the exit: who buys out whom, at what price, using which valuation method.

One technical point that costs dearly when it is neglected: article 1014
requires undivided co-ownership by agreement over an immovable to be published
to be set up against third persons. Unpublished, it holds between you, not
against the buyer of the other's share nor against their creditors.

## Separation: no one is bound to remain in undivided co-ownership

**Article 1030 is the least known and the most decisive rule.** No one is bound
to remain in undivided co-ownership, and partition may always be demanded unless
it has been postponed by agreement, by a testamentary disposition, by a judgment
or by operation of law. Either one of you can therefore force the exit. You can
postpone that right in writing, for a maximum of 30 years, renewable (art.
1013), and the court may stay the partition for at most two years to avoid a
loss (art. 1032).

If one of you keeps the house, that person will have to qualify alone with the
lender, on their own income and credit. The [affordability
calculator](/en/tools/affordability/) gives an order of magnitude before you
call your mortgage broker.

For couples in a union parentale, watch the calendar. The family residence
protections subsist only 120 days after cohabitation ends (art. 521.24), and
applications regarding the use of the residence must be presented to the court
within the same 120 day period (art. 521.27). Renunciation of the partition, for
its part, must be entered in the register of personal and movable real rights
within the year following the end of the union, failing which the renouncing
partner is deemed to have accepted (art. 521.41). The other steps are covered in
[the guide to separation and the
home](/en/articles/divorce-real-estate-guide/).

## Death: without a will, the house does not come to you

**This is where couples discover the gap.** Article 653 provides that the
succession devolves to the surviving spouse who was bound to the deceased by
marriage, by civil union or by union parentale. A common law partner who is not
in a union parentale is therefore not a legal heir: without a will, that person
inherits nothing, even after twenty years and even after paying the mortgage.

An important nuance, and it surprises people too. A partner in a union parentale
does inherit without a will, but not everything: where there are descendants,
that partner takes one third of the succession and the descendants the other two
thirds (art. 666). Failing descendants, the partner receives two thirds and the
privileged ascendants, meaning the deceased's parents, the other third (art.
672).

The practical consequence is the same in both cases: without a will, the
survivor can end up co-owning their own house with minor children or with
in-laws. A will, a life insurance policy large enough to buy out the heirs'
share and an up to date beneficiary designation settle the essentials. What
heirs go through is described in [inheriting property in
Quebec](/en/articles/inheritance-property-quebec/).

## What common law partners should sign before buying

| Document | What it settles |
|---|---|
| Shares stated in the deed of sale | Who holds what percentage |
| Published convention d'indivision | Expenses, first refusal, occupancy, exit |
| Acknowledgement of debt | Down payment lent by a partner or a parent |
| Cohabitation agreement | Residence, division of property, separation |
| A will for each of you | Who receives the deceased's share |
| Life insurance beneficiary designation | The cash to buy out that share |

All of this is prepared with [the
notary](/en/articles/quebec-notary-process/), before or at the same time as the
deed of sale, never after signing.

## Frequently asked questions

**After how many years of living together am I entitled to half the house?**
None. The Civil Code sets no duration threshold for conjoints de fait. Only
title, a written agreement or a union parentale create rights.

**We had a child in 2023 and another in 2026. Are we covered?** Yes. The union
parentale is formed at the birth of the common child occurring on or after June
30, 2025. Your notary will confirm the exact date.

**I pay half the mortgage on a house that is in his name. What happens to me at
the breakup?** Outside a union parentale, you have no right of ownership. Your
possible recourse is unjust enrichment, prescribed after three years, which
requires documented proof of your payments.

**Is mortgage life insurance enough to protect me if my partner dies?** No. It
reimburses the lender, it does not make you the owner. Without a will, the house
in the other's name passes to their heirs, free of the mortgage.
