The shed you buy at the hardware store fits in a box, and nobody at the cash
asks for your address. Your address is exactly what decides everything, though:
permitted floor area, height, distance from the lot line, permit or no permit.
Here is where the rule is written, city by city, and what happens when the
structure is already standing without paperwork.

## Construction accessoire: the term that decides everything

A shed, a storage building, a detached garage and a permanent detached carport
all belong to the same regulatory family. Montreal calls them dépendances,
Laval calls them constructions accessoires, accessory structures, and the
borough of Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles calls them bâtiments
complémentaires. The word changes, the logic does not: their use has to stay
accessory to the use of the main building.

The Info-permis sheet for Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles draws the
consequences. A main building must already exist on the lot, and the accessory
structure has to be detached from it: attaching it to the house turns it into an
extension, held to stricter standards. You may use it as a garage or as a
storage building, but not to live in, and a trailer, a temporary shelter or a
shipping container cannot stand in for one.

The central point fits in one sentence: the numbers do not come from the
province. They come from the zoning by-law of your city or your borough, and
they differ from one street to the next.

## When a shed permit becomes mandatory in Laval and in Montreal

**In Laval.** A permit is mandatory for any construction accessoire with a
ground footprint equal to or greater than 18 m², that is 193 sq ft. Below that
threshold, no permit is needed for a storage shed, but the rules of the Code de
l'urbanisme, the planning code, still apply. One exception: a permit can be
required whatever the size if the property is subject to a plan d'implantation
et d'intégration architecturale, a site planning and architectural integration
programme, which the Info-règlements tool indicates by address.

A garage and a permanent detached carport have no threshold at all: Laval writes
that a permit is mandatory for their construction, full stop. The published fee
is $92 for the first $20,000, plus $2.50 per $1,000 bracket or part of a
bracket, and the announced processing time is 30 days.

**In Montreal.** The common threshold is 15 m², and analysis fees run $9.80 per
$1,000 of work, with a minimum that varies. But the word "common" does all the
work here, because the borough decides:

| Borough | Permit required | Max height | Distance from lot lines | Minimum fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saint-Léonard | Always, one shed only | 3.05 m | 0.45 m | $133 |
| Ahuntsic-Cartierville | Over 15 m² | 4 m | 1.5 m | $167.40 |
| Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles | Over 15 m², or 25 m² in total | 4 m | 0.6 m | $259.90 |

Saint-Léonard adds a limit the other two do not have: a maximum length or width
of 4.6 m. In other words, the same 14 m² shed goes up without a permit in
Ahuntsic-Cartierville and requires a certificate of authorization in
Saint-Léonard.

## Shed or garage: what zoning limits on top of the permit

The permit is only the front door. Zoning then imposes constraints that plenty
of owners discover after delivery.

**Siting.** A bâtiment complémentaire cannot be installed in the front yard,
meaning the space between the front lot line and the main facade extended to
both side lot lines. On a corner lot or a through lot, the setbacks of the other
yards facing a public road apply as well.

**Distance from the lot line.** In Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles it
is 0.6 m from every line, unless the exterior cladding is masonry, in which case
the building may be sited right on the line. Even then, runoff, snow included,
has to fall on your own land.

**The relationship with the house.** This borough's zoning provides that the
area of each bâtiment complémentaire may not exceed 50% of the area of the main
building, and that its height may not exceed that of the main building, up to a
maximum of 4 m. Lot coverage, for its part, adds the house and every accessory
structure together, and varies by zone.

For Laval, no single value for setbacks, area or height is published for the
whole territory. The extracts of the Code de l'urbanisme on storage sheds and on
the detached carport can only be read zone by zone, inside Info-règlements:
"Recherche par lot ou par adresse" button, your address, then "Voir les
détails". Do not transpose a Montreal number onto a Laval lot. The reading
method itself is the one in [land value and
zoning](/en/articles/land-value-zoning-quebec/).

## The temporary carport and its dates

The winter shelter escapes the permit, but not the calendar, and the calendar is
not the same on both sides of the river.

| | Laval | Montreal |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized period | October 15 to April 30 | October 15 to April 15 |
| Permit | None | None |
| Outside the period | Structure removed | Metal structure fully dismantled |

In Laval, the shelter has to sit on the same lot as a residential building, and
be installed on a driveway, a parking area or a parking space, never on a
normally grassed surface. Minimum distances from the public road are published
as four diagrams, depending on whether there is a sidewalk, a curb, both or
neither.

In Montreal, the dates still vary by borough:
Mercier-Hochelaga-Maisonneuve allows October 1 to April 30. The tarp has to be
synthetic, white and translucent, on a dismountable metal frame, and a shelter
located less than 3 m from the sidewalk must carry a window of at least 0.5 m²
on each of its sides. Installation is prohibited in a heritage sector.

A non-compliant accessory structure is not just a neighbourhood annoyance. In
Laval, failing to comply with the Code de l'urbanisme lets inspectors demand
corrective work, and the published fine for a natural person is $400 for a
first offence and $800 for a second.

## What new construction changes on the assessment roll

The property assessment roll is filed every three years, but it is kept up to
date between filings. Montreal puts it this way: the rolls must reflect the
changes made to properties, and the update applies retroactively to the date the
work ended, without going back further than the fiscal year preceding the
current one. You then receive a notice of alteration setting out the reasons. It
is the Loi sur la fiscalité municipale, the municipal taxation act, that
requires this.

A nuance: among the work Montreal cites as liable to increase value are
renovating a kitchen or a bathroom, an extension and finishing a basement. The
shed is not on that list, and no official source puts a figure on the effect of
an accessory structure on a value entered on the roll. Remember the reference
date too: for the 2026-2027-2028 rolls, the market conditions used are those of
July 1, 2024. A notice that strikes you as off market can be contested, and
[contesting your municipal
assessment](/en/articles/contest-property-assessment-quebec/) follows a precise
deadline. Before the job starts, a [home value
estimate](/en/tools/home-estimate/) gives you the point of comparison.

## A missing shed permit: what the resale reveals

This is where the file resurfaces. The Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble
form, the seller's declarations on the property (DV 00001, version V24 06/2022),
asks question D2.11: since you acquired your property, has there been any
addition, replacement or modification, in particular of windows, a pool, a shed,
a sidewalk, a paved driveway, fences, a porch, a deck, hedges, retaining walls
or others?

The form adds an explicit note: if the answer is positive to questions D2.8,
D2.9, D2.10 and D2.11, a new certificat de localisation may be required if the
one provided does not reflect those changes. The 2026 suggested tariff guide of
the Ordre des arpenteurs-géomètres du Québec, Quebec's order of land surveyors,
sets the suggested fee for a certificate of location on an urban single family
home at $1,630.00, disbursements and taxes extra, each surveyor remaining free
to charge more or less.

Two other boxes follow. D2.12 asks whether you have ever received a notice of
non-compliance from an insurance company, the municipality or the government.
D2.13 asks whether an insurer has ever refused to insure the property in whole
or in part. A garage built without a permit has an unfortunate habit of
producing both.

Laval, for its part, already requires a recent certificate of location, prepared
by a land surveyor, among the documents for a detached garage permit
application. The document you file at the counter is the one that will serve at
resale.

## Insuring a shed or a detached garage

The guide Tout connaître sur l'assurance habitation, published by Info
Assurance, sets out the principle: if you are an owner, your home insurance
covers the house and its outbuildings, shed and detached garage included. The
amount required for the building corresponds to the cost of rebuilding it if it
were entirely destroyed, an amount that often differs from market value and from
the municipal assessment.

No official source, on the other hand, sets a general percentage of coverage for
outbuildings. That limit exists in many policies, but it belongs to the
contract, not to a public rule: read yours before you build. A non-compliant
structure that was poorly declared is exactly the kind of detail that makes [a
home hard to insure](/en/articles/hard-to-insure-home-quebec/).

## Frequently asked questions

**My shed is already there and I never had a permit. What now?**
First check whether the structure meets the current standards for your zone,
through Info-règlements in Laval or at your borough permit counter in Montreal.
If it complies, bringing it into order remains possible. If not, the inspector
can demand corrective work. Either way, question D2.11 is waiting for you at the
sale.

**Does a temporary carport count as an accessory structure?**
No, and that is why it follows its own dates. The
Rivière-des-Prairies-Pointe-aux-Trembles sheet does prohibit using a temporary
shelter as a year-round bâtiment complémentaire.

**Will the new garage push up my tax bill?**
The Loi sur la fiscalité municipale requires the roll to be kept up to date, and
the alteration is retroactive to the end of the work. No official source puts a
figure on the increase in advance: wait for the notice of alteration, then
compare.

**The seller says the shed is under the threshold. How do I check?**
Measure the ground footprint and compare it to your city's threshold, 18 m² in
Laval and 15 m² in most Montreal boroughs. Then ask for the certificate of
location: if it predates the construction, it shows nothing, and that is already
an answer.

the property rules](/en/articles/trees-property-rules-quebec/).
