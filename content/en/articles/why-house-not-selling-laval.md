A house that is not selling in Laval is diagnosed by comparing your listing with the sales in your neighbourhood: time on market, price range, showings. First benchmark: in the third quarter of 2026, a single-family home sold in Laval had spent 36 days on the market on average, compared with 51 on the Island of Montreal, according to Centris. Five causes are then checked.

## Why is my house not selling in Laval: the four-step diagnosis

In order, with public figures and the ones in your own file:

1. **Count your days since the brokerage contract was signed.** APCIQ (the Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers) defines average days on market as the average number of days between that signing and the date of sale. Compare yourself with your neighbourhood and your property category.
2. **Place your asking price within its range.** The APCIQ barometer classifies each price range as a seller's, balanced or buyer's market.
3. **Count your showings and read the feedback.** Each profile points to a different cause: see [the diagnosis of a property that is not selling in Quebec](/en/articles/home-not-selling-quebec/).
4. **Check the five causes:** price, presentation, access for showings, a known defect, buyers' financing.

**What the benchmark does not count.** For APCIQ, the date of sale is the date on which the promesse d'achat (promise to purchase) takes effect, once the conditions are lifted. A listing that was withdrawn or that expired has no such date: the average only describes the houses that sold.

## House not selling: the five causes, in order

**1. Price.** The OACIQ requires the broker to carry out a comparative analysis with similar properties recently sold in the same area: the price must reflect the reality of the market. Compare yourself with completed sales, not with houses that are listed. According to APCIQ, a broker's Centris search for a buyer can be filtered by price, by neighbourhood or by features: your price determines which searches your house appears in. Method: [auditing your neighbour's comparables](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).

**2. Presentation.** According to the OACIQ, the fiche descriptive (the listing sheet) is often the first information consulted, and what it contains must be accurate, verified and up to date. Reread the living area, the renovations, the inclusions and the season of the photos.

**3. Access for showings.** The exclusive contract allows the property to be shown at any reasonable time, with appointments set with the occupant (clause 8.8). A rented dwelling, narrow time slots, cancellations: count the refused requests as carefully as the showings that took place.

**4. A known defect.** The seller warrants the buyer against latent defects (article 1726 of the Code civil du Québec, the Civil Code of Quebec) and cannot exclude liability for a defect they knew of, or could not have been unaware of, and did not disclose (article 1733), except when a non-professional seller sells at the buyer's own risk. If an inspection reveals significant factors, the OACIQ requires the seller's broker to update the listing sheet and the Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble (the seller's declarations) through an Amendments form: the broker cannot go on distributing information that has become incomplete or inaccurate. Contractor quotes or an expert report in the file then put a number on the unknown. See [the seller's declaration](/en/articles/seller-declaration-form-quebec/).

**5. Buyers' financing.** For a CMHC Purchase insured loan on a one- or two-unit home occupied by the buyer, the minimum down payment is 5% on the first $500,000 and 10% on the rest, for a price under $1,500,000. At the quarter's median price for a single-family home in Laval, $630,000, that comes to $38,000 in cash; at $950,000, $70,000. Debt service ratios are calculated at the higher of the contract rate plus 2% and 5.25%. A mortgage broker can explain these rules for your price range.

## My house is not selling: what to do

Every move goes through the contract, explained in [the listing brokerage contract, clause by clause](/en/articles/listing-brokerage-contract-quebec/).

**Lower the price.** According to the OACIQ, during the contract, the broker must advise and inform you so that you can adjust the price as the market evolves. The broker then fills out the mandatory Amendments form, which you both sign; article 112 of the Règlement sur les conditions d'exercice d'une opération de courtage, sur la déontologie des courtiers et sur la publicité requires the advertised price to be the price in the contract. Point 2 of clause 7.1: the remuneration is owed if you refuse a promise to purchase that matches the contract's conditions of sale and is presented during its term. No public source measures the effect of a given price cut on the timeline.

**Rethink the marketing.** The OACIQ invites sellers to ask how the broker will promote the property: advertising, signs, open houses, the information-sharing service between agencies and brokers. The sharing of the remuneration should remain reasonable, so as not to reduce the interest of other brokers.

**Withdraw, then relist.** Unless otherwise stipulated in clause 11.1, you can terminate at any time, but you could owe the costs and expenses incurred, or compensation (clause 2.1). The remuneration remains owed if the house sells within 180 days after the contract ends or is terminated, to someone who showed interest in it during its term, unless a new exclusive contract has been signed in good faith with another agency or broker (point 3 of clause 7.1). And according to APCIQ, Centris gives brokers a property's sales history, "for information on how a property has evolved in the market over several years."

**Change brokers.** During an exclusive contract, another broker can neither set an appointment nor present a proposal without going through yours, unless authorized: that is article 99 of the same regulation. The contract ends on the date in its clause 2.1; without a date, 30 days after it was entered into.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my house not selling?**
Look among five causes, in this order: price compared with comparable sales, the listing sheet, access for showings, a known defect, buyers' financing. Compare your days with the average for your neighbourhood first.

**My house is not selling, what should I do?**
Do the diagnosis, then choose: adjust the price through the Amendments form, rethink the marketing, or withdraw the listing, knowing the possible costs and the 180-day clause. An inaccurate listing sheet gets corrected before anything else.

**Our house has not sold in Laval after 60 days: is that normal?**
For a single-family home, 60 days exceeds the average of every Laval neighbourhood where Centris publishes it for the third quarter of 2026, from 27 days in Duvernay to 48 in Sainte-Dorothée. For a condo, the Laval average is 57 days. These averages count only properties that sold.

**House not selling in Montreal: does it take longer than in Laval?**
On average, yes: in the third quarter of 2026, a single-family home sold on the Island of Montreal had spent 51 days on the market, compared with 36 in Laval; a condo, 67 days compared with 57. Compare yourself with your own territory.

**Divorce: what to do with a house that is not selling?**
For married spouses, selling the family residence is a decision made by both. Article 404 of the Code civil prohibits the spouse who owns a building with fewer than five dwellings from selling the family residence without the written consent of the other spouse, and the OACIQ then requires the spouse to intervene in the brokerage contract. Between co-owners, article 1030 provides that no one is bound to remain in indivision. Dividing the property is a matter for the notary, the lawyer or the mediator: see [separation, divorce and the home in Quebec](/en/articles/divorce-real-estate-guide/).
