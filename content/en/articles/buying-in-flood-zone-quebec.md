Quebec has replaced the old split between 0 to 20 year and 20 to 100 year zones
with a framework built on levels of flood exposure. The new regime is in force,
but the new mapping is being released piece by piece. Two generations of maps
therefore coexist, and an address can be classified differently six months from
now. Here is what the map decides, what it does not decide, and the checks to
make before you file a promise to purchase.

## Quebec's new flood zone framework, and the transition still under way

The cadre réglementaire modernisé pour la gestion des milieux hydriques
(Quebec's modernized regulatory framework for managing water environments) has
replaced the former transitional regime for flood zones, shorelines and littoral
areas since March 1, 2026. It rests on three regulations, one of which carries a
title that says everything about the period we are living through: Règlement
concernant les règles transitoires applicables en cas de changement à la
délimitation des zones inondables et des zones de mobilité. The government wrote
an entire regulation to govern what happens to projects already in motion when
the map changes under your feet.

**The transition is not finished.** The ministry calls this the largest revision
of flood zone mapping in Quebec's history. The work covers several hundred
municipalities in southern Quebec, led by the ministère de l'Environnement
(Quebec's environment ministry) under the INFO-Crue project and by eight
municipal communities judged particularly vulnerable. The government page
tracking the work in progress, updated on July 31, 2026, announces no firm date:
the planning could change, and new maps are added to the interactive map as they
come into force. The framework therefore applies to both generations of maps.
Verify which of the two governs the address you are looking at.

## What the map says, and what it does not

The old mapping knew only two categories. The new one adds more and changes the
criterion.

| | Old generation | New generation |
|---|---|---|
| Categories | High flow (0 to 20 years), low flow (20 to 100 years) | Low, moderate, high, very high |
| Criterion | Recurrence only | Frequency crossed with water depth |
| Additions | None | Ice jam zones, protected zones with residual risk, mobility zones |

Frequency is expressed as a probability over 25 years: frequent flooding above
70 per cent, moderately frequent from 20 to 70 per cent, infrequent from 7 to
20 per cent. Depth is read in three steps: 0 to 30 cm, more than 30 up to 60 cm,
and more than 60 cm above ground. A sector flooded frequently under more than
60 cm of water falls into very high intensity, as do ice jam zones, which are
classified there automatically. Protected zones with residual risk are the lands
behind a protective structure: protected, not risk free.

**What the map does not do.** It does not draw the precise boundary on your lot,
and the old generation data is provided for information only, with no legal
value. The government's interactive map, at zonesinondables.mrnf.gouv.qc.ca,
serves to locate a sector: the boundary that applies to a given lot is confirmed
with the municipality or the MRC, and nowhere else. It also says nothing about
the condition of the drain, the foundation or the backwater valve: that is the
job of [the pre-purchase
inspection](/en/articles/home-inspection-checklist-montreal/).

## Building, rebuilding and renovating in an exposed zone

An exposed zone is not a frozen zone. The government is explicit: repair and
maintenance of a building are permitted throughout water environments. Redoing a
roof, changing the windows, renovating the interior: none of that depends on the
exposure level.

Beyond that, the requirements rise. For major work, replacing a foundation for
example, the building must be adapted to withstand the flood risk, and the
opinion of a competent professional is required to confirm the result is safe.
Rebuilding a residential building is permitted only under certain conditions,
which vary with the environment. A destroyed house therefore does not
automatically get rebuilt as it was.

The permit, for its part, stays municipal. The municipality issues it, verifies
that the prescribed measures are respected, and can impose rules stricter than
the provincial minimum. A verbal answer at the counter is worth nothing in a
financing file: ask for it in writing.

## Insuring a property in a flood zone

This is where many files go off the rails. The Bureau d'assurance du Canada
(Insurance Bureau of Canada) defines flooding, in insurance terms, as the
overflow of a natural body of water that reaches your property. That protection
is not in the base policy. It exists as an endorsement, offered by some insurers
only, under certain conditions, after an assessment of the risk specific to your
situation.

**Three kinds of water, three treatments.** Sewer backflow, seepage through the
ground and the overflow of a watercourse are distinct risks, covered by distinct
protections. An owner who believes they are protected because they hold a water
damage endorsement can discover the opposite on the morning of the flood. Have
your insurance broker name all three, and get written confirmation before your
inspection period runs out.

When the endorsement is not offered, the building generally remains insurable
for the usual risks, fire first among them: it is the flood coverage that
disappears. What is left then is the financial assistance program of the
ministère de la Sécurité publique (Quebec's public security ministry), which
pays amounts set by expense category and according to the water height reached,
room by room. That is neither an insurance indemnity nor replacement value.

## The lender is looking at the same map you are

Let me be precise about what I can demonstrate. No Quebec law obliges a
financial institution to refuse a loan in an exposed zone, and no public
government source lets anyone say which lender refuses which sector: each
institution sets its own underwriting policy and changes it without notice.

What is verifiable is the mechanism. The mortgage creditor decides which
insurance protections it considers sufficient for its security. If the flood
endorsement is refused at the address, the question travels straight up into the
loan file. Hence the only safe method: get the insurer's written answer before
you lift your financing condition, never after. Test the full scenario with our
[mortgage calculator](/en/tools/mortgage/) and keep a margin for a higher
premium.

## The Déclarations du vendeur: the boxes that talk about water

The mandatory Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble form (the seller's
declarations on the immovable) is used when a brokerage contract on a mainly
residential immovable of fewer than 5 dwellings, excluding divided
co-ownership, is signed with an individual. Eight lines on it speak directly
about water.

| Line | What the seller declares |
|---|---|
| D2.9 | That the immovable is in a flood zone, as a public law restriction |
| D2.12 | A notice of non compliance received from an insurer, the municipality or the government |
| D2.13 | That an insurer has already refused to insure the immovable, in whole or in part |
| D2.14 | That an insurer has already refused to indemnify damage |
| D3.6 | That water periodically accumulates on the land |
| D4.1 and D4.2 | Water infiltration and leaks that caused damage |
| D9.12 and D9.13 | Wastewater drainage problems and the presence of a backwater valve |
| D14.1 | Damage suffered following a flood, ice storm, wind or fire |

Read D2.13 twice: a past refusal to insure is the most direct signal that an
insurer has already judged this address. The form also provides that a positive
answer to the questions on restrictions may make a new certificat de
localisation (location certificate) necessary.

The declarations are made to the seller's knowledge: someone who genuinely does
not know about a flood that happened before their time is not lying. Educaloi
recalls that a defect is latent only if it was not apparent, if it was unknown
to the buyer and if it existed at the time of purchase, and that defects a
prudent and diligent buyer could have detected are not warranted. And if the
sale is made without legal warranty, at the buyer's own risk, your recourse for
a latent defect disappears, unless you can show the seller knowingly hid the
truth. That mention must appear in the promise to purchase and in the deed of
sale.

## Buying in a flood zone: the checks, address by address

Seven steps, in this order, before the offer.

1. Locate the sector on the government's interactive map, then stop treating it
   as proof.
2. Write to the municipality giving the lot number: which mapping applies, which
   class, which flood level.
3. Ask whether the new mapping is expected for that territory.
4. Require an up to date certificat de localisation, and have your notary read
   it.
5. Get written confirmation from an insurer that the flood endorsement is
   available at that precise address.
6. Hand that answer to your mortgage creditor before lifting the financing
   condition.
7. Keep everything: the written file is what you are left with if the
   classification changes after the sale.

In Laval and on the North Shore, where the Mille-Îles and Prairies rivers shape
entire sectors, these seven steps often weigh more than negotiating the price.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I still renovate a house in a flood zone?**
Yes. Repair and maintenance of a building are permitted throughout water
environments. It is major work, such as replacing a foundation, that requires
adaptation to the risk and the opinion of a competent professional. The permit
is requested from the municipality, which can be stricter than the province.

**If the house is destroyed, can I rebuild it?**
Not automatically. Rebuilding a residential building is permitted only under
certain conditions, which vary with the environment. Ask the planning department
before you buy, not after.

**Does my home insurance cover flooding?**
Not in the base policy. The overflow of a watercourse is covered by an
endorsement, offered by some insurers only and after an assessment of the risk
at your address. Sewer backflow and seepage through the ground are separate
protections.

**Does the seller have to tell me the house has already flooded?**
The Déclarations du vendeur form asks them expressly, to their knowledge:
damage suffered following a flood (D14.1), water infiltration (D4.1), refusal to
insure by an insurer (D2.13). Require the form completed and dated, and keep it.

**Will the map change again?**
Probably. The work covers several hundred municipalities and no firm date has
been announced. A Quebec regulation is in fact devoted to the transitional rules
that apply when the boundary changes.
