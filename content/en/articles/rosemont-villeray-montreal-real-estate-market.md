In Montreal, Rosemont-La Petite-Patrie and Villeray-Saint-Michel-Parc-Extension recorded 209 and 126 residential sales in the third quarter of 2026, according to Centris: 23% and 26% fewer than in summer 2025, nearly double the decline for the island as a whole (-13%). Plexes carry a lot of weight in both boroughs, with nearly half of all sales in Villeray and nearly a third in Rosemont, and their median price holds at $950,000 and $880,000. Here are the figures, then what is changing in the area: the blue line, taxes and condo conversion.

## What changed in a year in Montreal

**Fewer sales, more supply.** Sales are down 23% and 26%, new listings are up 8% and 1%, and active listings have risen 22% and 10%. Yet no published median price is falling in either borough.

**The regional context.** In the Montreal census metropolitan area (CMA), APCIQ counts 9,265 sales in the third quarter (-12%) and 20,350 listed properties (+17%). For condos across the island, it places conditions on the border between a balanced market and one favouring buyers, which its pages for the Rosemont and Villeray sectors do not show. The Bank of Canada's policy rate remains at 2.25% as of October 20, 2026, and the next announcement is scheduled for October 28.

**The 2026 tax bill.** The 2026-2027-2028 assessment roll, in force since January 1, 2026, reflects the market as of July 1, 2024, against July 1, 2021 for the previous roll. Buildings with 2 to 5 dwellings gain 9.4% on average in Rosemont and 10.9% in Villeray, against 9.8% for the City of Montreal. According to the City's 2026 budget, their property tax burden rises on average by 4.5% and 4.1% this year, against 3.5% for the city as a whole. An average does not set the bill for any particular building. [Property taxes in Montreal and Laval](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/) walks through the calculation.

## What is moving in the area: the blue line and Pie-IX

**The blue line, scheduled for 2031.** The STM is extending the metro eastward from Saint-Michel station: five new stations, a tunnel of about 6 km, and a simultaneous opening of all five stations scheduled for 2031. Vertières station will stand at the corner of rue Jean-Talon and boulevard Pie-IX. Before the work, the STM has buildings inspected when they stand less than 75 m from the excavations or 30 m from the tunnel's centreline.

**The Pie-IX sector, still to be planned.** On a page updated October 31, 2025, the City of Montreal presents this 35-hectare sector around Pie-IX and Jean-Talon Est, straddling Villeray-Saint-Michel-Parc-Extension and Saint-Léonard, close to Rosemont-La Petite-Patrie. It aims for new housing and a central park there, and announces consultations in 2026. The page sets a 2050 horizon, with no construction schedule.

**New rentals, almost no condos.** From January to September 2026, CMHC recorded 519 housing starts in its Rosemont/La Petite-Patrie zone, against 62 a year earlier: 513 rental units and 6 condos. In the Villeray/St-Michel/Pc-Extension zone, it recorded 116 (140 in 2025), including 90 rental units, 26 co-operative units and no condos.

**No total vacancy rate published.** For October 2025, the most recent survey, CMHC provides no total rate for these two zones (for reasons of confidentiality or statistical reliability). For one-bedroom units, it publishes 0.5% in Rosemont/La Petite-Patrie and 1.2% in Villeray/St-Michel/Pc-Extension, rated "excellent", against 3.2% in the CMA.

## Condo conversion and tourist rentals: the Montreal rules

**Conversion to divided co-ownership.** In the Montreal agglomeration, section 51 of the Act respecting the Tribunal administratif du logement (Quebec's rental board) prohibits converting into divided co-ownership a building that has a dwelling, or has had one in the previous 10 years, unless an exemption is granted. The prohibition does not apply to a building whose dwellings are all occupied by undivided co-owners, but the Tribunal's authorization is still required.

In Rosemont, an exemption may be requested if the owner lives in one of the dwellings at the time of the request, within 6 months of the Tribunal's receipt of the notice of intention. Published fees: $3,119 for the review, then $1,274 per converted dwelling. An exemption is not an authorization: only the Tribunal can authorize the conversion.

In Villeray, the borough no longer allows conversion: its exemption by-law was repealed in 2019 (RCA19-14003). A rental building there can therefore no longer become a divided co-ownership, apart from the undivided co-ownership exception described above; [undivided co-ownership in Montreal](/en/articles/undivided-co-ownership-montreal/) explains that formula and how it is financed. Since April 8, 2026, by-law 01-283-125 also prohibits converting commercial spaces on certain commercial streets into dwellings there: for a mixed-use building, check at the permit counter before making an offer. Have your specific case confirmed by a notary or a lawyer.

**Tourist rentals.** In Rosemont, tourist accommodation outside a principal residence is prohibited, except for bed and breakfasts (gîtes), which are allowed only on rue Saint-Hubert between Bellechasse and Jean-Talon Est. In Villeray, commercial tourist rentals are allowed only on rue Saint-Hubert and rue Jean-Talon Est. Away from those streets, a plex's return is therefore calculated on ordinary leases.

## Selling or buying in Rosemont or Villeray

**Selling a plex.** Your building joins 140 plexes listed in Rosemont (+9%) or 160 in Villeray (+19%), with fewer sales than a year ago. The price is defended with recent sales of buildings with the same number of units, and with the leases: gather both before you list.

**Selling a condo.** In Rosemont, buyers have 37% more units to compare than a year earlier. The seller must give them the syndicate's attestation provided for in article 1068.1 of the Code civil du Québec: see [Bill 16 and condo documents](/en/articles/condo-buyer-disclosure-quebec/).

**Buying a plex.** The median price is not the price of any given triplex. Start from the rents written in the leases and from the borough's rules. [Income plexes in Montreal and Laval](/en/articles/income-property-beginners/) details the checks.

**Buying a condo.** The choice is growing in Rosemont, but APCIQ still classifies almost every price range there as favourable to sellers. No source puts a figure on the room for negotiation: it shows up in the comparable sales of the building you are considering.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are plex prices falling in Rosemont or Villeray?** Not their median prices in the third quarter of 2026, according to Centris: +1% in Rosemont and +7% in Villeray. What is falling is the number of sales, and a median price says nothing about the value of a specific address.

**Can a plex be turned into condos in Rosemont or Villeray?** In Rosemont, an exemption can be requested if the owner lives in the building; the Tribunal administratif du logement then decides. In Villeray, the borough has granted none since 2019. In both boroughs, a building occupied entirely by undivided co-owners escapes the prohibition, but not the Tribunal's authorization.

**Will the metro push prices up near Jean-Talon?** No official source measures that effect. What is published is the date: the STM plans to open the five stations in 2031.
