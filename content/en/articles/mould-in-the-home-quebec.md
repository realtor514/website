A black stain at the bottom of a basement wall almost always triggers the same
reaction: get the air tested, get a number, find out whether it is serious. That
reflex leads to a dead end, because in Quebec no number delivers that verdict.
Here is what actually decides, and three situations where the recourses and the
clocks are not the same: the owner in their own home, the buyer after the sale,
the tenant facing their landlord.

## Why no mould threshold exists in Quebec

The Institut national de santé publique du Québec (INSPQ) is categorical: there
is at present no exposure guideline value for mould inside non-industrial
buildings. This is not a regulatory lag. It is simply not possible, with current
knowledge, to quantify the relationship between exposure to airborne mould and
health effects.

Health Canada aims straight at the laboratory report: in the absence of guideline
values, the results of tests for the presence of mould in indoor air cannot be
used to assess risk. The department has even disowned its own 1995 values, which
do not represent a safe level.

**A Quebec standard does exist, and it contains no number.** The Bureau de
normalisation du Québec published standard BNQ 3009-600 on May 1, 2020. The INSPQ
presents it as the first Quebec standard on mould in dwellings: it covers
identifying the causes, assessing the contamination, carrying out the work and
quality control. It is a voluntary document, not a regulation, and it sets no
concentration threshold.

So be wary of a report announcing that a standard has been exceeded. What the
public bodies recommend is to correct the situation wherever there is excessive
humidity and persistent contamination, visible or hidden behind materials, whatever
the species.

## What feeds it, and the 48 hour window

Mould does not grow on a dry material. The INSPQ names the triggers: high relative
humidity, water damage, flooding, infiltration, sewer backups, and porous
materials soaked with water and exposed to humidity for more than 24 to 48 hours.
The SCHL (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation) sets the same limit after a
leak: you have less than 48 hours before mould starts to form. Water damage dried
within 24 hours therefore does not have the same sequel as water damage dried in
two weeks. The springtime origin of that damage is covered in our piece on
[spring melt and basement water infiltration](/en/articles/spring-melt-water-infiltration-montreal/).

**Indoor humidity level.** The SCHL and the INSPQ aim at the same range, 30 to
50%. The SCHL adds a winter detail few owners know: when it is -10 °C outside, the
indoor level should be 30%, and it can drop to 25% in extremely cold regions. A
hygrometer costs $10 to $60 according to the SCHL. Failing that, four signs are
enough: condensation on the windows, damp stains on walls and ceilings, mould in
the bathroom, a musty smell.

## The scale the SCHL uses instead of a number

Since concentration settles nothing, it is the surface affected that settles it.
The SCHL manual sorts situations into three tiers.

| Extent | SCHL definition | Who does the work |
|---|---|---|
| Small | One, two or three patches, each under 1 m² | The occupant, if in good health |
| Moderate | More than three patches under 1 m² each, total below 3 m² | A professional in many cases |
| Large | A single patch over 3 m², or numerous patches here and there | Contractors trained to do it |

Three details from the same source contradict widespread habits. Do not use
bleach: the SCHL recommends warm water and unscented dish soap, which lets you
smell whether the odour persists after cleaning. Do not paint over it: paint does
not destroy mould and does not stop it from growing. And cleaning alone stays
temporary, since the mould will come back if the source of humidity is not fixed.
Minimum equipment, even for a small surface: safety goggles, an N95 mask or
equivalent, rubber gloves.

## Mould in a home for sale: box D7.3

The mandatory Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble form, DV 00001 version V24 of
June 2022, devotes its section D7 to indoor air quality. Three questions there
touch the subject directly.

- **D7.1**: has regular and significant condensation ever formed in winter, on
  windows, skylights, walls or the ceiling?
- **D7.2**: has there ever been a foul odour, including from humidity?
- **D7.3**: have there ever been traces of mould, rot or fungus, and where?

Two other boxes catch the cause rather than the effect: D4.1 on water
infiltration, and D5.2 on problems that have affected the basement or the crawl
space, which lists rot. All of them bear on what has already happened, not on what
remains. The form is taken apart in our article on
[the seller's declaration in Quebec](/en/articles/seller-declaration-form-quebec/).

Hiding a known problem is not erased by a clause. Article 1733 of the Code civil
is explicit: a seller may not exclude or limit their liability unless they have
disclosed the defects of which they were aware or could not have been unaware,
except where the buyer buys at their own risk from a non-professional seller. If
an episode of humidity comes back to you before listing, the
[seller's guide](/en/seller/) lists the documents to gather.

## The buyer who finds mould after the sale

Here the clock counts for more than the stain. Article 1739 of the Code civil
requires the buyer to give written notice of the defect within a reasonable time
from its discovery, and it adds a sentence cut for mould: where the defect appears
gradually, the time begins to run on the day the buyer could have suspected its
seriousness and extent.

Éducaloi places that period between six months and a year depending on the
circumstances, longer if the defect shows up seasonally. Two steps condition what
follows: letting the seller observe the defect before having it repaired, except in
an emergency, and respecting the three year prescription of article 2925, which
runs from the discovery and not from the date of purchase. The four conditions of a
latent defect are detailed in our article on
[latent defects in Quebec](/en/articles/latent-defect-recourse-quebec/).

**The case of an odour with no stain.** An inspector who writes "musty smell in
the basement, source not located" is not giving you a diagnosis. He is giving you a
sign, and he is dating it. Have the source of humidity looked for while the promise
to purchase still protects you, then keep the report: it is what will establish
what you could see, and when.

## Tenant and lessor: article 1910 and what the TAL actually decides

Article 1910 of the Code civil obliges the lessor to deliver a dwelling in a good
state of habitability and to maintain it in that state throughout the lease. A
stipulation whereby the lessee acknowledges that the dwelling is in good condition
is without effect. Article 1913 covers serious cases: a dwelling is unfit for
habitation where its condition constitutes a serious risk to the health or safety
of the occupants.

The Tribunal administratif du logement (Quebec's rental board) classes mould among
situations of unsanitary conditions and immediately draws the nuance: a dwelling in
a poor state of habitability is not automatically unfit for habitation. The tenant
may ask for resiliation of the lease, an order compelling the lessor to carry out
work, a rent reduction or damages. Punitive damages are possible where the dwelling
became unfit through the lessor's negligence, for applications filed since
February 21, 2024. On obvious documentary evidence, inspection reports, evacuation
notices or photographs, the Tribunal summons the parties within ten working days.

Two decisions summarized by the Tribunal show why nobody can promise you a result.
In Lumande c. Raamco International Properties Canadian Ltd., of June 25, 2019, the
tenants obtained $10,060 in rent reduction and $8,000 in moral damages, with this
reasoning: proof of the contamination must rest on objective criteria, the
contaminant and the extent of the contamination have to be identified. In
Henrichon c. Hoang, of November 3, 2023, a microbiologist established that the
mould did not make the premises unfit, and the lease was resiliated at the tenants'
fault. Same family of facts, opposite results.

## What insurance covers, and what it excludes

Here is the most counterintuitive part. In its brochure on insurance and water
damage, the Bureau d'assurance du Canada lists as "not covered" damage caused by
humidity, condensation, rot, mould and gradual deterioration. Its example is
precise: a roof that has reached the end of its useful life and that you are slow
to replace, hence infiltration that causes humidity and mould on the ceiling and
the walls.

The word that decides is in the wording of the endorsements. Ground water and
sewer backup covers sudden and accidental infiltration of ground or surface water.
Above ground water covers sudden and accidental infiltration of rain or snow.
Sudden and accidental: slow degradation fits into neither one. Mould that follows a
covered loss can therefore be indemnified along with it, while the same mould born
of a leak tolerated for two winters is not.

The same organization recommends, for that matter, having a roof more than 20
years old inspected by a professional. Have your own situation confirmed by your
insurer, and ask for the answer in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will an air test prove my house is contaminated?**
No. Health Canada writes that in the absence of guideline values, those results
cannot be used to assess risk. A sample documents a file, it does not deliver a
verdict.

**I cleaned the stain and it came back. Why?**
Because cleaning does not fix the source. The SCHL says it plainly: as long as the
humidity problem persists, the mould comes back.

**The seller checked no on D7.3 and I found mould. Do I automatically have a
recourse?**
No, but you have a dated and signed document. What follows depends on proving that
the problem existed before the sale and that the seller knew of it or could not
have been unaware of it. Give written notice without delay and consult a lawyer
before undertaking any work.

**My landlord is doing nothing. Can I stop paying the rent?**
Not on your own initiative. The route provided runs through the Tribunal
administratif du logement, which can order work or grant a rent reduction. The rent
exemption of article 1915 applies to a dwelling that has become unfit and requires
notice to the lessor before leaving or within the 10 days that follow.
