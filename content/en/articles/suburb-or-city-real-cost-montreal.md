You will be told the suburbs cost less, and the gap between median prices seems
to confirm it. Then the tax bill arrives, along with the second car and the
minutes added to every trip. Here are the items to add up, each with its source
and its month, and the reason you will not find a winner at the end.

## Median prices by territory: the APCIQ numbers for August 2026

The Association professionnelle des courtiers immobiliers du Québec (Quebec's
professional association of real estate brokers) publishes monthly statistics
by territory and by category, drawn from the Centris system. Here are the
median prices for August 2026, released on September 4.

| Territory | Single-family | Condominium |
|---|---|---|
| Island of Montreal | $820,000 | $475,000 |
| Laval | $642,750 | $449,000 |
| Rive-Nord de Montréal (north shore) | $600,000 | $390,000 |

The raw gap on the single-family home therefore reaches $220,000 between the
island and the Rive-Nord. Note its limit right away: a median compares two
markets, not two houses. Building age, living area and lot size differ from one
territory to the next, with no adjustment whatsoever. The monthly reading of
these series is in
[my analysis of the Montreal and Laval market](/en/articles/marche-immobilier-montreal-analyse-donnees/).

## Municipal taxes: two rates, and the assessment roll trap

In Montreal, the residential rate is not a single number. The residual
category, meaning buildings of five dwellings or fewer, pays in 2026 a general
property tax of $0.4631 per $100 of assessment, a special water tax of $0.0831,
$0.0024 for roads and $0.0070 as a contribution to the ARTM. Added to that are
the debt of the former cities and the two borough taxes, services and
investments, which each council sets on its own. That is where the gap inside
the island comes from.

In Laval, a single base residential rate applies: $0.4973 per $100, plus
$0.0825 for the ARTM and $0.0111 for water infrastructure. But water service is
billed separately there, as a flat amount: $486 per dwelling in 2026, against
$337 in 2025.

On a roll value of $600,000:

| Territory | 2026 rate per $100 | Tax on value | Water service | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Montreal, Rivière-des-Prairies and Pointe-aux-Trembles | $0.7100 | $4,260 | included | $4,260 |
| Montreal, Le Plateau-Mont-Royal | $0.6511 | $3,907 | included | $3,907 |
| Laval | $0.5909 | $3,545 | $486 | $4,031 |

Laval therefore slots in between two Montreal boroughs, not below both. The
idea that the suburbs necessarily pay less tax does not survive the addition.

**The trap is the roll.** The rate applies to an entered value, never to the
price paid. Montreal taxes in 2026 on its 2026-2027-2028 roll, Laval on its
2025-2026-2027 roll, with the variation spread over three years. Two houses
sold on the same day at the same price therefore do not share the same tax
base. The mechanics of the bill are in
[property taxes in Montreal and Laval](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/).

## The cost of living in the Montreal suburbs is decided on the second car

This is the item that flips the calculation. Here are the monthly passes at the
regular fare from the Autorité régionale de transport métropolitain (the
metropolitan transit authority), in effect since July 1, 2026:

| Monthly pass | Territory | Per month | Per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tous modes A | Montreal agglomeration | $110 | $1,320 |
| Tous modes AB | adds Laval and Longueuil | $170 | $2,040 |
| Tous modes ABC | adds the outer rings | $206 | $2,472 |
| Tous modes ABCD | outside the ARTM territory | $281 | $3,372 |

Against that, the floor cost of one more car. The SAAQ charges $217.41 for the
annual renewal of a passenger vehicle of 3,000 kg or less: $140 in duties,
$66.01 of insurance, $5.95 of tax and $5.45 of charges. On the territory of the
Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal, add $30 of contribution to public
transit and $153.45 of registration tax, collected since January 1, 2024.
Total: $400.86, identical in Montreal, Laval and Terrebonne, since all three
sit inside the CMM. A reduction of up to $50 applies to the duties between
September 1, 2026 and August 31, 2027.

Add fuel. Statistics Canada measured self-serve regular unleaded gasoline at
184.9 cents a litre in Montreal in August 2026. At 15,000 km a year and
8 litres per 100 km, two assumptions to replace with your own, that comes to
1,200 litres, or roughly $2,219.

Registration and fuel therefore already exceed $2,600, before insurance,
maintenance, tires, parking and depreciation. I give no average total: an
average does not describe your vehicle. CAA-Québec's driving costs calculator
does it item by item.

The consequence reaches your financing too: the lender enters the car payment
in your total debt service ratio, exactly like the mortgage and the taxes. Test
both scenarios in the
[affordability calculator](/en/tools/affordability/) before choosing a sector.

## Commuting time: the data that contradicts the intuition

The 2021 Census gives the average commuting duration, by municipality of
residence and by mode.

| Residence | All modes | Car | Public transit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Montreal | 28.4 min | 24.8 min | 41.4 min |
| Laval | 26.6 min | 24.4 min | 47.0 min |
| Terrebonne | 29.4 min | 28.7 min | 56.5 min |
| Repentigny | 28.6 min | 27.8 min | 55.6 min |
| Mascouche | 27.9 min | 27.5 min | 60.2 min |

By car, the gap between Montreal and the northern ring runs around four minutes
per trip. On public transit it exceeds fifteen minutes, and reaches nineteen
minutes in Mascouche. The suburbs cost time above all to whoever gives up the
car.

More uncomfortable still: a Laval resident had a shorter average trip in 2021
than a Montreal resident, all modes combined. These averages count every
worker, not only those heading downtown. Measure your own: make the full trip,
on a Tuesday, there and back, at your hours.

## The space you actually get

What you are buying changes in nature as you cross the river. Among owners, in
2021:

| Municipality | Single detached house | Dwelling with 4 bedrooms or more |
|---|---|---|
| Montreal | 18.4% | 17.6% |
| Laval | 64.0% | 29.3% |
| Terrebonne | 77.0% | 34.8% |
| Repentigny | 75.0% | 35.2% |

No official source lets me give you a median price per square foot by
territory: that statistic is not published. The assessment roll, for its part,
gives the floor area, the year of construction and the lot size free of charge.
Space gets verified there, property by property, not in a gap between medians.

## Heating, water and transfer duties: the items people forget

**Heating follows the floor area.** Hydro-Québec's Rate D, in effect since
April 1, 2026, includes a network access charge of 46.154 cents a day, then
7.065 cents per kilowatt hour up to 40 kilowatt hours a day and 11.142 cents
beyond. A larger house crosses into the second tier more often, at 58% more per
kilowatt hour.

**Water is not billed the same way.** Montreal funds it through a rate on
value, Laval through a flat amount per dwelling. A low value property in Laval
therefore pays proportionally more for its water.

**Transfer duties hit once.** The base scale is provincial, but Montreal
applies its own upper brackets, which changes the closing bill before it even
changes the mortgage. Details in
[the welcome tax in Quebec](/en/articles/welcome-tax-quebec-guide/).

## Comparing the cost of living in the suburbs or in the city: the sheet to fill in

Eight lines are enough, in dollars per year, for the two addresses you are torn
between.

1. The mortgage payment, on the real price of each property you are targeting,
   not on the territory median.
2. Municipal taxes, at the 2026 rate of the borough or the city, applied to the
   value entered on the roll.
3. Water pricing, included in the rate in Montreal, extra in Laval.
4. Transit passes: one or two monthly subscriptions, in the right zone.
5. The cars: the exact number in each scenario, registration, insurance, fuel,
   maintenance and depreciation.
6. Parking, permit included where one exists.
7. Electricity, adjusted to the heated area.
8. Time: the minutes added per day, multiplied by your working days.

The result will tip on a single variable: does the household drop a car by
moving into the city, or add one by moving to the suburbs? A couple going from
two cars to one frees up more than the median price gap costs them. A couple
going from one to two travels the opposite road. So I do not decide for you:
the answer is on your sheet.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the suburbs really cheaper?**
On the purchase price, yes: a $600,000 median for a single-family home on the
Rive-Nord against $820,000 on the island in August 2026, according to the
APCIQ. On the annual cost, everything depends on the number of cars and on the
value entered on the roll.

**How much tax will I save by leaving Montreal for Laval?**
Perhaps nothing. On a value of $600,000, Laval comes to roughly $4,031 with the
water service in 2026, which is more than Le Plateau-Mont-Royal and less than
Rivière-des-Prairies and Pointe-aux-Trembles. Compare the precise borough, not
the city.

**Does a monthly pass replace a car?**
In cash terms, a Tous modes ABC subscription costs $2,472 a year, less than the
fuel and registration of a single car. In practice, it depends on your
schedule. Make the trip before you sell the vehicle.

**Should I choose based on future appreciation?**
No, and no public data allows anyone to promise a rate of appreciation by
territory. Choose on verifiable costs and on your holding horizon.
