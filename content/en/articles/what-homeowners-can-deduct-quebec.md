The house you live in gives you no deduction at all, and that is not an oversight
by the legislator. Everything changes as soon as a dwelling brings in rent or a
room is used to earn business income. Here is where the line runs for the 2026
taxation year, what capital cost allowance really costs you on resale, and the
two Quebec measures owners leave on the table.

## Principal residence: no deduction, and a filing all the same

**The rule has a reason, and it is written down.** Quebec's ministere des
Finances states it bluntly in its collection of tax expenditures. The capital
gain realized on the disposition of a principal residence is exempt under section
271 of the Taxation Act, and "in return, granting that exemption justifies not
allowing as a deduction from income the improvement expenses, mortgage interest,
property taxes and other costs incurred in respect of a principal residence". The
same document adds that capital losses on such a property give rise to no tax
relief. So you do not have an incomplete regime: you have the other half of a
trade. The IRS, for its part, does allow interest on a loan secured by a main or
second home, subject to a debt ceiling. The two systems cannot be cut up a la
carte.

**The exemption, though, is earned through a form.** Since the 2016 taxation
year, the Canada Revenue Agency grants the exemption only if the sale is reported
on Schedule 3, with the year of acquisition, the proceeds of disposition and the
description of the property. For dispositions from 2017 onward, form T2091(IND)
is added. A designation filed late costs the lesser of $8,000 or $100 per
complete month elapsed since the original due date.

A Quebec detail few sellers know: for a taxation year ending after October 2,
2016, a residence may be designated as a principal residence in Quebec only if it
was so designated federally. The federal step drives the Quebec treatment, not
the other way around.

## Income property: current expense or capital expense

On a rented building, almost everything eventually gets deducted. The real
question is when. The Canada Revenue Agency's T4036 guide sets out four tests,
and gives its own examples.

| The test | Current expense | Capital expense |
|---|---|---|
| Does the benefit last? | Repainting the siding | Replacing wood siding with vinyl |
| Restoration or improvement? | Reinforcing wooden steps | Replacing wooden steps with concrete |
| Is the item a separate asset? | Repairing the electrical system | Buying a refrigerator |
| Is the bill out of proportion? | Catching up on deferred maintenance | Spending out of proportion with the value |

Interest is deductible: the guide allows the deduction of interest on money
borrowed to buy or improve a rental property. Interest on money refinanced for
personal purposes, no. Costs incurred to borrow, appraisal, file processing and
mortgage loan insurance, are spread over five years, at 20% a year. All of it is
reported federally on form T776.

## Capital cost allowance on the building: the deduction that comes back on resale

A capital expense is not lost: it is added to the cost of the property and opens
the right to capital cost allowance. Most buildings acquired after 1987 fall into
class 1, at 4% a year. Furniture and appliances fall into class 8, at 20%. In the
year of acquisition, the half-year rule limits the deduction to half the net
additions to the class.

**It is optional.** The CRA is explicit: you may claim any amount, from zero up
to the maximum allowed.

**It cannot manufacture a loss.** Capital cost allowance can neither create nor
increase a rental loss. It reduces income, it does not deepen a deficit.

**It gets recaptured.** In the year you dispose of the property, you may have to
add an amount to your income as recaptured capital cost allowance, or deduct a
terminal loss. Capital cost allowance therefore shifts the tax in time: it does
not erase it. On a building whose value has risen, the recapture lands in the
same return as the capital gain, in the same year.

Before deciding, you need an order of magnitude for the building's current value.
The [home value estimator](/en/tools/home-estimate/) gives that starting point,
which replaces neither a chartered appraiser nor your accountant.

## The plex you live in: the proration, and the line not to cross

The T4036 guide settles the split with a worked example: if you rent 4 rooms of a
10 room house, you deduct 100% of the expenses that concern only the rented rooms,
plus 40%, that is 4 out of 10, of the expenses that affect the whole building,
such as taxes and insurance. Rented area in square metres is the other accepted
basis. Choose a method, write the calculation down, keep the same proration from
one year to the next.

The line not to cross fits in one sentence from the same guide: you may claim
capital cost allowance on the rented portion if doing so neither creates nor
increases a rental loss **and** if you do not designate the building as your
principal residence. The two advantages do not stack. On a duplex or triplex you
occupy, in Laval as in Montreal, the exemption is almost always worth more than
4% a year on the rented portion. The return calculation is in [the honest guide to
the income plex](/en/articles/income-property-beginners/).

## Home office: the self-employed deduct what the employee cannot

For a self-employed person, the CRA requires one of two conditions: that the
space be your principal place of business, or that you use it only to earn your
business income and use it on a regular and ongoing basis to meet clients.

The list of expenses is longer than an employee's: heating, electricity, home
insurance, cleaning materials, repairs, and a share of property taxes and
mortgage interest. The split is made on a reasonable basis, for instance the area
of the work space divided by that of the home. The limit: the deduction cannot
exceed the net income of the business before those expenses. The consolation: the
unused balance carries forward to the following year. All of it goes through form
T2125.

The trap is on the same CRA page: the capital gain and recapture rules will apply
if you claim capital cost allowance on the part of your residence used for the
business and then sell your residence. Claiming CCA on your office means taking a
fraction of your house out of the exemption. The conditions specific to an
employee are narrower and [the article on homeowner tax in
Quebec](/en/articles/homeowner-tax-deductions-quebec/) sets them out.

## Two Quebec measures owners forget

**The tax credit for home support for seniors.** It targets people aged 70 or
over, and its rate has risen by one point a year since 2022: 39% in 2025, 40% in
2026. The annual ceiling on eligible expenses is $19,500 for an independent
senior and $25,500 for a dependent senior. The reduction begins at 3% per dollar
of family income above $72,465 in 2026, then rises to 7% above $117,395. What
interests an owner: the recognized services include maintenance and supply in
respect of a dwelling or the land it sits on, but the cost of materials and other
goods acquired in connection with the service is excluded. The snow removal
contract, yes. The shovel, no. Reference: section 1029.8.61.5 of the Taxation Act.

**The grant for seniors to offset a municipal tax increase.** It has existed
since 2016, at sections 210.7 to 210.9 of the Act respecting municipal taxation.
You must be 65 at the end of the preceding year, have owned your residence for at
least fifteen consecutive years, and have family income of no more than $64,200
for a grant claimed for 2026. The substantive condition: the increase in the
value of your unit must exceed by 7.5% the average increase for all single
dwelling units entered on the roll. The amount is the lesser of $500 and the
portion of general property taxes attached to that excess.

This is not theoretical here. Laval's 2025-2026-2027 roll, in force since January
1, 2025 and based on market conditions as of July 1, 2023, shows a 37% increase
for the residential sector over the previous roll, and an average value of
$622,300 for a single-family home. When a roll moves that much, the gap between a
unit and the average stops being an abstraction. The tax bill is broken down in
[property taxes in Montreal and
Laval](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/), and the procedure to follow in
[contesting your municipal
assessment](/en/articles/contest-property-assessment-quebec/).

## The records to keep, and for how long

The CRA's general rule is six years from the end of the last taxation year to
which the records relate. One exception changes everything for an owner: records
concerning the acquisition and disposition of long term property are kept
indefinitely. A capital expense invoice from 2011 is still useful in 2038, because
it is what establishes the cost of the property, and therefore the gain.

The minimum to file per building: the notarial deed and the statement of
adjustments, the receipt for the transfer duties, every work invoice annotated
current or capital, the leases, the written calculation of your proration, and
the table of the undepreciated capital cost.

I am a broker, not a tax specialist. The rates and ceilings cited here apply to
2026 and change every year: have your situation validated by an accountant before
you file.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I claim capital cost allowance on my rental building?**
It is optional, and it is a cash flow decision, not a box to tick. Capital cost
allowance reduces the tax now and brings it back on resale as recapture. On a plex
you live in, it also makes you lose the principal residence designation on the
building.

**I am 72 and I pay for a snow removal contract. Does that count?**
Maintenance services provided in respect of the dwelling or the land are among the
recognized services under the home support credit, at 40% in 2026, subject to the
annual ceilings. The cost of materials and goods bought in connection with the
service is excluded.

**I rent out one dwelling in my duplex. Which form do I file?**
Federally, the T776, with the proration you calculated. Revenu Quebec has its own
rental income form: ask your accountant for it, because the two returns are not
completed the same way.
