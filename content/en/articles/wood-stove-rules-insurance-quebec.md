A wood fireplace is not a simple amenity. It is an appliance that gets declared
to the city, an emission rate you prove with a plate, a line in your insurance
file and two questions on the mandatory seller's form. Here is what the text
actually requires in Montreal, what Laval does differently, and the papers to
gather before you sell or buy.

## What the Montreal wood stove by-law actually prohibits

The Règlement concernant les appareils et les foyers permettant l'utilisation
d'un combustible solide (15-069), the by-law on solid fuel burning appliances and
fireplaces, was adopted at the August 17, 2015 sitting and applies to the 19
boroughs of the Ville de Montréal. The related cities of the agglomeration adopt
their own text.

**The prohibition on use.** Section 5 prohibits using or allowing the use of a
solid fuel burning appliance unless it has been recognized by a body listed in
schedule B, as part of a certification process, as having an emission rate equal
to or below 2.5 g/h of fine particulate. Schedule B names the American EPA and
the laboratory that attests to the CSA/B415.1-10 process. That section only took
effect on October 1, 2018, under section 11.

**Smog.** Section 6 prohibits use during a smog warning issued by Environment
Canada for a region that includes Montreal, in whole or in part. Even a certified
appliance.

**The outage.** Section 7 sets aside the prohibitions in sections 5 and 6 when a
power outage affecting the building has lasted more than 3 hours. It is the only
exception in the by-law.

**The fines.** Section 10 provides, for a natural person, $100 to $500 for a
first offence, $500 to $1,000 for a first repeat offence and $1,000 to $2,000 for
any additional repeat offence.

The by-law obliges no one to remove a non-compliant appliance: it prohibits using
it, and the city specifies that for such an appliance, "the prohibition is in
force at all times." No filter and no eco-log makes a non-compliant appliance
compliant. For a masonry fireplace, the only route to compliance is installing a
compliant insert. The reason is quantified: in the agglomeration's 2014 emissions
inventory, residential wood burning accounted for 701 tonnes of fine particulate,
or 39% of the total, behind transportation.

## Declaring the appliance: 120 days, even when you have just bought

Sections 3 and 4 require the owner to declare the appliance within 120 days of
its installation, and to declare any replacement or removal within 120 days of
that work.

The procedure the city publishes goes further than the text, and this is the
point buyers miss. It sets the deadline at "120 days (4 months) following the
installation, replacement or removal of the appliance, or within 120 days
following the purchase of the house if you are a new owner." It adds that an old
appliance you no longer use still has to be declared.

Installation, replacement and removal also require a borough renovation permit,
and the standards are not the same everywhere. In Ahuntsic-Cartierville, the city
writes that "only the installation of an EPA or CAN/CSA-B415.1 certified pellet
appliance is authorized," stricter than the 2.5 g/h rule, and publishes fees of
$9.80 per $1,000 of work, with a minimum of $167.40 in residential. Call your
borough permit counter before you buy the appliance.

## Laval: another by-law, other dates

By-law L-12792, in force since May 10, 2021 and amended by by-law L-13039 on
January 15, 2024, does not copy Montreal.

| Item | Montreal (15-069) | Laval (L-12792) |
|---|---|---|
| Installation | Certified, 2.5 g/h at most | Certified, 2.5 g/h at most (s. 5) |
| Use | Prohibited above 2.5 g/h since 2018 | Prohibited above 7.5 g/h on October 1, 2026 (s. 6) |
| Declaration | 120 days | 160 days (s. 3.1) |
| Smog | Prohibited (s. 6) | Prohibited (s. 7) |
| Outage over 3 h | Allowed (s. 7) | Allowed (s. 8) |
| Fines, natural person | $100 to $2,000 (s. 10) | $100 to $2,000 (s. 12) |

Two clarifications. An appliance installed before May 10, 2021 had to be declared
no later than October 18, 2021 (section 3), and the declaration must state the
make, the model, the installation date, the manufacturing date, the rate in g/h
and the certification (section 4.1). Laval also provides no deadline triggered by
the purchase of the property: if the declaration was never made, it is already
late, and the current owner is the one in default.

I have verified only those two texts. Both are adopted under sections 4, 19 and
85 of the Loi sur les compétences municipales, the municipal powers act: the rule
is municipal, not provincial. Terrebonne, Longueuil or Rosemère may have another
threshold, another date or no by-law at all. Never transpose the Montreal rule to
another address. The rest of the Laval checks are in [buying in
Laval](/en/articles/buying-in-laval-2025/).

## The certification plate decides, not the seller

Laval's Service de l'environnement et de l'écocitoyenneté publishes an
identification guide that settles the question in three eras.

| Year of manufacture | What Ville de Laval concludes |
|---|---|
| Before 1990 | No certification plate, so the appliance is not certified |
| 1990 to 2010 | Certification recommended to manufacturers, not mandatory |
| 2010 onward | Every appliance must be EPA or CSA certified, the plate exists |

Where to look for the plate, per the guide: on the side or at the back for a
boiler or a furnace, under the bottom grille for a prefabricated fireplace, at
the back for a freestanding stove, on the side or behind the metal face for an
insert. Photograph it.

Two useful visual clues during a showing. An open masonry fireplace, built brick
by brick, with no solid door and no plate, is not certified. And a
non-certified appliance often has a 7 or 8 inch flue, against 6 inches for a
certified one.

## What the insurer looks at, and what sweeping proves

The Bureau d'assurance du Canada, the insurance industry's national association,
places the wood stove among the seven factors that set your premium, and it is
blunt: "Improperly installed or maintained, wood stoves and fireplaces can cause
fires and carbon monoxide poisoning. Consult your insurer before buying or
renting a house with a wood stove. The insurer could require an inspection. Wood
heating appliances have an impact on the availability and affordability of home
insurance."

The risk is not theoretical. According to the ministère de la Sécurité publique,
chimney fires accounted on average for 5.4% of fires reported in Quebec in 2021
and in 2022, and chimney fires with no loss were more numerous from December to
April, averaging five a day.

**Sweeping frequency.** Montreal applies the Code national de prévention des
incendies, the national fire code, through its Règlement sur la prévention des
incendies (12-005). I was not able to open the text of the Code, so I will not
give you a numbered frequency presented as an obligation. What the Montreal fire
service does publish is clear: "Sweeping is required every two cords of wood
burned, or as soon as there is a creosote deposit of more than 3 mm on the
walls." The same service recommends notifying your insurer and installing a
carbon monoxide alarm.

**The installation standard.** For solid fuel burning appliances, the Régie du
bâtiment du Québec, Quebec's building board, refers to standard CAN/CSA-B365,
the installation code for solid fuel burning appliances and equipment. That is
the reference to name when you ask for the installer's invoice.

**The consequence of an omission.** Éducaloi points out that in the case of false
declarations, your insurer will not have to compensate you if it shows that you
deliberately lied or that it would have refused the risk, and that the indemnity
is reduced in other cases. Same consequence if you fail to report an aggravation
of the risk.

## What gets declared on form DV 00001

The mandatory Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble form, the seller's
declarations on the property, asks the questions for you. Question D12.1 asks for
the energy source of the main heating system, and wood is one of the choices.
Question D12.9 targets the auxiliary appliance in seven sub-questions: the type,
stove or fireplace; the energy source, including wood and pellets; the documents
attesting to its characteristics and its year of installation; the year of
installation; the frequency of use; problems that have already occurred; and
finally g) "Does it comply with the applicable regulations?"

Question D12.10 covers the chimney: the date of the last sweeping, the frequency
of sweepings, the documents attesting to its characteristics and its year of
installation, its compliance with the applicable regulations.

Three other questions matter here: D2.12, on a notice of non-compliance received
from an insurance company, the municipality or the government; D2.13, on a
refusal to insure the property; D2.15, on a contravention of environmental
quality regulations. A ticket issued under the municipal wood heating by-law
falls squarely within those three questions. The rest of the form is in [the
seller's declaration
form](/en/articles/seller-declaration-form-quebec/).

## Buying a home with a wood stove or fireplace

The file to demand from the seller comes down to five pieces: the photo of the
certification plate with the make, the model and the rate in g/h; proof of the
declaration to the city; the permit obtained for the installation; the last
sweeping invoice; and answers D12.9 and D12.10 on the form, obtained before you
draft the promise to purchase, not after.

If the appliance is non-compliant, price out the replacement and the permit
before the offer. Those amounts add to the price like the other outlays on the
notary's day, which the [closing costs
calculator](/en/tools/closing-costs/) helps you frame. The pre-purchase
inspection answers a different question: it describes the condition of the
appliance and the chimney, not their regulatory status. See [the pre-purchase
inspection checklist](/en/articles/home-inspection-checklist-montreal/).

## Frequently asked questions

**My fireplace is not certified. Do I have to remove it?**
No. In Montreal, the by-law prohibits use, not possession, and the city imposes
neither replacement nor removal. You do have to declare it, though, even if it is
no longer used. In Laval, the prohibition on using an appliance that is not
certified or that exceeds 7.5 g/h comes into force on October 1, 2026.

**I bought a house in Montreal this year, with a stove. Do I have anything to
do?**
Yes. The city asks for a declaration within 120 days following the purchase if
you are a new owner. The form can be completed online, by mail or by email with
the Service de l'environnement.

**Can I light a fire during a Hydro-Québec outage?**
Yes, after three hours. Section 7 of the Montreal by-law and section 8 of the
Laval by-law set aside the prohibitions when the outage affecting the building
has lasted more than 3 hours, certified or not.

**The seller has no sweeping invoice. Is that serious?**
It is a hole in the file, not a dead end. A sweeping done before signing costs
less than a conversation with an insurer after a loss. Ask for the proof of
declaration to the city at the same time.
