Redoing a flat roof in Montreal or Laval is no longer a colour choice. In both
cities, the list of permitted coverings is closed: white, vegetated or
reflective. In Montreal, the figure required changes from one borough to the
next; in Laval, it is the same everywhere. Here is what each city imposes, what
the Régie du bâtiment du Québec demands before soil goes onto an existing frame,
and what your insurer needs to know.

## Montreal: a closed list of coverings for flat roofs

The city's page "Rénover un toit plat ou à faible pente," updated on
February 2, 2026, starts with the threshold: a slope below 2:12, that is,
16.7%. Below that threshold, the only authorized coverings are a material that
is white, painted white or covered with a reflective coating or white gravel, a
material whose solar reflectance index reaches your borough's threshold, a
green roof, or a combination of those coverings.

So the by-law does not require you to plant anything. It requires you to reach
an index and accepts a green roof as an equivalent.

The permit, on the other hand, depends on the address. In Anjou, no permit is
needed to replace a flat roof, but the list of materials applies all the same.
In Plateau-Mont-Royal and in Côte-des-Neiges and Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, replacing
the membrane counts as maintenance, except for a heritage building. In
Plateau-Mont-Royal, the materials list covers only new buildings, extensions and
a change of volume or slope. In Pierrefonds-Roxboro and in Rivière-des-Prairies
and Pointe-aux-Trembles, redoing an asphalt roof with an identical or similar
material requires no permit. In Saint-Léonard, for a single family home, the
authorized materials are those permitted by the building code.

Elsewhere, a permit is required to replace the covering, and the manufacturer's
technical sheet stating the index is one of the documents demanded. Review fee:
$9.80 per $1,000 of work ($11 in Montréal-Nord), with a minimum of $167.40 for an exclusively
residential building and $491.70 for a commercial or mixed building. For a
vegetated roof, several boroughs require an application in person at the permit
counter. Winter scheduling and permits are covered in
[winter renovation permits in Montreal and Laval](/en/articles/winter-renovation-permits-montreal-laval/).

## The index required changes from one borough to the next

Two neighbouring streets in two different boroughs do not have the same list.
Here is what the city's page showed on October 31, 2026, unchanged since its
February 2, 2026 update.

| Solar reflectance index required | Boroughs |
|---|---|
| 56 | Côte-des-Neiges and Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, LaSalle, Plateau-Mont-Royal, Mercier and Hochelaga-Maisonneuve |
| 66 | Ahuntsic-Cartierville, Pierrefonds-Roxboro, Villeray, Saint-Michel and Parc-Extension |
| 78 | Anjou, Lachine, Sud-Ouest, Île-Bizard and Sainte-Geneviève, Montréal-Nord, Rivière-des-Prairies and Pointe-aux-Trembles, Rosemont and La Petite-Patrie, Saint-Laurent, Verdun, Ville-Marie |
| 82 | Saint-Léonard |
| 78 over 75% of the surface, or 66 over the whole | Outremont |

Several boroughs exclude the part of the roof taken up by mechanical equipment
or a terrace. The page cites the Règlement d'urbanisme 01-274 and the Règlement
sur la construction et la transformation de bâtiments 11-018 in the
Ahuntsic-Cartierville entry; for any other address, the by-law involved is
confirmed at the borough's permit counter. For the rest of the climate
adaptation work, see
[climate resilient renovations](/en/articles/climate-resilient-renovations-quebec/).

## Laval: the urban planning code also requires white or green

Since November 11, 2022, Laval has applied its urban planning code (Code de
l'urbanisme), by-law CDU-1, which replaced the old zoning by-law L-2000. Its
section 380 is Laval's counterpart to the Montreal list. A main or accessory
building with a flat roof must carry one of the following coverings:

- a green roof;
- a material that is white, painted white or covered with a reflective coating
  or white ballast;
- a material with an SRI of at least 78, attested by the manufacturer's
  specifications or by a professional's specification;
- a combination of those coverings.

The code defines a flat roof as one with a slope of 16.7% (2:12) or less. Three
exemptions: structures and equipment authorized on a roof, accessory buildings
to a dwelling with a footprint under 40 m², and farm buildings. Unlike Montreal,
the threshold is the same in every neighbourhood: 78.

**The permit.** Laval's exterior residential renovation permit sheet separates
two cases. Replacing the roof covering with the same material, and adding
insulation, requires no permit. Replacing it with a new material requires one:
stated processing time of 30 days, cost of $92 for the first $20,000 of work,
plus $2.50 per $1,000 or part of $1,000. Going from a black membrane to a white
one, or to a green roof, is precisely the case that triggers the permit. If the
address is covered by a site planning and architectural integration program
(PIIA), further requirements apply: the city's Info-règlements tool shows this
address by address.

**No permit does not mean no rule.** Section 2 of the code requires all work to
comply with the by-law, permit or not. What happens to a black roof installed
before November 11, 2022 and redone identically is a matter of acquired rights,
covered in title 9 of the code: the city confirms it for a given address, before
the materials are ordered.

**New buildings.** Section 141 adds a structural requirement: the
frame of a flat roof must be designed to receive an extensive green roof, on top
of the Construction Code loads, when the building has more than 3 storeys and
the roof area exceeds 250 m².

| | Montreal | Laval |
|---|---|---|
| Slope covered | under 2:12 (16.7%) | 16.7% or less |
| Index required | 56 to 82 depending on the borough | 78 everywhere |
| Permit to change material | yes, except Anjou | yes, 30 days |
| Permit cost | $9.80 per $1,000, $167.40 minimum | $92 up to $20,000, then $2.50 per $1,000 |
| Green roof on a wood frame | RBQ guide: noncombustible construction | extensive only, 45 minute fire separation without sprinklers |

compares the two options.

## White roof, green roof, blue roof: three techniques, not three shades

The white roof is a covering. It changes nothing structurally and satisfies the
by-law through its index.

The green roof is a system laid over the waterproofing. According to the
environment ministry's Guide de gestion des eaux pluviales, the studies
reviewed give an average retention rate of 60%, in a range of 39 to 72%, a
delay in peak flow of 20 to 40 minutes, and a reduction in runoff volume of
45 to 60%.

The blue roof is temporary water storage. The guide is clear about where it
belongs: it applies above all to large flat industrial or commercial roofs,
residential roofs usually being sloped. And about its effect: retention reduces
the peak flow downstream, but the volume discharged to the sewer does not go
down, since the discharge is spread over a longer period.

| | White roof | Green roof | Blue roof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nature | a covering | a system on the membrane | water retention |
| Volume discharged to the sewer | unchanged | reduced | unchanged, spread out |
| Permanent load added | no | yes | the retained water |
| On the Montreal and Laval lists | yes, if the index is met | yes | not mentioned |

That last line counts: a blue roof does not exempt you from the index,
retention not being a covering.

## The load a green roof adds to an existing frame

The reference document is the guide from the Régie du bâtiment du Québec,
Critères techniques visant la construction de toits végétalisés, updated in
June 2015. Its basic conditions already rule out many buildings:
noncombustible construction, a roof no more than 46 m above grade, a slope of
no more than 17%, and a covering that is not of the multilayer, asphalt and
gravel type. The fire department must be consulted from the planning stage on.

**The weight.** The guide requires the structure to be designed under part 4 of
the Code taking into account the weight of a water saturated vegetated roof for
every calculation, including seismic loads. Substrate density is determined
under standard ASTM E2399-12 or, failing that, with an unfactored value of
2,000 kg/m³ for saturated density. The substrate must be at least 100 mm. The
arithmetic is simple: 100 mm at that density is 200 kg per square metre, before
the vegetation, before the snow and before the water held in the drainage
layer. The real figure for your roof comes from the engineer, under standard
ASTM E2397-11.

The calculation must also assume the worst: blocked drains, flooded substrate,
water rising to the scuppers or overflows, whose accumulation the guide caps at
150 mm. To the live loads you add the snow that vegetation holds differently
from a wind swept roof, and the substrate stored on site the day the membrane
gets redone. The environment ministry's guide says it among its limitations:
the weight of the snow can restrict the application to roofs with sufficient
load bearing capacity.

**What Laval's code takes over.** For buildings exempted from the Building
chapter of the Construction Code by its section 1.04, section 144 of Laval's
urban planning code takes over these requirements: calculations on the saturated
substrate under standards ASTM E2397 and E2399, a density of 2,000 kg/m³ when no
test is done, calculations by an engineer. It also sets three types of roof,
extensive (0.1 to 0.15 m of substrate), semi-intensive (over 0.15 m and up to
0.3 m) and intensive (over 0.3 m), and allows an extensive roof on a building of
combustible construction, with a fire separation of at least 45 minutes if the
building is not sprinklered. A green roof section may not exceed 300 m² on a
combustible building or 900 m² on a noncombustible one, nor 30 m in any
dimension. A green roof is prohibited over shingles or tiles.

## Permit, sealed plans and attestation: what the owner keeps

Two requirements in the RBQ guide answer the question everybody asks. All loads
must be shown on plans and specifications bearing the seal and signature of an
engineer. And for an existing building, structural resistance must be analyzed
by an engineer, taking into account the new permanent load, the increased snow
loads, rainwater accumulation and seismic behaviour. Part 10 of the Code, the
one on alterations, applies.

The waterproofing must be verified before the plant cover goes down, by one of
the six tests the guide lists, and the report is given to the owner, who must
also obtain from the designer an attestation that the guide's conditions have
been met. In Laval, section 144 puts this waterproofing test on the owner, at
the owner's cost, before installation, and requires a water supply point and
access to the roof.

Sealed plans, waterproofing report and attestation: that is what a buyer will
ask for, and what the
[seller's declaration](/en/articles/seller-declaration-form-quebec/) leads them
to question. The [seller's guide](/en/seller/) describes the rest of the file.

A limit to keep in mind: the RBQ guide governs buildings subject to the Code,
and its first condition is noncombustible construction. A Montreal plex with a
wood frame is not. What applies to your building gets verified with the Régie du
bâtiment, the municipality and an engineer, not in an article.

## What Montreal pays per square metre

The amounts grid of RénoPlex, Montreal's renovation program for buildings of 1
to 5 dwellings, updated on August 18, 2026, has two lines for flat roofs:

| Work | RénoPlex amount |
|---|---|
| Install a green roof | $100 per square metre |
| Redo the roof with a white membrane | $70 per square metre |

The caps are $26,000 for a single family home and $52,000 for a building of 2 to
5 dwellings. Eligibility depends on the block of work the building qualifies
for, and the amounts change from one update to the next: have yours confirmed
before signing with a roofer.

In Laval, no municipal green roof grant was found. By-law L-13114, in force
since June 10, 2024, subsidizes planted landscaping, but its text does not
mention roofs.

## Mandatory maintenance, and what the insurer has to know

Maintaining a green roof is not a good practice, it is a condition. The RBQ
guide requires a program drawn up by a landscape architect or another
specialist, given to the owner and recorded in a register kept on site,
covering irrigation, fertilization, weeding, replanting and inspection of the
edges, the drains, the flashing and the membrane. The mature vegetation cover
must be maintained at 80% or more.

On the insurance side, the rule is in the Civil Code of Québec. Article 2466
requires the insured to promptly declare circumstances that aggravate the risk
and result from the insured's own acts, where they are of a nature to
materially influence the premium, the assessment of the risk or the decision to
continue the insurance. Article 2467 gives what follows: the informed insurer
may cancel the policy or propose a new rate in writing, which the insured must
accept and pay within 30 days, failing which the policy ceases to be in force.

The Bureau d'assurance du Canada puts "had the roof redone" on its list of
changes to review at renewal. Two of its rules touch the green roof directly.
The indemnity for the building is the cost of rebuilding in the same place with
materials of the same kind and quality: a vegetated system must therefore sit
inside the insured amount. And the insurer must have the damaged part repaired,
not the whole roof redone. On a green roof, that means finding the leak under
the substrate, then lifting it. The ministry's guide notes it as a limitation:
since water is encouraged to stay longer on the roof, faulty waterproofing
becomes damaging.

No Bureau d'assurance du Canada publication consulted puts a figure on what a
vegetated roof does to a premium. The files that become hard to place are
described in
[a home that is hard to insure in Quebec](/en/articles/hard-to-insure-home-quebec/).

## What can be demonstrated, and what cannot

No official source consulted puts a figure on what a green roof or a white roof
adds to the resale price of a Montreal or Laval plex. If a supplier announces a
percentage of added value, ask for the study.

What can be documented is more modest. The National Research Council of Canada
instrumented two large green roofs in Toronto and published its observations in
2005: green roofs reduce heat flow through the roof, which lowers energy demand
for heating and cooling, whatever the roofing system, with greater efficiency
in summer than in winter. Neither the NRC nor the ministry's guide translates
that into dollars on an electricity bill: do not budget a saving. For the
membrane, the guide states a longer useful service life, thanks to protection
against ultraviolet light and the impact of rain, without advancing a number of
years. The particularities of a duplex flat roof are in
[the features of the Quebec house](/en/articles/quebec-house-construction-features/).
