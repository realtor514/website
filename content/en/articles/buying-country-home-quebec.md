A principal residence in the country is not a cottage with the heat turned up.
The rules that govern it come from the Commission de protection du territoire
agricole (CPTAQ, Quebec's agricultural land commission), the MRC, the municipal
council and the insurer, never from the seller. Here is what changes when a
rural address becomes the one where you live year round.

## Buying a country home in Quebec: the agricultural zone comes before the municipality

**The first document to ask for is not the certificat de localisation, it is the
zoning attestation.** The protected agricultural zone covers more than
63,500 square kilometres and extends over the territory of 955 municipalities,
in all 17 administrative regions of Quebec. Since November 9, 1978, on a
territory identified by decree, using land for purposes other than agriculture,
subdividing land, removing topsoil and cutting maple trees in a sugar bush have
been prohibited there.

The Commission sums up its rule without nuance: in the agricultural zone, you
may not subdivide, alienate or use a lot for purposes other than agriculture
without first obtaining its authorization. "Alienate" means sell, transfer or
give. So it is not only your project that can run into the Act, it is sometimes
the transaction itself. Any applicant can also obtain from the Commission an
official document attesting whether or not a lot is located in the agricultural
zone, by supplying the required information and paying the fees provided for in
section 15. A listing sheet does not replace that paper.

## Residential acquired rights: what you are really buying in the green zone

**A house that exists legally in the agricultural zone rests on a right, and not
every right is sold with it.** The real rights under sections 101, 103, 104 and
105 are attached to the lot: they apply to the extent that a non-agricultural
use was legally carried on or authorized there at the time the Act became
applicable to it. The personal rights under sections 31, 31.1 and 40 are
attached to the owner, and do not follow the property.

**An acquired right can be extinguished.** Section 102 extinguishes it when the
area that benefits from it has remained under plant cover for more than one year
since the provisions of the Act came into force on the lot, or after an
alienation. The Commission specifies that this section does not target sections
104 and 105. A lot gone to fallow is therefore not an aesthetic detail.

**Adding a use is not automatic.** Since June 21, 2001, adding or changing a
principal use for a purpose other than agriculture, within the area of acquired
rights, requires the Commission's authorization (section 101.1).

**The additional dwelling is blocked.** Since Bill 86 was tabled on
December 5, 2024, you may not add or build an additional dwelling, an additional
residence or any other additional building in which a dwelling is set up, on an
area benefiting from a residential acquired right, without the Commission's
authorization (section 101.2). Transitional measures cover those who held a
valid notice of compliance between June 21, 2001 and December 5, 2024, with five
years starting March 25, 2025 to begin the work. An intergenerational plan is
therefore decided here.
[The checks before buying vacant land](/en/articles/buying-vacant-land-quebec/)
go over this Act from the point of view of a bare lot.

## The farm next door: manure spreading, odours and separation distances

Article 976 of the Code civil du Québec requires neighbours to accept the normal
neighbourhood annoyances, according to the nature or location of their land, or
according to local custom. In the agricultural zone, the nature of the land and
local custom are agricultural.

The rest is in writing. The complementary document to the land use and
development plan of an MRC whose territory includes an agricultural zone must
contain parameters for determining the separation distances referred to in the
third paragraph of section 113 of the Act respecting land use planning and
development. These parameters take up the normative framework of the government
orientations, which sets spreading distances measured from any dwelling house.

| Spreading | June 15 to August 15 | Rest of the year |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid manure by tanker, on the surface | 75 m | 25 m |
| Liquid manure by tanker, incorporated | 35 m | None |
| Liquid manure by drop hose, on the surface | 40 m | None |
| Fresh solid manure, on the surface | 40 m | None |
| Compost | 20 m | None |

"None" means spreading is allowed right up to the edge of the field, as long as
the other laws and regulations are respected. Ten months a year, the field next
to your porch is therefore fully available.

The framework works in the other direction when a new dwelling house is built
near an existing commercial livestock operation: 322 metres from a hog fattening
operation, 119 metres from a dairy farm, 25 metres from a cultivated field. If
you are buying to rebuild, ask for the zoning grid and the MRC's interim control
by-law.

## The private road year round: who clears it, who pays, and undivided co-ownership

**No municipality is required to maintain a private road.** Section 70 of the
Municipal Powers Act gives it a discretionary power to maintain a private road
open to the public by tolerance, at the request of a majority of the owners or
occupants along it. Saint-Donat, in the MRC de Matawinie, has turned this power
into a by-law, and its text shows what a municipal "yes" looks like: a road
accessible at all times, connected to a public road or to a private road already
maintained, cleared for emergency vehicles, at least 100 metres long, serving at
least 4 dwellings, with a request filed before June 30 and signed by the
majority of the owners along it. The council keeps full discretion to accept or
not.

The service is paid for by the owners along the road. In Saint-Donat, winter
maintenance runs from October 15 to April 30, snow is cleared over a width of
six metres, and the charge is levied with the property tax, split equally over
each assessment unit, at the net cost of the service plus administrative fees of
about 3%. That item shows up on the tax bill.

**If the roadbed is held in undivided co-ownership**, the Code civil takes over.
The co-owners manage the property together (article 1025) and bear the
administration costs and other common charges in proportion to their share
(article 1019). Administrative decisions are taken by a majority of the
co-owners, in number and in shares, but unanimity is required to alienate the
property, partition it, charge it with a real right, change its destination or
make substantial changes to it (article 1026). Have the notary determine which
category replacing a culvert falls into. For winter,
[liability for snow removal](/en/articles/snow-removal-liability-quebec/) stays
the same as in the city.

## Insuring and financing a country home far from services

The Insurance Bureau of Canada names seven factors that determine the rate in
home insurance. One of them is proximity to a fire hydrant or a fire station: if
the home is not close to one, it could cost more to insure. The IBC publishes no
percentage, and no one can honestly give you one. The only reliable answer is a
written quote obtained before you waive your financing condition, as with any
[hard to insure home](/en/articles/hard-to-insure-home-quebec/).

The SCHL (CMHC) purchase program sheet is clear: the property must be habitable
and ready to be occupied at all times, and accessible year round. A private road
with no written maintenance agreement is hard to defend against that sentence.

**The appraisal can cost more than the rate.** The SCHL writes that down payment
requirements rest on the lending value, which is the lowest of the property's
market value, its purchase price and its construction cost. In a rural market,
where comparable sales are rare and sometimes far away, the lender's appraisal
can come in below your accepted price. The gap is not financed, it is paid in
cash. Run both scenarios with the
[mortgage calculator](/en/tools/mortgage/), and read
[how a comparable is built](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).

On resale, no promises. The APCIQ publishes by census metropolitan area and by
broad sector, not according to how rural a property is. In August 2026, in the
Montreal CMA, single-family homes sold in 42 days on average and inventory rose
26% year over year on the North Shore. No equivalent public series puts a figure
on the resale time for a rural road outside the CMA.

## Internet, collection and school buses: the answer depends on the address

**Internet.** Opération haute vitesse ended in March 2024 and more than
339,500 households now have access to a service offering at least 50 Mbps
download and 10 Mbps upload, with no data cap. The government publishes an
interactive map giving service status by civic address, last published on
August 28, 2026: served, project under way, or service to be confirmed. The
subsidized satellite program ended on March 31, 2026.

**Collection.** On a private road, the truck does not always come to your door.
In Sainte-Lucie-des-Laurentides, by-law 648-24 provides that units not fronting
on a public road leave their bins at the nearest intersection between the
private road and the public road.

**School transportation.** No provincial distance threshold exists. Student
transportation is organized by the centre de services scolaire (school service
centre), which sets its terms, and it cannot be required if the student lives
outside the area served by the school, as defined by that centre. Write to it
with the civic address, before the offer.

## The documents to demand before buying a country home

| Check | The document to obtain |
|---|---|
| Agricultural zone | The zoning attestation, by lot number |
| Acquired right | The notice of compliance or the Commission's decision |
| Odours | The zoning grid and the interim control by-law |
| Road | The status, the maintenance agreement, the resolution, the tax bill |
| Undivided co-ownership | The published deed, the agreement, the budget of charges |
| Insurance and loan | A written quote, the lender's position |
| Services | The Internet map, the collection by-law, the school service centre's reply |

The well and the septic system are checked as for a cottage, with one
difference: year round occupancy changes the daily flow and the pumping
schedule. The documents to request are in
[the checks before buying a cottage](/en/articles/buying-a-cottage-quebec/).
Each line becomes a dated condition in the promesse d'achat.

## Frequently asked questions

**The house is in the agricultural zone. Can I still buy it?**
Often yes, because an acquired right attached to the lot already exists. But
alienating a lot in the agricultural zone may require the Commission's
authorization, or a declaration of exercise of a right. That is a question for
the notary and the Commission before the promise to purchase, not after.

**I want to add a unit for my parents. Is it possible?**
Not without the Commission's authorization since section 101.2, on an area
benefiting from a residential acquired right. Have the lot's file checked before
buying for that project.

**The municipality has cleared the road for ten years. Is that guaranteed?**
No. Section 70 of the Municipal Powers Act is a power, not an obligation. Ask
for the resolution in force and the amount billed on the tax bill.

**How much will my insurance premium go up far from a fire station?**
No official source puts a figure on it. The Insurance Bureau of Canada only
confirms that this distance is one of the rating factors. Get two or three
written quotes for the exact address.
