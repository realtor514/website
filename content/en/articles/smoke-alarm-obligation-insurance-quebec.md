Your smoke alarm complies with your city's by-law. That says nothing about what
your insurer expects of you. Two sets of obligations overlap here, and most
owners only know the first one. Here are both, in Montreal and in Laval, and the
point where they meet: after a loss.

## The municipal by-law and the insurance contract do not measure the same thing

The city sets a safety floor and enforces it with a fine. The insurer prices a
risk. Among the seven factors that set your premium, the Bureau d'assurance du
Canada, the insurance industry's national association, includes the features of
the home, where it asks in so many words: "Is it equipped with a burglar alarm
system and fire or carbon monoxide detectors? Do they allow activated remote
monitoring?"

No municipal by-law requires remote monitoring. No insurer issues a statement of
offence. You have to satisfy both.

## The smoke alarm obligation in Quebec starts in your city's by-law

**In Montreal.** The Règlement sur le Service de sécurité incendie de Montréal
(RCG 12-003), the by-law on the city's fire service, requires the alarm to be
installed, maintained and in good working order. Every dwelling must have at
least one per storey, basement included. For buildings constructed after June
2015, an alarm must also be located in each room where people sleep and between
those rooms and the rest of the storey.

The location is precise: on the ceiling or between 10 and 30 cm from it, and in
the middle of the corridor if the storey has bedrooms. Across the agglomeration,
residential buildings constructed before 1985 must be equipped with lithium
alarms with a non-removable 10 year battery, unless they already have 120 volt
electric alarms.

One detail almost nobody knows: in apartment buildings, rooming houses and
condominiums, the alarms' proper functioning has to be verified and recorded in a
register at least once a year.

**In Laval.** By-law L-12137 requires a functioning alarm on every storey of a
dwelling unit or a dwelling. In residential buildings, the non-removable 10 year
lithium battery is mandatory as a replacement for the 9 volt battery. And in
buildings constructed after 1985, all alarms must be electrically connected in
accordance with the Code de construction du Québec.

**The date of manufacture, not the date of purchase.** Both cities say the same
thing: the alarm is replaced 10 years after the date of manufacture printed on
the casing, and without delay if no date appears on it. The Bâtiment chapter of
the Régie du bâtiment du Québec's Code de sécurité imposes the same deadline.

| | Montreal | Laval |
|---|---|---|
| By-law | RCG 12-003 | L-12137 |
| Minimum | 1 per storey, basement included | 1 per storey |
| 10 year lithium battery | Residential built before 1985 | Replacement for the 9 volt |
| Annual register | Apartments, rooming houses, condos | Not provided for in the by-law |
| Fine, natural person | $80 to $2,000 | $400 to $1,000 |
| Fine, legal person | $1,000 to $4,000 | $600 to $2,000 |

The amounts above are for a first offence. In Laval, two cases have their own
tariff: $80 for a tenant who fails to maintain their alarm, and $250 for the
absence of a non-removable lithium battery. The city also specifies that the fine
applies for each dwelling or room concerned. In a triplex, the multiplication
changes the order of magnitude.

## The carbon monoxide alarm: where it is mandatory

Carbon monoxide is released when an appliance burns a fuel: natural gas, propane,
heating oil, wood, gasoline. Colourless and odourless, it can only be detected
with an alarm.

In Laval, every property has to be equipped with one where there is a
combustion appliance or direct access to an indoor parking garage, and in a
multi-dwelling building the owner must install one in each of the dwellings. In
Montreal, the fire service states the same rule for residential, rental,
supervised and seniors' buildings, and recommends an appliance bearing the seal
of a recognized body such as CSA, ULC, cUL or cETL.

At the provincial level, the Bâtiment chapter of the Code de sécurité uses the
same trigger and requires a device compliant with standard CAN/CSA-6.19 with a
built-in alarm. Watch the scope, though: that chapter covers apartment buildings,
condominiums, hotels, motels and rooming houses, not the detached single family
home. For that one, the municipal by-law decides, and it differs from one city to
the next.

## Owner or tenant: who installs the smoke alarm, who maintains it

The split is written into the lease. The mandatory form of the Tribunal
administratif du logement, Quebec's rental board, contains this sentence in
section B: "The lessor and the lessee undertake, according to the
responsibilities of each, to comply with the regulations concerning the presence
and proper functioning of one or more smoke alarms in the dwelling and in the
building."

The lessor must deliver the dwelling in a good state of habitability and comply
with the laws on safety and sanitation, under articles 1854, 1910, 1911 and 1912
of the Civil Code. The lessee must comply with those same laws and give notice of
a defect, under article 1866.

In practice, Montreal speaks of a shared responsibility: the owner supplies and
installs at the required locations, the occupant maintains and tests the device
with the test button once a month. Laval adds that the tenant must inform the
owner of any absence or defect, and settles the battery question for carbon
monoxide: the occupant keeps the device in working order, the owner replaces it
when it is defective.

## The prevention visit: what the firefighter checks

These visits flow from the risk coverage plan, and the ministère de la Sécurité
publique sets their minimum content.

Seven items must at minimum be verified on each alarm: certification by a
recognized body such as CSA or ULC, the date of manufacture, the required number,
the condition of the device, unpainted and unaltered, the location, the
functioning and the primary and secondary power supply. The guide strongly
recommends adding the carbon monoxide alarm as soon as a risk exists: wood
heating, gas cooking, an attached garage.

Two practical points. The programme's cycle may not exceed seven years, and the
ministry recommends a maximum of five: the visit is not annual. It takes place at
reasonable hours, the Code municipal speaking of 7 a.m. to 7 p.m., and any
non-compliance has to be explained to you before the person leaves the premises.

the [buyer's guide](/en/buyer/).

## What the insurer asks for beyond the smoke alarm

Here the register changes. Before the contract is concluded, and at renewal, you
have to give the insurer all the relevant information that could influence its
decision to insure and the amount of the premium. This is a duty to disclose, not
a duty to equip.

Éducaloi describes the sanction precisely. In the case of a false declaration,
the insurer will not have to compensate you if it shows that you deliberately
lied or that it would have refused to insure you had it known the truth.
Otherwise you are compensated, but less than if the declaration had been
accurate. You must also inform it of a situation you created that aggravates the
risk: it may then adjust the premium, and you have 30 days to accept it, failing
which the insurance ends.

Note what that does not say. A missing alarm does not automatically void
coverage. The question, after a loss, will be what you had declared and what your
contract required of you. The Autorité des marchés financiers points out that the
contract can impose concrete risk reduction obligations, and that exclusions are
read in the policy, not in the municipal by-law. The only right address to settle
your case is your insurer.

The positive lever exists too. The BAC writes that installing loss prevention
devices can reduce your premiums, and that you should call your insurer when you
update the home's protection systems. The AMF adds a useful warning: ask for the
amount of the discount before you install the equipment. And one sentence from
the BAC sums up the original misunderstanding: "home insurance is not a
maintenance contract." Service lives are in [home maintenance costs in
Quebec](/en/articles/home-maintenance-cost-quebec/).

## Before you buy: what the mandatory form does not ask

Here is the gap I flag to every buyer. The mandatory Déclarations du vendeur sur
l'immeuble form, the seller's declarations on the property (DV 00001, version
06/2022), contains no question about smoke alarms or about carbon monoxide
alarms. It asks question D14.1 about damage suffered as a result of a fire, and
that is all. Nobody will declare the age of the devices on your behalf.

Three checks take five minutes during a showing. Read the date of manufacture on
the back of the casing. Count the devices per storey, basement included. And if
there is a combustion appliance or an attached garage, look for the carbon
monoxide alarm. In a Montreal plex, ask for the annual register as well.

What the inspector looks at item by item is in [the pre-purchase inspection
checklist](/en/articles/home-inspection-checklist-montreal/), and what makes an
insurer hesitate on one specific address is in [a home that is hard to
insure](/en/articles/hard-to-insure-home-quebec/).

## Frequently asked questions

**I have just bought. Do I have to replace the alarms?**
Look at the date of manufacture on the casing. In Montreal as in Laval, every
alarm is replaced 10 years after that date, and immediately if no date is
legible. In the Montreal agglomeration, a residential building built before 1985
has to move to non-removable lithium batteries, unless it already has 120 volt
electric alarms.

**My tenant removed the battery. Who is at fault?**
Both cities speak of a shared responsibility, and the Tribunal administratif du
logement lease puts it in black and white. In Laval, a tenant who fails to
maintain their alarm faces $80 for a first offence. The owner, for their part,
remains bound to supply and install.

**Can the insurer refuse to pay if the alarm was dead?**
It is not automatic, and nobody can answer in place of your insurer. The rule
targets false declaration and aggravation of the risk, not the equipment as such.
Read your particular conditions, then call your representative before a loss.

**Is a carbon monoxide alarm mandatory in a single family home?**
In Laval, yes, as soon as there is a combustion appliance or direct access to an
indoor parking garage, and the Montreal fire service states the same condition.
The Bâtiment chapter of the Code de sécurité, for its part, does not cover the
detached single family home: so it is your city that decides.
