The day you list your property, someone hands you an eleven page document, and
most sellers sign it in fifteen minutes. That document decides your price, your
freedom to change your mind, and the exact moment you owe a remuneration. Here
is the clause by clause read, with the questions to ask before you sign.

## The listing brokerage contract is a mandatory OACIQ form

Your broker does not draft this contract. Section 24 of the Loi sur le courtage
immobilier (Quebec's real estate brokerage act) is blunt: "The contract must be
evidenced in writing on the mandatory form drawn up by the Organization." For a
house or a plex of fewer than five dwellings owned by an individual, that form
is the *contrat de courtage exclusif, vente* (the exclusive listing brokerage
contract), identified as CCVE.

Three practical consequences. A verbal brokerage contract is null, under
section 26. You are required to perform your obligations only from the moment
you hold a duplicate of the contract, under section 25. And section 30 prohibits
renouncing, by private agreement, the rights set out in that chapter.

The regulation also frames how the form gets filled in: the broker must let you
read through the terms and give you every explanation before you sign, and may
make no addition, no change and no erasure once a party has signed.

## Clause 2: exclusivity, the term, and how to get out

The box on the first page says it plainly. If the property is sold during the
term of the contract, whether through the broker or not, the seller will owe a
remuneration. You keep the right to market the property yourself, but not the
right to sell without paying.

Clause 2.1 sets the closing date and time, at 11:59 p.m. If no date is written
in, the contract ends 30 days after it is entered into. The term is not imposed
on you: you are the one who writes it.

That same clause 2.1 governs the exit. Unless clause 11.1 stipulates otherwise,
you may terminate without cause at any time, but you must then pay the costs and
expenses the broker has already incurred, or compensate the broker for the
injury suffered. If the contract is instead stipulated to be non terminable,
section 28 of the Act still applies: you may terminate at your discretion within
the three days following the day you receive a duplicate signed by both parties,
by simple written notice. Section 29 adds that no remuneration may then be
claimed, unless a sale meeting the conditions of section 27 occurs.

The broker, for their part, may terminate only for serious cause, and not at an
inopportune time.

## Clause 4: the asking price, the inclusions, and what changes if you lower it

Clause 4.1 holds the asking sale price. That is not an appraisal, it is your
decision. Lowering it later cancels nothing: it is done through the
*Amendments* form, signed by both parties, and the rest of the contract keeps
running.

Read the rest of clause 4 carefully. Point 4.3 puts on you the costs of
repaying and discharging any hypothec, including any penalty payable in case of
early repayment. Point 4.4 specifies that the included items are sold without
the legal warranty of quality and at the buyer's own risk, but that they must be
in working order at delivery. Point 4.6 asks you to identify the rental
contracts the buyer will have to take over: water heater, alarm system, propane
tank. A leased tank forgotten on that line becomes a conversation at the
notary's office.

Clause 5 sets the date or the deadline for signing the deed of sale, and the
occupancy date. Clause 6 authorizes the distribution of the information and the
photographs to the broker to broker services.

Before you settle on the figure in clause 4.1, place your starting point with a
[property value estimate](/en/tools/home-estimate/), then ask the broker which
comparable sales support the recommendation.

## Clause 7: remuneration and the four cases where it becomes payable

No rate is set by law. Clause 7.1 offers two boxes: a percentage of the sale
price, or a lump sum, plus applicable taxes. The figure is negotiated before you
sign, not after.

That same clause 7.1 lists the four situations that make the remuneration
payable.

| Case under clause 7.1 | What triggers the remuneration |
|---|---|
| 1 | An agreement to sell is entered into during the contract, with or without the broker, and all of its conditions are met |
| 2 | A promise to purchase conforming to the conditions of the contract is presented to you and you refuse it |
| 3 | A sale occurs within the 180 days following the end of the contract, with a person who was interested during its term |
| 4 | A voluntary act on your part prevents the contract from being performed |

Clause 7.3 sets the share offered to the collaborating broker. The form warns
you that an unreasonable share toward other brokers can reduce their interest in
proposing your property to their clients. That is a figure to discuss, not a box
to fill in quickly.

Clause 7.4 provides for the reverse: no remuneration is owed if the broker or
the broker's spouse acquires an interest in the property, nor if, through the
buyer's fault, the deed is not signed or the price is not paid.

## The 180 days after the brokerage contract ends

This is the clause that surprises the most sellers, and it is the one to
understand exactly. Section 27 of the Loi sur le courtage immobilier renders
without effect any agreement binding you to pay a broker after the contract
expires, unless three conditions are met together: the contract is stipulated to
be exclusive, the sale is made with a person who was interested in the property
during the term of the contract, and the transaction occurs no more than 180
days after the expiry date, without your having entered into, in the meantime,
an exclusive contract with another licence holder.

Clause 7.1 of the form repeats those conditions word for word, adding that the
new exclusive contract must have been entered into "in good faith".

In other words: if you change brokers and sign a new exclusive contract, the old
claim falls away. If you let the contract expire and sell on your own, four
months later, to someone who visited during the listing, it does not fall away.
The question to ask before signing: at the end of the contract, will you give me
the list of interested persons?

## Clause 8: your declarations about the property, and their legal weight

Clause 8.1 is not a formality. In it you declare that you are the sole owner or
duly authorized to sign, that the property is not the subject of any other
brokerage contract, that no lease prevents the sale, and that you are a Canadian
resident within the meaning of the tax laws. Clause 8.5 commits you to provide a
certificat de localisation (location certificate) reflecting the current
physical state of the premises. Clause 8.6 requires the spouse's consent when
the property is the family residence.

To this is added the annex referred to in clause 12.1: the mandatory form
*Déclarations du vendeur sur l'immeuble* (the seller's declarations about the
property), the DV. In it you answer to the best of your knowledge and in good
faith about the soil, the roof, the water, infiltrations, public law
restrictions, a violent death that occurred on the property. The broker must
fill it in with you and have you sign it; if you refuse, the broker cannot enter
into the brokerage contract.

Why the rigour. Article 1726 of the Code civil du Québec requires the seller to
warrant the buyer against latent defects, and article 1728 adds that if the
seller knew of the defect or could not have been unaware of it, the seller must
also make good the injury suffered. A complete declaration is your best
protection. For a question that leaves you hesitating, have the wording
validated by a notary before you sign.

## Double representation and clause 9: what the broker owes you in return

Double representation is prohibited in Quebec. Concretely: if a buyer who
already has a buyer brokerage contract with your broker wants to make an offer
on your property, section 29.1 of the Act requires that broker to terminate the
buyer's contract, to recommend another broker to them, and to claim no
remuneration from that buyer.

If the buyer has no contract, this is not double representation, but the broker
must inform them that they represent your interests alone and must give them
fair treatment: objective information, without disclosing your confidential or
strategic information.

In return, clause 9.1 lists fourteen undertakings by the broker. The most useful
to remember: to act with loyalty, diligence and competence; to present you as
promptly as possible with any written promise received; to carry out the usual
verifications on the data used to describe the property; to place the "sold"
mention only once all the conditions of the agreement have been met, except the
signing of the deed and the payment; to disclose to you in writing any personal
interest and any sharing of remuneration other than the one provided for; and to
give you a duplicate of the contract. Clause 14.1 adds that in case of a
dispute, the OACIQ may act as conciliator, mediator or arbitrator if both
parties request it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I sign a three month contract rather than a six month one?**
Yes. No term is imposed by law, and you are the one who writes the date in
clause 2.1. Just know that the 180 day clause runs from that end date, whatever
term you choose.

**If I terminate, do I pay anything?**
Outside the three day window in section 28, clause 2.1 provides that you may
have to reimburse the costs and expenses already incurred, or compensate the
broker for the injury suffered. Ask in advance which expenses are planned.

**My neighbour visited during the listing and buys six months later. Do I owe
the remuneration?**
If more than 180 days have passed since the end of the contract, no. Within the
180 days, yes, unless you had entered in good faith into a new exclusive
contract with another broker.

**What happens if my broker changes agencies?**
Clause 10 gives you the choice: follow the broker to the new agency by sending
written notice, or stay with the agency. Failing notice, the contract is deemed
terminated on the day the broker begins practising there.
