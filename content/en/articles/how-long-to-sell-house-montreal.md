When a seller asks me how long it will take, they are usually thinking about the gap between listing and accepted offer. That is only part of the answer, and often the shortest part.

Here is the full timeline, including where the time actually gets lost.

## Three periods to separate

**1. Preparation.** From our first meeting to going live: usually two to four weeks. Professional photos, fixing visible minor repairs, decluttering, updating the certificate of location if needed, preparing the seller's declarations. You can compress this, but rarely to your advantage.

**2. Time on market.** From listing to accepted offer. This is the number everyone talks about.

**3. Time to closing.** From accepted offer to signing at the notary: usually 45 to 60 days, sometimes 90. That window covers the inspection, the buyer's financing and the notary's work. It compresses very little.

In other words, even a fast sale rarely gets you to less than three months between deciding to sell and having the money in your account.

## Time on market, by segment

Average selling time varies significantly by property type and area. In Greater Montreal, the usual order of magnitude:

- **A well-positioned single-family home in a strong area:** often a few weeks, sometimes days when inventory is low.
- **Condominiums:** generally longer than single-family, because condo inventory is more abundant and buyers comparison-shop more.
- **Plexes and income properties:** longer still. The buyer is an investor analyzing numbers, and financing is more complex.
- **Atypical or luxury properties:** measured in months, not weeks. The buyer pool is small by definition.

These figures shift year to year with interest rates and inventory. The Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers publishes current average selling times by area and category, and that is the reference to check at the moment you are asking the question, rather than a statistic you read two years ago.

## The three decisions that set your timeline

The market explains part of your timeline. Your decisions explain the rest, and that is the part you control.

### Asking price

By far the number one factor.

A property gets maximum visibility in its first 7 to 14 days on Centris. That is when it appears in the automatic alerts of every buyer whose criteria match. If the price is too high, those buyers look, compare, and move on.

After 30 days the question changes. Buyers stop asking whether your property suits them and start asking why nobody else wanted it. After 60 days, perceived value has dropped and the offers you receive are often below what you would have gotten in week one at a fair price.

Cutting the price three times in a row takes longer and nets less than setting it correctly once.

### Presentation

Buyers eliminate online before they ever visit. Dark photos, a cluttered space, a badly framed main room: the property is ruled out before anyone walks through the door. Preparation is not cosmetic. It determines how many showings you get, and showings determine your timeline.

### Showing availability

The most underestimated factor. A seller who only allows showings on Saturday afternoons adds weeks to their own timeline. Serious buyers view when they are available, not when you are.

## Where time is lost after the offer

An accepted offer is not a sale. Timelines stretch most often for three reasons:

**The inspection turns something up.** You then renegotiate, get quotes, sometimes do work. Add one to three weeks.

**The buyer's financing hits a snag.** A pre-approval is not a final approval. The lender's appraisal can come in below the sale price, or the buyer's employment situation may have changed.

**The certificate of location is out of date.** If yours is more than ten years old, or if work has been done since, the notary will require a new one. Land surveyor turnaround can exceed several weeks in peak season. This is the most common and most avoidable cause of delayed closings.

Have your certificate of location checked at the start of the process, not when the notary asks for it.

## The realistic timeline

For a properly prepared, properly priced property with open showing availability, in a normal Greater Montreal area:

- Preparation: 2 to 4 weeks
- On market: a few weeks
- Accepted offer to signing: 45 to 60 days

Realistic total: three to four months between your decision and the signature. Plan for that window if you are coordinating with a purchase.
