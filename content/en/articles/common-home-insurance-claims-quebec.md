Almost everybody believes they have "home insurance." Almost nobody knows whether
the loss most likely to happen at their address is inside or outside the
contract. Here is what the base policy covers in Quebec, what has to be bought
separately, and the three numbers that decide the amount you will receive.

## Common home insurance claims in Quebec: what the industry measures

The Bureau d'assurance du Canada, the insurance industry's national association,
is categorical: for more than ten years, water damage has been the leading cause
of home insurance claims. In 2024, 68% of losses paid in Quebec involved water,
at an average cost of $23,550.

The data point that matters to you is rarely quoted: the BAC specifies that the
main cause of those claims is water damage linked to water infiltration from the
ground and from sewers. So the most frequent scenario is the one the base
contract does not cover. The Autorité des marchés financiers confirms it: you
generally have to buy additional protection.

## What the base home insurance policy covers, and what it excludes

Every contract has two sides: civil liability, for the damage you cause to
others, and insurance of your property, for the damage you suffer. This article
is about the second. According to the Bureau d'assurance du Canada, whatever the
policy:

| Generally covered as standard | Never covered | Bought by endorsement |
|---|---|---|
| Fire and forest fires | Landslides and rockslides | Ground water and sewers |
| Theft or attempted theft | Pollution | Water above ground |
| Vandalism | Criminal acts of the insured | Overflow of a body of water |
| Wind, hail, lightning | War and terrorism | Earthquake |
| Explosion and smoke | Nuclear risk | Outdoor pool and spa |
| Impact of objects or vehicles, riot | Illegally acquired property | |

Source: Bureau d'assurance du Canada, consumer information pages.

One point the summaries skip: earthquake is **never** included in a base
contract, and an outdoor pool or spa, with its equipment and its deck not
attached to the house, also requires an addition to the contract.

## Water damage, the most frequent loss: four origins, only one included

The word "water" does not designate one risk, it designates four, and the insurer
treats them separately.

**What is included as standard.** Damage caused by a plumbing fixture, a bathtub,
a washer, a water heater, and the breakage of a drinking water pipe, what the AMF
calls the public water main. A pipe that bursts, a washer that overflows: you are
covered with nothing added.

**What requires an endorsement.** Water that enters through the roof, the walls, a
door or a window falls under the Eau au-dessus du sol endorsement, above ground
water. Water that enters through the foundation or a basement window, or that
backs up through a weeping tile or a sewer, falls under a different endorsement,
Eau du sol et égouts, ground water and sewers. Two protections, two purchases.

**Flooding.** The BAC gives the exact definition: in insurance, a flood is the
result of the overflow of a natural body of water that reaches your property. It
requires a third endorsement, which the AMF says is not offered everywhere,
before adding a sentence worth reading twice: emergency financial assistance from
the government is not guaranteed and is often limited. In a flood zone, start with
[the checks to make before you buy](/en/articles/buying-in-flood-zone-quebec/);
for the physical points of entry, see [spring melt and basement water
infiltration](/en/articles/spring-melt-water-infiltration-montreal/).

## Deductible, endorsement limit and replacement cost: the three numbers on the cheque

A covered loss does not mean a full reimbursement. Three mechanisms reduce the
indemnity, and they stack.

**The deductible.** The amount you agree to pay. The BAC indicates that it usually
ranges between $300 and $1,000 in home insurance, and that it is deducted from the
indemnity.

**The endorsement's coverage limit.** The BAC is explicit: a water damage
endorsement comes with a coverage amount distinct from that of your policy. If the
house is insured for $500,000 and the ground water endorsement caps out at
$25,000, that cap is what applies to a backup. Check it before, not after.

**Replacement cost or value on the day of the loss.** Your policy generally
provides for reimbursement without applying depreciation, what is called
replacement cost value. It comes with conditions, and that is the part nobody
reads: the property must actually be replaced, or the house rebuilt on the same
site. If you do not replace, you receive the value on the day of the loss, less
depreciation. The BAC's worked example is the clearest.

| Refrigerator bought ten years ago | $800 |
|---|---|
| Price of an equivalent appliance today | $1,000 |
| Indemnity at replacement cost | $1,000 |
| Depreciation, $40 a year over 10 years | $400 |
| Indemnity at value on the day of the loss | $600 |

On top come limits by category, indicative and negotiable upward: stolen jewellery
capped at $1,000, works of art at $5,000, cash at $200 on most contracts.

One last number, the building's: the BAC reminds you that the amount of insurance
is neither your mortgage, which includes the land, nor the municipal assessment,
but the cost of rebuilding. The first premium is paid before you take possession,
along with the other outlays priced by [the closing costs
calculator](/en/tools/closing-costs/).

## Declaring what aggravates the risk: articles 2408, 2411 and 2466

Article 2408 of the Civil Code requires you to declare all the circumstances known
to you "which are likely to materially influence an insurer in the setting of the
premium, the appraisal of the risk or the decision to cover it." Éducaloi gives
the example that touches a buyer directly: the fact that the basement has already
suffered water damage is a past event to be declared.

Article 2410 makes misrepresentation and concealment punishable by nullity of the
contract, "even with respect to losses not connected with the risk so
misrepresented." Article 2411 softens the penalty in damage insurance: unless bad
faith is established, or proof that the insurer would have refused the risk, the
insurer remains bound to the indemnity, but reduced in the proportion of the
premium collected to the premium it should have collected.

The obligation continues after signing. Article 2466 requires you to promptly
declare the circumstances that aggravate the risk and that result from your own
acts. Once informed, the insurer may cancel the contract or propose a new premium
in writing, which you have 30 days to accept and pay, failing which the policy
ceases to be in force, under article 2467. Article 2468 sets aside two cases:
non-occupancy of no more than 30 consecutive days and tradespeople's work of no
more than 30 days.

The contract also imposes concrete actions. The AMF's example for a winter
absence: the insurer may require that someone come by to check the heating, or
that the water be shut off and the systems drained. Not respecting the condition
means losing the right to claim for freeze damage.

## Claiming: article 2470, the 60 days, and the backwater valve your city requires

Article 2470 requires you to declare to the insurer any loss that may give rise to
coverage "as soon as he becomes aware of it." No deadline in days: the rule is
awareness. If the insurer is not informed and suffers injury as a result, it may
invoke its forfeiture clause. Article 2472 is brutal: any deceitful
representation entails the loss of the right to indemnity for the risk concerned,
which the AMF translates into three consequences, refusal to indemnify,
cancellation of the contract, difficulty insuring yourself afterwards. On its
side, the insurer must pay within 60 days following the declaration or the
documents requested: that is article 2473.

**The municipal side, often forgotten.** A sewer backup sometimes opens a second
recourse, against the city, with a short deadline and a condition of entry.
Montreal writes that its liability is not automatic for a backup if you did not
have a backwater valve in good working order, and that a claim for material damage
is filed within 15 days, the lawsuit within 6 months. Backwater valves have been
mandatory there in all new buildings since 2011, under by-law 11-010. In Laval,
section 3.03.02 of by-law L-5057 requires backwater valves on drain connections
located below the crown of the street, then adds that if they are not installed or
maintained, "the city is not liable for damage caused to the building and/or its
contents as a result of flooding or sewer backup."

## The effect of a claim on the premium and on insurability

The BAC ranks claims experience among the premium factors, in two parts: the
number and type of claims you have already made, and the crime and fire rate of
the area where your home is located. For a buyer, the consequence is concrete. You
do not inherit the seller's contract: article 2475 of the Civil Code provides that
an insurance contract may be assigned only with the consent of the insurer. So you
have to insure yourself, and answer a questionnaire about a property whose history
you do not know.

Let us be precise. No open official source describes a Quebec claims registry kept
by address. What is verifiable is your own obligation: article 2408 forces you to
declare the circumstances known to you, and Éducaloi counts among those the past
events connected with the property, such as water damage in the basement. So ask
the seller the question before the offer: you will then have to answer it
yourself. The mandatory form touches on it, and its detail is explained in [the
seller's declaration in
Quebec](/en/articles/seller-declaration-form-quebec/).

## Frequently asked questions

**I have water damage protection. Am I covered for a backup?**
Not necessarily. Sewer backup falls under the Eau du sol et égouts endorsement,
and infiltration through the roof under a different one. Ask in writing which one
appears on your contract and what its coverage amount is.

**Is it worth claiming for small damage?**
Do the math before you call. The deductible, usually $300 to $1,000, is deducted
from the indemnity, the property may be subject to a limit, and the number of
claims goes into the calculation of your future premium according to the BAC.

**My basement flooded from a backup. Who do I contact first?**
Your insurer, as soon as you become aware of it, as article 2470 requires. Then
open the municipal file in parallel if you believe the city is at fault: in
Montreal the deadline is 15 days, and the absence of a valve in good working
order blocks you.
