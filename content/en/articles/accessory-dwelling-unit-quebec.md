An empty basement, an attached garage used as a shed, a lot deeper than the
others on the street. The reflex is to calculate a rent. The useful order is the
reverse: what the by-law allows at your address, what the authorization costs,
then what a lender and an insurer agree to see in it. Here are the three steps,
with the figures published by Laval, by Montreal and by CMHC.

## A lot's potential is read in the zoning grid

Two documents decide before you do: the zoning sheet for your address and a
recent certificate of location. The tax bill decides nothing.

**In Laval, the check is done online.** The Info-reglements tool allows a search
by lot or by address. You enter the address, you click Voir les details, then you
scroll below the map. If the notice "Attention! Des dispositions particulieres
s'appliquent a votre zone" appears, a site planning and architectural
integration programme applies and additional requirements are added. Without the
notice, the file follows the standard process.

**In Montreal, there is no single rule.** The urban planning by-law is the
borough's, and the City names it in its procedures: by-law 01-274 for
Ahuntsic-Cartierville, for example. Fees, documents and timelines vary from one
permit counter to the next. A high land value on the assessment roll is never an
authorization, and [reading the roll and the
grid](/en/articles/land-value-zoning-quebec/) explains why.

## Adding an accessory dwelling unit in Laval: what is allowed

Laval publishes a dedicated procedure. The official vocabulary speaks of an
unite d'habitation accessoire, the UHA, and a permit must be obtained before
fitting out an additional dwelling inside an existing single-family home. Four
limits shape the project:

- The conversion is authorized only in an already existing 1 dwelling home.
- Creating a UHA entirely detached from the principal residence is not permitted
  anywhere on the territory.
- The main configurations are the addition, the attached garage converted into a
  dwelling, and the basement fitted out as a bachelor unit.
- Access is flexible: a door opening onto the main dwelling or a shared hall, a
  connecting door locked or not, a separate entrance possible on any facade.

One detail matters for the budget: adding an extra parking space is not
mandatory.

Among the advantages it lists, the City names rental income and the possibility
of occupying the additional dwelling while renting out the main portion. The
municipal authorization therefore does not lock you into a family use in Laval.
Elsewhere, the answer is read on the zoning sheet for the address, never by
analogy with a neighbouring city.

## The permit, the documents and the bill

The fees are published. They get budgeted before the plans.

| Item | Laval | Montreal |
|---|---|---|
| Permit | $732 for an additional dwelling, plus $92 for the first $20,000, then $2.50 per $1,000 bracket | $9.80 per $1,000 of work, minimum $167.40 residential |
| Service connection | $450 for the first dwelling, $20 per additional dwelling | Depends on the borough |
| Work in the street right of way | $300 for 1 to 4 dwellings | Depends on the borough |
| Announced timeline | 30 days | 30 to 60 days in Saint-Laurent |

In Montreal, the estimate must include all costs related to the work, taxes
included, and the review fees are not refundable. The permit for a building of
four storeys or less stays valid for 18 months, and the work must begin within
12 months.

**The file is heavier than the bill.** For an addition, a second storey or the
conversion of the attached garage, Laval requires a complete architect's plan,
signed and sealed by an architect under section 16 of the Loi sur les architectes
where that applies, a recent certificate of location from a land surveyor, and a
service connection sheet. In a flood plain or on a waterfront lot, add the
shoreline, the flood elevations, the zone d'intervention speciale and the highest
known water levels. In Montreal: certificate of location, building sheet, cost
quotation and complete plans of every level.

## What a lender counts as income from an accessory dwelling unit

This is where projects get revised: the projected rent does not enter the
lender's calculation in full, and the rule depends on the number of dwellings and
on occupancy. CMHC publishes the approaches it accepts in mortgage loan
insurance. For an owner occupied building covered by the loan application: with
two dwellings, the gross rental income approach allows up to 100% of gross rents
to be added to gross annual income, and the taxes and heating of the subject
building may be excluded from the ratio calculation. With three or four
dwellings, only two choices: up to 50% of gross rents, or net rental income,
meaning gross rents less operating expenses. If the owner does not occupy the
building, the range also drops to 50% of gross or to net income.

The practical consequence: going from one dwelling to two is the most
advantageous step from the calculation's point of view, and the advantage is lost
at the third. Run the cost of the work and the projected rent through the
[mortgage calculator](/en/tools/mortgage/), then have the ratios validated by a
mortgage broker: he and the lender are the ones who decide.

**Do not budget on help that does not exist yet.** The Canada Secondary Suite
Loan Program appears in Canada's Housing Plan with the notation "in development".
It promises low interest loans. As long as it is not accepting applications, it
is not part of your financing structure.

## Subdividing rather than adding: the question comes before the plans

If your lot clearly exceeds the sector's standard, severance is a distinct
option, with its own entry door: the subdivision permit, then the cadastral
operation. Laval handles these applications through an electronic service, and
the cadastral documents attached must be valid according to the instructions of
the minister responsible for the cadastre, with a prevalidation of the files done
before the permit application. The file therefore begins at the land surveyor's
office, not at the counter. The rest of the route, from the parks contribution to
the servitudes, is detailed in [reading the roll and the
zoning](/en/articles/land-value-zoning-quebec/).

## Taxes, assessment and insurance: what changes after the work

**The roll moves.** One more dwelling modifies the inventory of your assessment
unit. When the modification gives rise to a notice, Montreal indicates that the
application for review is filed within 60 days following the sending of that
notice, or before the May 1 following the coming into force of the roll,
whichever is later, with fees starting at $90 for a building of $500,000 or less.
That deadline gets watched, it does not get discovered.

**Insurance is settled before the first tenant.** The Insurance Bureau of Canada
is blunt: failing to disclose all information relating to occupancy can lead to
the cancellation of a home insurance policy. Its example is exactly our scenario,
the tenant who accidentally starts a fire in the basement while the insurer did
not know he was there. The Bureau adds that ordinary home policies cover neither
the contents nor the civil liability of tenants, and that landlord coverage can
include loss of rental income and defence costs. Consult your representative
before the work, keep written proof of the notice, require annual proof of
insurance from the tenant.

On the tax side, renting out part of your principal residence opens the change of
use regime, explained in [the intergenerational home
by-law](/en/articles/intergenerational-home-montreal-laval/). An accountant
validates your case, not a broker.

## Renting the accessory unit: the lease and setting the rent

As soon as there is a lease, you enter the jurisdiction of the Tribunal
administratif du logement (Quebec's rental board), and three rules get checked
before setting a price.

**The form is mandatory.** The Tribunal's lease has been required since
September 1, 1996 for every new residential lease, whether it is a room, an
apartment or a house.

**The notice to the new tenant is not optional.** The landlord must state the
lowest rent paid during the 12 months preceding the start of the lease or, where
applicable, the rent fixed by the Tribunal. If no rent was paid during those 12
months, the notice must state the last rent paid and its date. And no deposit may
be required, whether for keys or furniture.

**The restriction on the right to have rent fixed targets the building, not the
dwelling.** Neither the landlord nor the tenant may ask the Tribunal to fix the
rent where the dwelling is located in a building erected five years ago or less,
or in a building whose use for residential purposes results from a change of
destination five years ago or less. A tenant who then refuses the increase must
leave at the end of the lease. Since February 21, 2024, to invoke this
restriction, the landlord must also state in the lease the maximum rent that may
be imposed during the five years following the date on which the building was
ready for habitation.

No source I consulted lets me say whether a dwelling added inside an already
residential house falls into either of those two descriptions. Do not assume it
does, and do not copy the clause into the lease "just in case": have the question
settled by a legal adviser. The other obligations that arrive with a first tenant
are gathered in [managing a rental
property](/en/articles/rental-property-management/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I build a small detached dwelling at the back of my yard in Laval?**
No. The City writes that creating a UHA entirely detached from the principal
residence is not permitted on its territory. What remains is the addition, the
converted attached garage and the bachelor style basement.

**Do I have to add a parking space?**
In Laval, adding an extra space is not mandatory in a single-family building. In
Montreal, the number of parking spaces adjusts to the intended use: the answer
comes from the borough.

**How much does an accessory dwelling unit add to resale value?**
No official source lets me put a number on it. What can be demonstrated: a
permitted, compliant dwelling with its lease in order gets financed, whereas an
improvised dwelling will have to explain itself to the next buyer's lender and
insurer.
