The question I get most often about the North Shore is "which sector is the
best one?" I never answer it, and not out of commercial caution: a broker has
no business handing out grades to neighbourhoods. What I can do, and what this
article does, is give you the comparison grid and the exact address of every
official data source, so you can decide for yourself.

Pick two sectors you are hesitating between, open the seven sources below for
each of them, and the conversation changes completely.

## 1. Transit, priced honestly

The Autorité régionale de transport métropolitain divides the region into four
fare zones. Laval sits in zone B. The northern crown, which includes Blainville,
Boisbriand, Bois-des-Filion, Lorraine, Mascouche, Mirabel, Repentigny, Rosemère,
Sainte-Thérèse, Saint-Eustache, Saint-Jérôme and Terrebonne, sits in zone C.

That split has a price. Since July 1, 2026, the All Modes monthly pass at the
regular fare costs $110 for zone A, $170 for AB, $206 for ABC and $281 for
ABCD. A household with two adults commuting downtown therefore pays $72 more
per month from the northern crown than from Laval, or $864 a year. That amount
belongs in the housing budget, not the transport budget.

Then check the actual service rather than the map: schedules and frequency for
exo train lines 12 Saint-Jérôme and 15 Mascouche on exo.quebec, and, since
November 17, 2025, the REM Deux-Montagnes branch, which changed the options
available from western Laval and Deux-Montagnes. Then make the trip yourself,
on a Tuesday at 7:30 a.m., in both directions.

## 2. Municipal taxes, which really do differ between cities

Every municipality sets its own rate per $100 of assessment, every year, plus
its own tariffs for water and waste collection. Those tariffs are often a flat
amount per dwelling, which means they weigh proportionally more on a modestly
valued property.

Two tax bills cannot be compared head to head, because the two cities'
assessment rolls may not share a reference year. Compare the rates published in
each city's budget instead, along with the actual overall tax rate each
municipality files with the Ministère des Affaires municipales et de
l'Habitation, published in the Profil financier des municipalités locales and
on Données Québec. For the mechanics, read
[how a tax bill is built](/en/articles/property-tax-explained/).

## 3. School tax, which does not differ

This one works the other way around, and many buyers do not know it. The school
tax rate is a single rate for all of Quebec. For 2026-2027 it is $0.07899 per
$100 of assessment, with an exemption on the first $25,000 of the assessment of
every taxable building. Moving to another city does not change your school tax
rate: only the property's assessment does. Cross that criterion off the list.

## 4. Transfer duties, to be checked city by city

For 2026 the provincial scale is 0.5% on the portion up to $62,900, 1% from
$62,900.01 to $315,000, and 1.5% above that. But a municipality may adopt a
by-law imposing a higher rate on the portion above $500,000, up to 3%, and
Montreal is allowed to go higher still. Ask the city for its by-law before you
sign. The details are in
[the welcome tax explained](/en/articles/welcome-tax-quebec-guide/), and the
[transfer duty calculator](/en/tools/welcome-tax/) does the arithmetic.

## 5. The building stock and its age

The property assessment roll is produced every three years by the municipal
body responsible for assessment. It carries owners' names, the characteristics
of the land and the building, their values and the cadastral lot number.
Several cities, Laval and Terrebonne among them, publish it online.

The year of construction tells you what to look for before you even visit:
aluminum wiring, an oil tank, the type of attic insulation, an original
foundation drain. For a picture of a whole sector rather than one address,
Statistics Canada's 2021 Census Profile gives the period of construction of
dwellings by municipality. After that, use the
[first visit checklist](/en/articles/first-visit-property-checklist/) and the
[pre-purchase inspection guide](/en/articles/home-inspection-cost-timing-quebec/).

## 6. Zoning is what can be built next door

A city's zoning by-law states which uses are permitted in each zone, at what
height and at what density. The vacant lot behind the yard and the parking area
on the corner are already described in it.

Add the metropolitan layer. The Plan métropolitain d'aménagement et de
développement of the Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal identifies 159 TOD
areas, drawn as a radius of 0.5 or 1 km around access points of the structuring
transit network, and aims to direct at least 60% of new households from 2011 to
2031 into them. The interactive map sits at the Observatoire du Grand Montréal.
If your sector appears there, densification is not a rumour, it is a written
planning direction.

## 7. Schools, without a ranking

The Ministère de l'Éducation calculates a socio-economic environment index
every year. It combines two census indicators: the share of families with
children whose mother holds no diploma, which carries two thirds of the weight,
and the share of households whose parents were not employed during the census
reference week, for the remaining third. Public schools are then sorted into
deciles.

It measures the environment, not the quality of teaching. Treat it as data,
never as a ranking. Then verify the catchment area, the boundary that ties a
specific address to a specific school: it is set by the centre de services
scolaire and it can be revised. Two houses on the same street are not always in
the same catchment, and a real estate portal's map carries no weight on this.
Call the centre de services scolaire with the exact address. Quebec.ca's Trouver
un organisme scolaire tool points you to the right one. Also worth reading:
[schools and property values](/en/articles/school-districts-property-values/).

## 8. Water and soil

The regulatory framework for water environments has changed: it replaced the
transitional regime on March 1, 2026. The Quebec government's interactive map of
flood zones and watercourse mobility zones can be searched by address at
zonesinondables.mrnf.gouv.qc.ca. It is indicative: the municipality or the MRC
confirms the exact boundary. On the North Shore, any property fronting the
rivière des Mille Îles deserves that check every single time.

For soil, the Ministère de l'Environnement's contaminated sites registry and
its Repère GTC mapping tool list the files brought to the ministry's attention,
including sites already rehabilitated. It is not an exhaustive inventory: the
absence of a record is not proof of anything. The certificate of location, for
its part, flags flood zones and servitudes.

## 9. Market numbers

The Quebec Professional Association of Real Estate Brokers publishes monthly
statistics drawn from the Centris database, by metropolitan area and by
municipality. That is the source to quote, rather than a national average or an
automated estimate. My
[market analysis](/en/articles/marche-immobilier-montreal-analyse-donnees/)
starts there.

## Verifying beats trusting a reputation

A sector's reputation gets repeated, not verified, and it usually runs about ten
years behind the ground. The opposite method takes one evening per sector: open
the assessment roll, the zoning by-law, the flood map, Repère GTC, the ARTM fare
grid and the city budget, write down seven answers, keep the source for each
one. Add two visits on site, a Tuesday at rush hour and a Saturday morning, and
read the agendas of the last few city council meetings, which announce projects
before the papers do.

If a number cannot be found anywhere, it does not enter the decision. That is
the only rule that genuinely protects a buyer.
