The offer on the table does not cover the balance of your loan. The sale is still
possible, but it will not be signed until one precise figure is gathered: the gap
between what the property brings in and what has to be paid to free the title.
Here are the items to add up, and what Quebec law requires before the signing.

## Selling a home worth less than the mortgage: the full calculation

This situation does not require a market collapse. It arrives with a thin down
payment, a term already under way and selling costs nobody had budgeted for. For
the Montreal metropolitan area in July 2026, the APCIQ recorded a 4% rise in the
median price of single family homes, but also 17% more active listings and an
average selling time of 38 days. More choice for the buyer, less pricing power for
the seller.

The calculation is done item by item, and every item has a source.

| Item | Who gives the figure | What to ask for |
|---|---|---|
| Payout balance | The lender | The amount as at the target closing date |
| Prepayment penalty | The lender | The amount and the two rates used |
| Administration and appraisal fees | The lender | The complete list, in writing |
| Cash back received at signing | The lender | The repayable portion |
| Fees for the quittance | The notary | A written quote |
| Registration of the discharge | The Registre foncier | Public tariff, see below |
| Broker's remuneration plus taxes | The brokerage contract | Clause 7.1 |
| Certificate of location | The land surveyor | The turnaround as much as the price |

The proceeds of the sale are no more a matter of invention: they start from
adjusted comparables, not from the price your neighbour is asking, and the method
is in
[auditing the comparables in your sector](/en/articles/real-estate-comparables-quebec/).
The gap to cover is the total of the left column minus those net proceeds. A
number, not an impression.

## The penalty, the item everyone underestimates

**A sale that repays the loan before the end of the term triggers the penalty.**
The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) says so, and it states the
calculation rule in one sentence: the penalty is usually the higher of an amount
representing three months' interest on what you owe, and the interest rate
differential. Its worked example gives the measure of the gap. A balance of
$200,000, a rate of 6%, 36 months left on a five year term, a current posted rate
of 4%: three months' interest gives $3,000, the differential gives $12,000, and it
is $12,000 that is payable, plus possible administration fees.

The two rates compared are the one posted when you signed and the current posted
rate for a term of similar length. A drop in current rates therefore widens the gap
and swells the differential. The Bank of Canada's target for the overnight rate was
2.25% on September 2, 2026, with the next announcement on October 28, 2026, but the
differential is not calculated on that rate: it is calculated on the posted rates
for a comparable term. Which method applies to your loan is stated in your contract,
not by the product's reputation, and the agency publishes no separate scale for
variable rates. The possible combinations are detailed in
[how the mortgage penalty is calculated](/en/articles/breaking-mortgage-penalty-quebec/).

This item is not a contractual surprise. Clause 4.3 of the OACIQ exclusive
brokerage contract to sell, form CCVE 00001, places on the seller the costs
relating in particular to the repayment and the discharge of any claim secured by
hypothec, and specifies that they include any penalty that may be exigible in the
case of a prepayment. Clause 8.4 binds the seller to provide their loan and
hypothecary security deeds, penalties included.

## Why the notary cannot sign without the quittance

**The blockage is notarial before it is financial.** Article 1723 of the Code civil
obliges the seller to warrant to the buyer that the property is free of all rights
except those declared, and to discharge the property of all hypothecs encumbering
it, even declared or registered, unless the buyer has assumed the debt.

The Government of Quebec describes the deed at issue: the quittance is the document
in which a creditor declares having received all the sums lent and due, only
notaries and lawyers are qualified to prepare it, and the fees are usually borne by
the borrower. Article 3065 of the Code civil adds the rule that decides everything
here: a total quittance of a claim entails consent to the cancellation of the
registration, whereas a partial quittance entails only consent to a corresponding
reduction.

Read that sentence twice. A creditor who is not paid in full has no reason to issue
a total quittance, and a partial quittance cancels nothing. A sale at a loss
therefore requires the lender's consent to cancel the registration against a payment
lower than the balance. No article obliges it to.

The registration fees, for their part, are public. Here is the Registre foncier
tariff in force since April 1, 2026.

| Item | Tariff |
|---|---|
| Cancellation: quittance, release, per application | $165 |
| Each additional registration to be cancelled | $101 more |
| Registration of a right, including a hypothec | $145 |
| Certified statement of rights | $17 |

Two registrations to cancel, a home equity line of credit for example, and the item
goes from $165 to $266. The notary's fees, for their part, are set by no public
scale. The full sequence is in
[the notary's role in Quebec](/en/articles/quebec-notary-process/).

## Getting the lender's agreement: everything is asked in writing

One letter, with the target closing date, and six answers expected: the payout
balance, the penalty and the two rates used to calculate it, the administration and
appraisal fees, the repayable portion of any cash back received at signing, how long
the amount communicated stays valid, and the lender's position on cancelling the
registration against a payment lower than the balance.

The SCHL (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation) also lists the tools an approved
lender may use in the face of a default: short term payment deferral, extension of
the amortization, spreading out missed payments, special arrangements. Those tools
run through the lender, and none of them is a given.

Transferring to a next property is the avenue few sellers know about, and it has to
be named correctly. SCHL portability allows existing mortgage loan insurance to be
transferred to a new property without taking out new coverage, on precise
conditions: at least one borrower common to both loans, an existing loan in good
standing and not in arrears, an application received within six months of closing,
an identical intended use. That mechanism concerns the insurance. Whether your loan
itself follows, and on what conditions, depends on your contract and on the
lender's decision. It is not a right.

Put a number on the numerator of the calculation first with the
[home value estimator](/en/tools/home-estimate/), then correct that starting point
with adjusted comparables.

## Cancelling the hypothec does not erase the debt

**Here is the most expensive confusion in the file.** Article 2661 of the Code
civil says it in one line: a hypothec is merely an accessory right and subsists
only as long as the obligation whose performance it secures continues to exist. The
security and the claim are two distinct things, and cancelling the first does not
make the second disappear. Article 2789 confirms it in the case of a sale made by
the creditor: if the proceeds of the sale are insufficient to pay the claim and the
costs, the creditor retains, against the debtor, a claim for what remains due.

If your loan was insured because the down payment was under 20%, the insurer enters
the equation. The SCHL writes it without hedging in its frequently asked questions
on mortgage loan insurance: in the event of a payment default, the proceeds of the
sale of the property may not be enough to extinguish the debt, and the borrower may
be obliged to pay the difference. The insurance protects the lender, not you.

I stop where my documentation stops. No official document I was able to open
describes the mechanism by which the insurer, after indemnifying the lender, claims
that difference from the borrower. Ask the question in writing, and demand the name
of the insurer on file.

## The 60 day prior notice and what it really changes

Once the default has begun, the calendar becomes public. Article 2757 obliges the
creditor to file at the registry office a prior notice accompanied by proof of its
service. Article 2758 sets its content and the period: the notice must disclose any
default, recall the right to remedy it, state the amount of the claim in principal
and interest and name the hypothecary right intended to be exercised, and the period
for surrendering an immovable is 60 days from registration.

Article 2761 opens the window: the debtor, or any other interested person, may
defeat the exercise of the creditor's right by paying what is owed or by remedying
the default, and by paying the costs incurred, until the property has been taken in
payment or sold. Article 2762 limits what the creditor may demand in the meantime:
no indemnity other than the interest due and the costs incurred.

**The belief to correct holds in one sentence.** People repeat that an ordinary sale
is always better than a repossession, and that is true of the price, the choice of
buyer and the calendar. It is not automatic on the debt side: article 2782 provides
that taking in payment extinguishes the obligation and excludes subrogation against
the former debtor, whereas article 2789 leaves the creditor a claim for the balance
after a sale. Both procedures are described in
[buying a foreclosure in Quebec](/en/articles/buying-foreclosure-quebec/).

## Tax: a loss on a principal residence is not deductible

This is the last false consolation to remove from the calculation. In its guide on
capital gains, the Canada Revenue Agency treats the principal residence as personal
use property, and states the rule: even if you have to report a gain from the sale of
personal use property, you are generally not allowed to deduct a loss.

No line of your return will therefore soften the gap paid at the notary's. If the
property was not always your principal residence, if a portion was rented, or if
capital cost allowance has already been claimed, the treatment changes: see an
accountant before the signing. The other selling costs are listed in
[the real cost of selling a home in Quebec](/en/articles/real-cost-selling-home-quebec/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my bank stop me from selling?** It does not stop the sale, it stops the
closing. Article 1723 obliges the seller to discharge the immovable of hypothecs,
and article 3065 reserves consent to cancellation to a total quittance. Without full
payment or the lender's written agreement, the title is not delivered.

**Does the broker get paid even if I sell at a loss?** Clause 7.1 of form CCVE
00001 provides for remuneration expressed as a percentage of the price set for the
sale or as a lump sum, plus taxes, due in particular where an agreement to sell the
immovable is entered into during the term of the contract and all of its conditions
are fulfilled, except the signing of the deed of sale and the payment of the price.
That amount is negotiated before signing the brokerage contract, never after.

**Can I transfer my loan to my next property?** SCHL portability concerns the
insurance, on conditions, including a loan in good standing and an application
received within six months of closing. The fate of the loan itself depends on your
contract and on the lender's written agreement.

**Would it be better to wait for the market to come back up?** It is not a question
of the market, it is a question of figures: compare the monthly cost of keeping the
property, maintenance and taxes included, against the gap to cover today. No
official source allows anyone to predict the price in your sector twelve months from
now.
