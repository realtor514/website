This calculator compares two net worth positions at the end of a 5- to 30-year horizon.

**Buy side:** the property's value, growing at the chosen appreciation rate, minus the mortgage balance. The monthly cost of owning includes the payment (CMHC premium included below a 20% down payment), the property tax you enter and maintenance set at 1% of the price per year.

**Rent side:** the down payment is invested at the chosen return. Each month, the gap between the cost of owning and the rent is added to it; if the rent exceeds that cost, the gap is withdrawn. Rent rises once a year at the rate you enter.

**The assumptions that decide.** Four numbers weigh the most: appreciation, investment return, rent increase and mortgage rate. The preloaded values (price $550,000, down payment $110,000, rate 4.99% over 25 years, property tax $275, rent $2,300, appreciation 4%, return 6%, rent increase 3%) are a starting point, not a forecast.

Calculator results after 20 years, changing one value at a time:

| Changed assumption | Net worth, buy | Wealth, rent |
|---|---|---|
| None (4% appreciation) | $1,069,443 | $520,533 |
| 2% appreciation | $681,596 | $520,533 |
| 1% appreciation | $535,430 | $520,533 |
| 8% return | $1,069,443 | $751,796 |
| 4% return | $1,069,443 | $356,047 |

One point of appreciation moves the result by more than $100,000 over 20 years. Test a cautious value and an optimistic one, then look in the yearly table for the year the winner changes.

**Limits.** The calculation does not deduct buying costs (welcome tax, notary, inspection), selling costs at the end of the horizon, home insurance or tax on investment returns. Property tax and maintenance stay fixed in dollars for the whole horizon, while rent rises every year. Most of these choices favour buying, especially over a short horizon. The result compares two scenarios; it does not predict the market.
