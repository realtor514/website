This calculator looks for the highest purchase price that meets three rules at the same time: the two debt service ratios, the qualifying rate and the minimum down payment. It keeps whichever constraint is tightest.

**GDS and TDS.** The gross debt service ratio (GDS, called ABD in Quebec) compares housing costs with the household's gross income: mortgage payment, property tax, heating and half of condo fees. The total debt service ratio (TDS, or ATD) adds all other monthly debts. For an insured mortgage, CMHC sets the maximums at 39% and 44%. For an uninsured mortgage, OSFI Guideline B-20 lets each lender set its own; the calculator applies 39% and 44% in every case. It estimates property tax at 1% of the price per year and heating at $150 per month.

**The qualifying rate.** Ratios are calculated at the greater of the contract rate plus 2 points or 5.25%: a 4% rate qualifies at 6%. The payment the calculator shows is the payment at this qualifying rate, not your actual payment.

**The minimum down payment.** 5% up to $500,000, 10% on the portion between $500,000 and $1,500,000, 20% from $1,500,000, the price at which mortgage loan insurance is no longer available. Below 20%, the CMHC premium is added to the loan: 4.00%, 3.10% or 2.80% of the amount borrowed, depending on the down payment. A 30-year amortization is insured only for a first-time buyer or a newly built home, with a premium 0.20 points higher; the calculator applies the 25-year premium.

Detail of the first case: a $400,000 loan, a 3.10% premium ($12,400), a $412,400 insured loan. At 6%, the payment is $2,638.56; with $375 of property tax and $150 of heating, housing costs reach $3,163.56, or 37.96% of a monthly income of $8,333.33. With the $500 of debts, TDS rises to 43.96%: that is what sets the ceiling. At the actual 4% rate, the payment would be $2,169.31.
